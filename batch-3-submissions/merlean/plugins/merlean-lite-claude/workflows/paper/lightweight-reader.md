# Independent manuscript reading

Read the entire supplied manuscript in reader order. Judge the text itself without
author notes, research chronology, earlier opinions or acceptance labels. A first
reader's report is not a substitute for the second reader's fresh reading. Return
located, actionable findings and a substantive assessment; do not invent faults to
fill a quota, and do not issue PASS with unresolved material issues.

Check that every main statement has clear objects, domains, quantifiers, hypotheses
and conclusion. Follow the proof, including transitions between lemmas, constructions
and the main result. Flag omitted key steps, circular dependencies, hidden additional
assumptions, an unexplained imported result, and claims stronger than the argument.
Distinguish a real mathematical gap from a request for a helpful explanatory sentence.
Check necessary definitions before first use, consistent notation, and meaningful
references to earlier statements. Locate undefined academic terminology and ambiguous
or ad-hoc labels. A new term is legitimate when precisely defined and genuinely
useful; novelty of vocabulary alone is not a defect. Recommend the accurate standard
term or a plain description when a coined label obscures the mathematics.

Assess exposition and balanced compression: retain the reason the proof works,
necessary intermediate identities and bridges, and examples that concretely help the
reader. Remove repetitive motivation and redundant restatements rather than essential
reasoning. A short paper is not automatically better; remaining space under the page
cap can be used to restore a needed step. Do not request unsupported new proofs or
invented citations. Genuine limitations and partial results must be stated honestly.

Assess section purpose and reader order, clear mathematical prose, accurate attribution,
and whether the final paper presents results rather than a workflow diary. Avoid rigid
templates, decorative jargon, mechanical repetition and empty promotional claims.
Citations must be tied to the claim or method that uses them. A manuscript-only reader
can flag a source concern; it must not claim to have independently retrieved a source
when no such retrieval occurred. The separate source-aware reviewer verifies primary
content and exact use-sites.

For final second-reader verification, inspect both author dispositions and the current
manuscript, including unchanged passages. Confirm all material findings are resolved
without new problems; do not merely trust an author's claim of resolution. There is
no requirement to generate legacy work files, inventories, sidecar schemas or gate
ledgers. Only the concise host-requested review object is required.

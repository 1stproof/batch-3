## FINDING 1 (Mainline Gap -- blocking): false "Schreier" claim in

(a) *The subgroup statement is false.* The genuine Schreier
transversal-`{1, x2}` computation for the index-2 kernel of
`K -> Z/2` (parity of the x2-exponent-sum) produces **five**

The set `{x0, x1, x2^2}` is a subset of a free basis of
`ch^{-1}(2Z)`, so `S := <x0, x1, x2^2>` is a **proper free factor** of
`ch^{-1}(2Z)`; in particular `[K : S] = infinity`, not 2, and
`S != ch^{-1}(2Z)`. Concretely, `x2*x1*x2^{-1}` lies in
`ch^{-1}(2Z) \ S`. Hence item (iv) as stated ("S has index 2 in K; it
is `ch^{-1}(2Z)`, free on x0,x1,x2^2") is false.

(b) *The two displayed forms of "the same subgroup" are genuinely
different subgroups.* In the basis `<x0, x1, x3>` the element
`(x0 x1 x3)^{-2} = x2^2` still has **even** x2-charge (`ch = -2`); the
element that has odd x2-charge in this basis is
`x2 = (x0 x1 x3)^{-1}` *itself*. Therefore
`<x0, x1, x3, (x0 x1 x3)^{-2}>` -- which as a subgroup of
`K = <x0, x1, x3> = F3` is just the whole of `K` (all three basis
generators plus a redundant fourth element) -- **is all of `K`**, and
is certainly not equal to `ch^{-1}(2Z)`. Meanwhile the first form
`<x0, x1, x2^2>` is, per (a), an infinite-index proper subgroup of the
correct kernel. The manuscript's own sentence -- "the same subgroup is
`<x0,x1,x3,(x0x1x3)^{-2}>`; either form will be denoted
`S = <x0,x1,x2^2>`" -- thus identifies three mutually distinct
subgroups (`K`, a proper free factor of `ch^{-1}(2Z)`, and
`ch^{-1}(2Z)` itself) as one. This is an internally visible
contradiction, not a subtle one.

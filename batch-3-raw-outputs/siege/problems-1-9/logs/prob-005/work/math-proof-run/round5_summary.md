## Headline: a complete written REFUTATION is in hand (round 4)
Five independent round-4 answers each give a complete explicit counterexample: round4_q1, round4_q2, round4_q4, round4_q6 and round4_q7. All five use the same mechanism.

Mechanism. Take a "Krasner-type" hyperfield built from a projective plane on which an abelian group G acts regularly:
- H = G ∪ {0}, with -1 = 1;
- a⊞a = {0,a};
- a⊞b = ℓ(a,b) \ {a,b} for a ≠ b, where ℓ(a,b) is the line through a and b.

Here the plane is NON-Desarguesian, and infinite. It comes from an infinite perfect (planar) difference set D ⊂ Z, built by M. Hall's greedy free extension; round4_q7 instead uses a free abelian group of countable rank. The greedy construction can force a prescribed finite Sidon configuration into D while keeping a chosen integer out of D.

The failure. The claim fails whenever:
- 1, x, y are collinear;
- x^3, y^3 and xy are distinct and lie on a line M not through 1.

Then T := x^3⊞y^3⊞xy⊞xy⊞xy = M ∪ {0} ∌ 1 (round4_q2, Lemma 2). Round4_q6 and round4_q7 use a variant: 1, x^3 or y^3, and xy are collinear, with the third point off that line.

The chain chosen for audit is round4_q2:
- Proposition 1: the hyperfield axioms for any plane satisfying (P1)–(P4) with ≥5 points per line, including a full associativity case analysis.
- Lemma 2: the target computation.
- Lemma 3: a perfect difference set gives (P1)–(P4).
- Lemma 4: there is a perfect difference set D ⊇ {0,1,4,103,105,112} with 100 ∉ D.
- Instance: x ↔ 1, y ↔ 4, z = 1.

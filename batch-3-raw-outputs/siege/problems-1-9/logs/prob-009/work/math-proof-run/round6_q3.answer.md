# round6_q3: answer on MS5 (ledger entry 40)

## Plan / status (written first; updated at the end)
Target: prove MS5, or give an explicit counterexample.
Approach taken:
(1) Show a structural fact: the candidate stratum G = Γ never works (Lemma A).
(2) Exhibit a totally geodesic surface V_0 all of whose 1-dimensional boundary pieces lie in Δ_Γ (Example 1: the Z/5-covers y^5 = ∏_{j=1}^5 (x − a_j), V_0 ⊂ M_6). For it MS5 fails.
(3) Check the smoothness hypothesis for that V_0. It FAILS, both in the orbifold sense and in the coarse sense. So Example 1 is NOT a counterexample to MS5 as stated. It shows only that any proof of MS5 must use the smoothness of V_0 in an essential way.

## 0. Summary of results

- **Lemma A (proved).** For a candidate (Δ_G, P_0) with G = Γ, condition (a) always fails. Hence MS5 needs a 1-dimensional piece of closure(V_0) in a product-type stratum G ≠ Γ, and the closure of that piece must meet closure(∂V_0).
- **Theorem 1 (proved, modulo standard facts on covering constructions, cited from memory).** Let V_0 = Φ(M_{0,5}) ⊂ M_6, where Φ(a) is the Z/5-cover y^5 = ∏(x − a_j). Then:
  - V_0 is a closed, 2-dimensional, totally geodesic subvariety;
  - V_0 satisfies every hypothesis of MS5 other than smoothness;
  - the conclusion of MS5 fails for V_0. In fact it fails even if non-product strata are allowed.
- **Proposition 2 (proved).** This V_0 is not smooth.
  - It is singular in the orbifold sense at the Fermat quintic: three branches with pairwise distinct tangent planes meet there.
  - It is singular in the coarse sense at X_4 = {y^5 = x(x^4 − 1)}: the germ there is C²/(Z/4), with Z/4 not generated by pseudoreflections.
- **Consequence.** MS5 is neither proved nor disproved here.
  - Any proof of MS5 must use smoothness of V_0, an interior condition, to control the boundary.
  - Equivalently, it must exclude the configuration "every 1-dimensional boundary piece of closure(V_0) lying in a product-type stratum lies in Δ_Γ". Total geodesy alone does not exclude it.
- **Remarks (sketched only).**
  - (R-a) On Example 1, a modified (T0) works: G = Γ, but with the vertex u ≠ i. It would pin Y_t if (R2) held on a level cover. This suggests how the chain could be repaired.
  - (R-b) The Picard family shows a second defect of MS5: the restriction to product-type strata.
  - (R-c) Within prime-order cyclic covers of M_{0,5}, Example 1 is essentially the only example of this type.

## 1. Readings and conventions
- M̄_g is the Deligne–Mumford compactification. Δ_G denotes the stratum of graph G, and ξ̄_G: ∏_w M̄_w → M̄_g is the gluing map.
- MS5 is exactly as in the task file. In particular, E^{(a)} runs over the 1-dimensional irreducible components of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G, and 𝐂^{(a)} = closure of ξ_G^{-1}(E^{(a)}) in ∏_w M̄_w.
- "Smooth" is considered in both senses:
  - (orbifold) the preimage of V_0 in Teichmüller space T_g, equivalently in a level cover M_g[m] with m ≥ 3, is a complex submanifold;
  - (coarse) V_0 is a smooth subvariety of the coarse space M_g.

## 2. Lemma A (the stratum Γ itself never works)

**Lemma A.** Let (Δ_G, P_0) be any candidate in MS5 with G = Γ. Then (a) fails for some admissible triple (a, R, u). Hence MS5 can only hold with G ≠ Γ.

*Proof.*
1. Take a with P_0 ∈ closure(E^{(a)}) \ E^{(a)}; such an a exists by the definition of a candidate.
2. E^{(a)} ⊂ closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_Γ = ∂V_0. Since π_i ≡ Y_0 on ∂V_0 and ξ_Γ is bijective onto Δ_Γ, we get ξ_Γ^{-1}(E^{(a)}) ⊂ {Y_0} × ∏_{w≠i} M_w. The set {Y_0} × ∏_{w≠i} M̄_w is closed, so 𝐂^{(a)} ⊂ {Y_0} × ∏_{w≠i} M̄_w.
3. A suitable R exists. 𝐂^{(a)} is compact and ξ̄_Γ is continuous, so ξ̄_Γ(𝐂^{(a)}) is compact. It contains E^{(a)}, hence also closure(E^{(a)}), which contains P_0. Pick R ∈ 𝐂^{(a)} with ξ̄_Γ(R) = P_0.
4. Take u = i. Then R_i = Y_0 ∈ M_{g_i,n_i} is a smooth curve. Its only irreducible component, with its special points in the given order, is Y_0. So u = i is a vertex that MS5 requires us to check.
5. (a) requires R_i ∈ ∂M̄_{g_i,n_i}. This is false. ∎

**Corollary A.** Suppose that no 1-dimensional component of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G, for any product-type G ≠ Γ, has closure meeting closure(∂V_0). Then the conclusion of MS5 fails for V_0. This holds in particular when every 1-dimensional component of closure(V_0) \ V_0 lying in a product-type stratum lies in Δ_Γ.

*Proof.* G = Γ is excluded by Lemma A. For any other product-type G, no admissible P_0 exists. ∎

## 3. Example 1: the quintic cyclic covers, V_0 ⊂ M_6

### 3.1 Construction
- For a 5-element set A = {a_1, …, a_5} ⊂ P^1, let X_A be the cyclic Z/5-cover of P^1 branched exactly over A, with local monodromy the generator 1 ∈ Z/5 at every a_j. Affinely, X_A is y^5 = ∏(x − a_j).
  - The cover is unbranched over ∞ because Σ k_j = 5 ≡ 0.
  - It is connected because 1 generates Z/5.
  - Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2g − 2 = 5·(−2) + 5·4 = 10, so g = 6.
- X_A depends only on the unordered set A. This gives Φ: M_{0,5} → M_6, which factors through M_{0,5}/S_5. Put V_0 := Φ(M_{0,5}).
- Let H ⊂ Mod_6 be the topological type of the deck action. It is a Z/5 with 5 fixed points, and the rotation number at each fixed point is the same (all equal to the generator).

**Cited facts (from memory, standard).**
- (C1) **Covering constructions.**
  - Φ lifts to a holomorphic map Φ̃: T_{0,5} → T_6. It is an isometric embedding for the Teichmüller metrics, and its image is the fixed locus Fix(H), a connected complex submanifold of dimension 2.
  - Φ̃ is equivariant for N_{Mod}(H) acting on T_{0,5} through N(H)/H.
  - Reason: the Teichmüller map of (P^1, A) defined by q lifts to the Teichmüller map of X_A defined by π^*q. Here π^*q is integrable, because q has at most simple poles at A, which pull back to holomorphic points.
  - At a point X ∈ V_0, every complex tangent direction of V_0 is dΦ̃(v) for some v. The unique Teichmüller disc of T_6 tangent to dΦ̃(v) is the image of the Teichmüller disc of T_{0,5} tangent to v, which lies in Fix(H). So V_0 is totally geodesic in the sense of the problem. (A Teichmüller disc is determined by its tangent direction, by Teichmüller's uniqueness theorem.)
- (C2) **Admissible covers** (Harris–Mumford; equivalently twisted stable maps to BZ/5).
  - Φ extends to a continuous (indeed algebraic) map Φ̄: M̄_{0,5} → M̄_6. Φ̄ sends a stable 5-pointed rational curve B to the stable curve obtained as the admissible Z/5-cover of B.
  - In our case this cover is described explicitly in 3.2. All node monodromies are nonzero, so the cover is unique and no component is contracted.

**Consequences.**
- Φ̄(∂M̄_{0,5}) ⊂ ∂M̄_6 (see 3.2). Hence Φ̄^{-1}(M_6) = M_{0,5}, and Φ: M_{0,5} → M_6 is proper.
- So V_0 is a closed algebraic subvariety of M_6, and closure(V_0) = Φ̄(M̄_{0,5}).
- dim V_0 = 2 by (C1).

### 3.2 The boundary of closure(V_0)

Let B be a stable 5-pointed rational curve. Over a component B_v of B, the admissible cover restricts to the Z/5-cover branched at the special points of B_v, with these local monodromies:
- 1 at each marked point a_j;
- |S| mod 5 at a node, where S is the set of marked points on the far side of the node.

The two sides of a node carry opposite monodromies, |S| and −|S|; the sign convention plays no role below. Since 1 ≤ |S| ≤ 4, every local monodromy is nonzero mod 5. So every special point is totally ramified, with exactly one preimage. The covers of the components are glued at these unique points.

Riemann–Hurwitz gives the genus of the cover over a component with 3, 4 or 5 special points as 2, 4 or 6 respectively. In each case the cover has the same number of special points as B_v and is stable. There are three cases.

- **Open boundary divisors D_{jl}°** (a_j, a_l on a bubble A, the other three on the main component M).
  - The cover of A has monodromies (1, 1, 3) at (a_j, a_l, node). The node monodromy seen from A is −2 ≡ 3, so the sum is 0. It is a genus-2 curve with one special point. Call this 1-pointed curve Y_0. It is the same curve for every pair {j,l} and every point of D_{jl}°.
  - The cover of M has monodromies (1, 1, 1, 2) at (a_m, a_n, a_p, node). It is a genus-4 curve with one special point. It varies along D_{jl}°, because by (C1) its family is the image of the Teichmüller embedding T_{0,4} → T_{4,1}.
  - So Φ̄(D_{jl}°) ⊂ Δ_Γ, where Γ has two vertices i = (2,1) and 2 = (4,1) joined by one edge.
  - Γ has no automorphisms (the genera differ). So ξ_Γ: M_{2,1} × M_{4,1} → Δ_Γ is bijective and Δ_Γ is of product type.
- **Corner points D_{jl} ∩ D_{mn}** ({j,l} ∩ {m,n} = ∅; let p be the fifth index).
  - B is a chain A(a_j, a_l) – B'(a_p) – C(a_m, a_n), with node monodromies 2 and 2.
  - The covers are genus 2 over A, genus 2 over B' (monodromies (1, 2, 2)), and genus 2 over C.
  - The image is a chain of three genus-2 curves, in a stratum Δ_{G'} with |E(G')| = 2. G' has a flip automorphism, so Δ_{G'} is not of product type; this does not matter below.
  - There are 15 corners, so closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_{G'} is a finite set.
- **Interior.** Φ(M_{0,5}) = V_0 ⊂ M_6.

Therefore:
- ∂V_0 := closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_Γ = ∪_{j<l} Φ̄(D_{jl}°), which has dimension 1;
- π_i(∂V_0) = {Y_0}, and dim M_{2,1} = 4 > 0;
- the only strata of M̄_6 meeting closure(V_0) are M_6, Δ_Γ and Δ_{G'};
- closure(V_0) ∩ M_6 = V_0 is irreducible of dimension 2, so it has no 1-dimensional components;
- closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_{G'} is finite, so it has no 1-dimensional components either.

### 3.3 Theorem 1
**Theorem 1.** V_0 = Φ(M_{0,5}) ⊂ M_6 is a closed, 2-dimensional, totally geodesic subvariety. With Γ and i as above it satisfies all hypotheses of MS5 except, possibly, smoothness: ∂V_0 is 1-dimensional, constant on the factor M_{2,1} of positive dimension, and Δ_Γ is of product type. The conclusion of MS5 fails for V_0.

*Proof.*
1. The hypotheses hold by 3.1 and 3.2.
2. By 3.2, the only stratum of any kind in which closure(V_0) has a 1-dimensional component is Δ_Γ.
3. By Lemma A, no candidate with G = Γ satisfies (a). By step 2, no other product-type stratum has 1-dimensional pieces. So no candidate (Δ_G, P_0) exists. ∎

**Remark.** The failure in Theorem 1 does not come from the product-type restriction. Even if non-product strata were allowed in MS5, with 𝐂^{(a)} taken in ∏ M̄_w, the only stratum with 1-dimensional pieces is Δ_Γ. So the conclusion still fails.

## 4. Proposition 2: the V_0 of Example 1 is not smooth

So Theorem 1 is NOT a counterexample to MS5 as stated.

**Proposition 2.**
- (i) (Orbifold sense.) Let F be the Fermat quintic x^5 + y^5 + z^5 = 0, which lies in V_0, and let F̃ ∈ T_6 lie over F. Near F̃, the preimage of V_0 in T_6 contains two smooth 2-dimensional germs whose tangent planes meet only in 0. Hence the preimage of V_0 is not a submanifold there. The same holds in any level cover M_6[m], since T_6 → M_6[m] is a local biholomorphism.
- (ii) (Coarse sense.) Let X_4 := {y^5 = x(x^4 − 1)} ∈ V_0. The germ of V_0 at X_4 in the coarse space M_6 is singular: it is isomorphic to C²/(Z/4) with weights (−i, −1), which is an A_1 singularity.

### Proof of (i)
1. **F lies in V_0.** In affine coordinates z = 1, F is y^5 = −∏(x − η_k), where the η_k are the fifth roots of −1. Replacing y by −y gives the form X_A with A = {η_k}.
2. **Three structures.** Let σ_x: (x:y:z) ↦ (ζx:y:z), with ζ = e^{2πi/5}, and define σ_y, σ_z similarly. Put H_x = ⟨σ_x⟩ and so on.
   - F → P^1, (x:y:z) ↦ (y:z), is the quotient by H_x. It is branched exactly where x = 0, at 5 points, with the same rotation at each.
   - The topological type of a Z/5-action with quotient P^1 is determined by its generating vector up to Aut(Z/5) and the braid action. The vector (1,1,1,1,1) is unique. So H_x, H_y and H_z are conjugate in Mod_6 to H.
   - Hence Fix(H_x), Fix(H_y) and Fix(H_z) are Mod_6-translates of Fix(H) = Φ̃(T_{0,5}). They lie in the preimage of V_0, and all pass through F̃.
3. **Tangent planes.**
   - We have T_{F̃}T_6 = Q(F)^*, and T_{F̃} Fix(H') = (Q(F)^*)^{H'}.
   - For a plane quintic, Q(F) = H^0(2K) = H^0(O_F(4)) = {P(x,y)·(dx/y^4)^2 : deg P ≤ 4}, with dim 15 = 3g − 3 (adjunction, and projective normality of plane curves).
   - σ_x multiplies dx/y^4 by ζ, so it multiplies x^a y^b (dx/y^4)^2 by ζ^{a+2}.
   - σ_y multiplies dx/y^4 by ζ^{−4} = ζ, so it multiplies the monomial by ζ^{b+2}.
   - Hence Q^{H_x} = span(x^3, x^3 y) and Q^{H_y} = span(y^3, x y^3).
   - Both groups act diagonally on the monomial basis. So (Q^*)^{H_x} and (Q^*)^{H_y} are spanned by the dual basis vectors of {x^3, x^3y} and of {y^3, xy^3} respectively. These are two 2-planes meeting in 0.
4. **Conclusion.** A 2-dimensional submanifold germ is irreducible, with a single tangent plane. The preimage germ contains two distinct irreducible 2-dimensional germs. Indeed it is a finite union of germs Fix(H′) with H′ ⊂ Aut(F), since each translate of Fix(H) through F̃ is the fixed locus of a subgroup of Stab(F̃) = Aut(F). So the preimage germ is not smooth. ∎

### Proof of (ii)

**Step 1 (the generic automorphism group is H).**
- Let K ⊂ Mod_6 be the pointwise stabilizer of Z := Fix(H). It is finite and contains H, and Fix(K) = Fix(H) has dimension 2.
- The quotient X/K is dominated by X/H ≅ P^1, so it has genus 0. Since dim Fix(K) = (number of cone points) − 3, it has exactly 5 cone points, of orders m_1, …, m_5.
- Riemann–Hurwitz for X → X/K: 10 = |K|·(−2 + Σ_k (1 − 1/m_k)). Writing n = [K:H], this gives Σ_k (1 − 1/m_k) = 2 + 2/n.
- Suppose n ≥ 2. Each of the 5 H-fixed points of X has K-stabilizer of order divisible by 5, so its image is a cone point contributing at least 4/5. Let r ≥ 1 be the number of distinct such images. The other cone points contribute at least 1/2 each, so Σ ≥ 4r/5 + (5 − r)/2 = 5/2 + 3r/10.
- From Σ ≤ 3 we get r = 1. Then the 5 branch points of X → X/H all lie over a single point of X/K under the degree-n map X/H → X/K, so n ≥ 5. Then Σ ≤ 2.4 < 2.8 ≤ Σ, a contradiction.
- So K = H.

**Step 2 (the base stabilizer).**
- Let A_4 = {0, ±1, ±i} be the branch set of X_4. Its stabilizer in PGL_2(C) is ⟨g⟩ ≅ Z/4, with g(z) = iz.
- Elements fixing 0 preserve the harmonic quadruple {±1, ±i}. Among the symmetries of that quadruple (D_4), the ones fixing 0 are the maps z ↦ i^k z.
- No element moves 0. For instance, a map sending 0 ↦ 1 would carry {1, i, −1, −i} (cross-ratio −1) to {0, i, −1, −i}. The latter has cross-ratio (0, −1; i, −i) = (1+i)/(1−i) = i, which is not in the harmonic orbit {−1, 2, 1/2}. The same argument applies to the other targets by the Z/4-symmetry.
- g lifts to X_4: (x, y) ↦ (ix, λy) with λ^5 = i, because (ix)((ix)^4 − 1) = i·x(x^4 − 1).

**Step 3 (the germ).**
- Let 𝒜 = Aut(X_4) act on (T_6, X̃_4). The coarse germ of M_6 at X_4 is (T_6, X̃_4)/𝒜.
- The preimage of V_0 is a finite union of germs Fix(H′). Suppose the coarse germ (V_0, X_4) were smooth. It would then be irreducible and normal.
- The image of Z is a closed irreducible 2-dimensional subgerm, so it is the whole germ.
- Let N = Stab_𝒜(Z). The map ψ: Z/N → (V_0, X_4) is finite.
- ψ is generically injective. Suppose z, z′ = a·z ∈ Z are generic, i.e. they avoid the proper analytic set where Z meets other branches. Then aZ is a branch through z′, so aZ = Z and a ∈ N.
- Z/N is normal. By Zariski's main theorem (analytic version; cited), a finite birational map onto a normal germ is an isomorphism, so (V_0, X_4) ≅ Z/N.
- By Step 1, N normalizes the pointwise stabilizer H of Z. Hence N = N_𝒜(H), and N/H acts on Z ≅ (T_{0,5}, b_4) through an injection N/H ↪ Stab_{PGL_2}(A_4) = Z/4. This injection is onto by Step 2.
- So (V_0, X_4) ≅ (C², 0)/(Z/4), using Cartan linearization, with the tangent representation at b_4.

**Step 4 (the tangent representation).**
- T_{b_4}T_{0,5} = Q(b_4)^*, where Q(b_4) = {P(z) dz²/(z(z^4 − 1)) : deg P ≤ 1}. The bound deg P ≤ 1 is the condition for holomorphy at ∞.
- g^* multiplies P(z) dz²/(z(z^4 − 1)) by i·P(iz)/P(z). So the eigenvalues on Q are i (for P = 1) and −1 (for P = z).
- Hence g acts on T by (−i, −1). Neither g nor g³ is a pseudoreflection, while g² = (−1, 1) is one.
- So Z/4 is not generated by pseudoreflections, and by Chevalley–Shephard–Todd (with Serre's converse; cited) C²/(Z/4) is singular.
- Explicitly, the invariants are u^4, v², u²v, with the single relation (u²v)² = u^4·v², which is an A_1 singularity. This contradicts smoothness.
- If the coarse germ is instead reducible, it is singular anyway. ∎

**Remark.** At the Fermat point the coarse germ is in fact smooth. There N/H ≅ D_5, which is generated by reflections. So neither sense of smoothness holds everywhere on V_0: the orbifold sense fails at F, and the coarse sense fails at X_4.

## 5. Further remarks (sketches; NOT fully proved)

### (R-a) A modified pinning configuration on Example 1 (sketch, conditional)
On Example 1, Theorem P could still pin Y_t, with G = Γ but a different vertex u, under a stronger reading of (R2).

1. **The cusp.** Let P_0 = Φ̄(D_{12} ∩ D_{34}). It is the chain A – B′ – C, with A ≅ C ≅ Y_0. The edge e joins A and B′, and the edge f joins B′ and C. B′ has an involution swapping its two node-points, so Aut(P_0) contains a flip exchanging A and C.
2. **Two local branches.** In the orbifold chart at P_0, with smoothing coordinates s_e and s_f, the preimage of ∂V_0 has two branches:
   - β_1 = {s_e = 0, s_f ≠ 0}, which keeps A and merges B′ ∪ C into vertex 2;
   - β_2 = {s_f = 0, s_e ≠ 0}, which keeps C and merges A ∪ B′.
   These come from D_{12} and D_{34}. The flip exchanges them, so in the coarse space they are a single branch.
3. **The modified (T0).** Take G = Γ, branch β_2, and u = vertex 2. The limit R_u = A ∪ B′ lies in ∂M̄_{4,1} and contains the component A ≅ Y_0. Moreover π_2(β_2) is the Teichmüller curve of the cover of M_{0,4}, by (C1). So this is (T0) with u = 2 instead of the forbidden u = i.
4. **Pinning, if (R2) holds in level charts.** Suppose (R2) is a stratified homeomorphism after lifting to a level cover, so that both local branch types persist at P_t.
   - On the branch of type β_1 at P_t, vertex i is the A-component, so A_t = Y_t.
   - On the branch of type β_2, vertex 2 tends to A_t ∪ B′_t, which lies in K ∩ ∂M̄_{4,1} by Lemma R*, assuming (O). This is a finite set.
   - So Y_t takes values in a finite set, and is constant.
5. **Why this needs the level-cover reading.** With (R2) only in the coarse sense, P_t may lose the flip. The single coarse branch at P_t then keeps either A_t or C_t, not both. This is the twin issue of PR-G again, and the argument does not close.

Example 1 is not smooth, so this remark matters only as a hint for repairing the chain: allow u ≠ i in (T0) and carry the transport out in level charts.

### (R-b) The Picard family: a defect of the product-type restriction (checked in outline)
- **The surface.** V_0^P = {y^3 = ∏_{j=1}^4 (x − a_j)} ⊂ M_3. This is a Z/3-cover with monodromies (1,1,1,1,2), the last one at a_5 = ∞. It is a covering construction from M_{0,5}, hence totally geodesic.
- **The divisors D_{jl}, j, l ≤ 4.** They map to Δ_Γ, with Γ = (1,1) – (2,1) of product type. The constant factor is Y_0 = (E_ω, point) ∈ M_{1,1}, coming from the bubble cover with monodromies (1,1,1). The genus-2 factor varies.
- **The divisors D_{j5}.** Here the node monodromy is 1 + 2 ≡ 0. They map to Δ_{G_2}, where G_2 is a (1,3)-vertex and a (0,3)-vertex joined by three edges. This stratum is NOT of product type: relabelling the three edges simultaneously gives the same curve.
- **The corners** map to finitely many points.
- **MS5 fails.** In product-type strata, only Δ_Γ has 1-dimensional pieces, so MS5 fails by Corollary A.
- **The non-product version holds** (in outline). Take P_0 = Φ̄(D_{12} ∩ D_{35}). This is E_ω – B′ ≡ C, where B′ is a P^1 with 4 special points, C is a P^1 with 3 special points, and B′ and C are joined by three edges. Take E = Φ̄(D_{35}°) ⊂ Δ_{G_2}.
  - Every lift R has R_{(0,3)} = C (the only component with exactly 3 special points, all of them nodes) and R_{(1,3)} = E_ω ∪ B′ ∈ ∂M̄_{1,3}. So the Y_0-component is merged.
  - π_{(1,3)}(E) is the covering-construction Teichmüller curve: the cover E_ω → P^1 branched at 3 of the 4 points, marked at the 3 preimages of the fourth.
  So the restriction to product-type strata in MS5 and Theorem P is a genuine defect. Theorem P's proof works on ∏ M̄_w anyway, and should be restated for arbitrary strata.
- **Smoothness again fails** (orbifold sense). The curve C_48 = {w^4 = x(x^3 − 1)} carries several distinct Picard structures:
  - τ: (x, w) ↦ (ωx, ωw) has 5 fixed points: one over x = 0 and four over x = ∞.
  - Its conjugates under lifts of the tetrahedral group A_4, which preserves {0, 1, ω, ω²}, are other Picard structures.
  - The Riemann–Hurwitz argument of Prop. 2, Step 1 (with 5 → 3 and 10 → 4) shows that the generic Picard curve has automorphism group exactly Z/3. So these fixed loci are distinct germs, and V_0^P is singular at C_48.
- So (R-b) is also not a genuine counterexample.

### (R-c) Rigidity of the mechanism among cyclic covers (sketch)
Consider Z/d-covers of M_{0,5} with d prime and data (k_1, …, k_5), with all k_j + k_l ≢ 0 mod d. Then every D_{jl} maps to the same graph (genus (d−1)/2 – genus d−1). So π_i is constant on ∂V_0 iff all bubble covers (k_j, k_l | −(k_j + k_l)) are isomorphic as 1-pointed curves.

Assume that such an isomorphism forces the triples to agree up to a unit and a swap. This is true when the bubble curve has no automorphisms beyond the normalizer of Z/d; that assumption is not checked in general. Then all ratios k_l/k_j lie in {r, r^{−1}}. Five values with this property force r = 1, i.e. all k_j equal, hence d | 5 and d = 5. (For r ≠ 1 one needs r³ = 1 and the values in k_1{1, r, r²}; by pigeonhole two values are equal, giving ratio 1 ∉ {r, r^{−1}}.) So among such covers, Example 1 is the only one. Allowing pairs with k_j + k_l ≡ 0 gives further examples such as the Picard family; there the extra divisors land in non-product strata.

**Not proved / open.**
- (Q1) Is there a SMOOTH (orbifold or coarse) closed totally geodesic surface V_0 ⊂ M_g with a product-type Γ, ∂V_0 1-dimensional and constant on a positive-dimensional factor, such that every 1-dimensional piece of closure(V_0) in a product-type stratum lies in Δ_Γ? By Corollary A, such a V_0 would be a genuine counterexample to MS5.
- (Q2) MS5 itself for smooth V_0. By Theorem 1, any proof must use smoothness (an interior condition) to control the boundary, or must use an input not yet in the chain. I see no mechanism by which interior smoothness would forbid the boundary configuration of Example 1.
- (Q3) Whether the problem's own claim (Y_t constant) holds for families deforming surfaces like Example 1. It is outside the problem, since Example 1 is not smooth. The natural route is (R-a), which needs a level-chart version of (R2), the input (O), and a version of (T0) allowing u ≠ i.

**Assessment for the chain.** MS5 as formulated is probably too strong. It forbids G = Γ, and also forbids non-product strata (R-b). Neither restriction is needed by the argument of Theorem P in the form sketched in (R-a). A weaker hypothesis would suffice for pinning, and is more plausible: some branch at a cusp, in any stratum including Γ, merges a component that is provably Y_t at time t into a vertex whose projection is a rigid Teichmüller curve. The twin issue (PR-G) is the real obstruction there.

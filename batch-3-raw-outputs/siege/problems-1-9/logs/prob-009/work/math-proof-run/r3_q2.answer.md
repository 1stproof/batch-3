## 0. Standing conventions and hypotheses

**Readings.** (R1) and (R2) are used exactly as in the brief (coarse reading). (R2^orb) is never used, and V_t is never assumed totally geodesic for t ≠ 0. Notation:
- X_t := closure(V_t) ⊂ M̄_g.
- G is a product-type stratum, E_0 is a piece, and E_t := H_t(E_0).
- Ê_t is the normalisation of E_t, with ν_t : Ê_t → E_t.
- W, 𝓦^ν, n, p̂, B, D_1, D_3, R, S, Orb are as in round14_q7 (Theorem JD).
- (Rig-v) is assumed wherever case (A2) or (B2) is discussed, since Theorem JD assumes it.

**(Pure).** At the points P ∈ E_t considered, Aut(P) = ∏_w Aut(P_w, special points). That is, every automorphism fixes every node and every branch of every node. I take this to be part of "product type", which the problem itself imposes ("we exclude those" quotients).

**Facts on the local structure of M̄_g, cited from memory** (deformation theory of stable curves; Deligne–Mumford, Arbarello–Cornalba–Griffiths vol. II ch. XI).
- **(Ch1) Chart.** For P ∈ Δ_G there are a ball B ⊂ ℂ^{3g−3}, on which A := Aut(P) acts linearly, with centre P̂, and a map q : B → M̄_g. The map q induces a homeomorphism (indeed an isomorphism of analytic spaces) from B/A onto an open neighbourhood U of P.
- **(Ch2) Coordinates.** There are coordinates (s, τ) = ((s_w)_w, (τ_e)_{e∈E(G)}) on B such that:
  - {τ_e = 0} is the locus where the node e persists;
  - q^{-1}(Δ_G) = {τ = 0};
  - q^{-1}(M_g) = {all τ_e ≠ 0};
  - the slice {τ = 0} is A-equivariantly a product of neighbourhoods of P̂_w in the Teichmüller spaces T_w of the pointed components.
- **(Ch3) Action on the smoothing coordinates.** Under (Pure), a = (a_w)_w ∈ A acts on τ_e by multiplication by ζ_e(a) := a′_{w′}(n′_e)·a′_{w″}(n″_e). Here n′_e and n″_e are the two branch points of the node e, and a′ denotes the derivative at the fixed point.

Under (Pure) and (Ch2), the joint vertex map of E_t **lifts locally at x ∈ Ê_t** if the germ ν_t : (Ê_t, x) → ∏_w M_w factors holomorphically through the slice {τ = 0} ≅ ∏_w (ball in T_w). A point x where it does not lift is called a **cone point of E_t** (manifold sense).

**Topologically generic points.** P′ ∈ E_0 is *topologically generic* if both of the following hold:
- (a) X_0 ∩ ∂M̄_g = E_0 near P′;
- (b) the pair (X_0, E_0) is locally homeomorphic at P′ to (D × Y, D × {0}). Here D is a disc and Y is a cone with vertex 0 and Y ≠ {0}.

**Lemma 0 (generic points are cofinite).** All but finitely many points of every compact subset of closure(E_0) ∩ Δ_G are topologically generic. The same holds near the punctures: points of E_0 close enough to a puncture point are topologically generic.

*Proof.*
- Take a Whitney stratification of the compact analytic set X_0 ⊂ M̄_g that is compatible with the closed analytic sets X_0 ∩ closure(Δ_{G′}), for all strata G′, and with closure(E_0). It exists and has finitely many strata (cited from memory).
- The 0-dimensional strata form a finite set F.
- At a point P′ ∈ E_0 ∖ F, the stratum through P′ is an open subset of E_0, of real dimension 2.
- The Thom–Mather first isotopy lemma (cited from memory) gives a local homeomorphism (X_0, E_0) ≅ (D × Y, D × {0}), where Y is the cone over the link of a normal slice. Y ≠ {0} because X_0 is pure 2-dimensional and E_0 is 1-dimensional.
- For (a): every other boundary component of X_0 is a closed analytic set that meets closure(E_0) in finitely many points. Add these points to F.
- Near a puncture, the finitely many points of F accumulate nowhere except possibly at the puncture point itself, which lies in a deeper stratum and not in E_0. ∎

## 1. Lemma CP (topological cone-point lemma)

**Hypothesis (NoInt) at P ∈ E_t.** Let X̂ := q^{-1}(X_t) ⊂ B and let K_X := {a ∈ A : a fixes X̂ pointwise near P̂}. Then (NoInt) at P means: there is a neighbourhood B′ ⊂ B of P̂ such that every a ∈ A that fixes some point of q^{-1}(V_t) ∩ B′ lies in K_X.

**Lemma CP.** Let t ∈ Δ, let P′ ∈ E_0 be topologically generic, and put P := H_t(P′). Let x ∈ Ê_t be the preimage of P; it is unique by transport (Step 1 below). Assume (Pure) and (NoInt) at P. Then the joint vertex map of E_t lifts locally at x.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (transport).** H_t is a homeomorphism X_0 → X_t that preserves strata.
- So (a) holds at P: X_t ∩ ∂M̄_g = E_t near P, and X_t ∖ E_t = V_t near P.
- E_t is a topological disc near P. Hence E_t is unibranch at P, and x is the unique preimage of P.

**Step 2 (the preimage of E_t).** Put Ê := q^{-1}(E_t) ∩ B. Shrinking B, Ê = X̂ ∩ {τ = 0}.
- Ê is a curve germ at P̂. Its irreducible branches Ê_1, …, Ê_r are permuted transitively by A, because E_t is unibranch at P.
- Branches meet only at P̂.
- Let S_1 := Stab_A(Ê_1) and N_1 := {a ∈ S_1 : a|_{Ê_1} = id}. N_1 is normal in S_1, and K_X ⊂ N_1.
- **(i) Stabilisers along Ê_1.** If a ∈ A fixes some x̂ ∈ Ê_1 ∖ P̂, then a ∈ N_1. Indeed, a maps x̂ to a point of Ê_1 ∖ P̂, so aÊ_1 = Ê_1. Moreover a acts on the normalisation of Ê_1 (a disc, mapped homeomorphically onto Ê_1 because Ê_1 is unibranch) as a finite-order holomorphic automorphism fixing the centre. Such an automorphism is a rotation, and a rotation with a second fixed point is the identity. Hence Stab_A(x̂) = N_1 for every x̂ ∈ Ê_1 ∖ P̂.
- **(ii) The branch over the punctured disc.** q|_{Ê_1} : Ê_1 ∖ P̂ → E_t ∖ P (near P) is the quotient by S_1/N_1. If q(x̂) = q(x̂′) with x̂, x̂′ ∈ Ê_1, then x̂′ = a x̂, and aÊ_1 = Ê_1 because branches meet only at P̂. By (i), S_1/N_1 acts freely on Ê_1 ∖ P̂, as a finite cyclic group of rotations. So this map is a connected cyclic covering of degree d := |S_1/N_1|.
- **(iii) The lifting criterion.** The joint map lifts locally at x iff d = 1.
  - If d = 1, q maps Ê_1 bijectively onto the germ of E_t. The two normalisations are then isomorphic, and composing gives a holomorphic lift into {τ = 0}.
  - Conversely, a local lift has image a branch that maps bijectively, so its d is 1. All branches are conjugate under A, so they all have the same d.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that d ≥ 2.

**Step 3 (a covering of the interior).** Let Ā := A/K_X, which acts effectively on X̂. By (NoInt), after shrinking B, Ā acts freely on X̂ ∖ Ê = q^{-1}(V_t) ∩ B. A free action of a finite group on a Hausdorff space has quotient map a covering. So q : X̂ ∖ Ê → V_t ∩ U is a finite covering map.

**Step 4 (a null-homotopic loop near E_t).** Use the product chart (D × Y, D × {0}) at P′ from genericity.
- Let β_0(θ) := (r e^{iθ}, 0), θ ∈ [0, 2π], with small r > 0. It generates π_1 of the punctured disc E_0 ∖ P′ near P′.
- For y ∈ Y ∖ {0}, put γ_0^y(θ) := (r e^{iθ}, y). This is a loop in V_0 = X_0 ∖ E_0. It is null-homotopic in N ∖ E_0, where N := D × Y: contract the first coordinate inside the disc D and keep y fixed.
- As y → 0, γ_0^y → β_0 uniformly.
- Put β := H_t∘β_0 and γ := H_t∘γ_0^y. Take N so small that H_t(N) ⊂ U.
- Then β generates π_1 of the punctured disc E_t ∖ P near P.
- γ is a loop in V_t ∩ U, null-homotopic in V_t ∩ U, because H_t maps V_0 into V_t.
- γ is uniformly as close to β as we like (uniform continuity of H_t on the compact set N), once y is small.

**Step 5 (its lift does not close).**
- Lift β to a path β̂ in Ê_1 ∖ P̂ starting at some x̂. By Step 2(ii), β̂ ends at s x̂ with s ∈ S_1 ∖ N_1, since β generates and d ≥ 2.
- Fix an A-invariant metric on B. By Step 2(i) every point of Ê_1 ∖ P̂ has stabiliser exactly N_1, so the orbit type is constant along the compact path β̂. Hence there is ρ_0 > 0 such that, for every θ, the balls B(aβ̂(θ), ρ_0), a ∈ A, are equal or disjoint, and they are equal exactly when aβ̂(θ) coincide.
- Choose y so small that γ(θ) lies in q(B(β̂(θ), ρ_0)) for all θ.
- Let γ̂ be the lift of γ with γ̂(0) ∈ B(x̂, ρ_0). The set of θ with γ̂(θ) ∈ B(β̂(θ), ρ_0) is open and closed. Here we use that the preimage of q(B(β̂(θ), ρ_0)) is the disjoint union of the balls around the orbit points. So γ̂(2π) ∈ B(s x̂, ρ_0).
- By Step 3, γ̂(2π) = b·γ̂(0) for a unique b ∈ Ā. Then B(b x̂, ρ_0) ∩ B(s x̂, ρ_0) ≠ ∅, so b x̂ = s x̂, and s^{-1}b ∈ Stab(x̂) = N_1 (mod K_X ⊂ N_1). Hence b ∈ sN_1, so b ≠ 1.
- But γ is null-homotopic in V_t ∩ U, so by homotopy lifting its lift is closed, i.e. b = 1. Contradiction.

Hence d = 1, and the joint map lifts at x. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. Lemma CP uses only H_t as a stratum-preserving homeomorphism. Neither automorphism groups nor orbifold structures need to be transported, so it is a genuinely coarse-reading statement. The point is that X_t is **2-dimensional**: a cone point of the boundary curve that is not "absorbed" by interior fixed curves is detected by the topology of the interior near the curve. For curves alone this fails, since the quotient of a disc by a rotation is a disc; that is the reason recorded in round7_q9 §2.1.
2. **(NoInt) cannot be dropped.** Example: A = ℤ/m acting on ℂ² by (w, τ) ↦ (ζw, τ), with X̂ = ℂ² and Ê = {τ = 0}. Then X = ℂ²/A ≅ ℂ² with coordinates (w^m, τ), and E = {τ = 0}. The pair is topologically generic, yet Ê → E is m : 1, so there is a cone point. Here A fixes the interior curve {w = 0}, so (NoInt) fails.

## 2. Lemma NJ (orbifold fibres give cone points) and Lemma BAL (sufficient conditions for (NoInt))

**Lemma NJ.** Assume (Rig-v). Let x ∈ Ê_t with p_t(x) = y ∈ Orb of orbifold order m, and let e be the local degree of p_t at x. If m ∤ e, then x is a cone point of E_t.

*Proof.*
- Suppose the joint map lifts at x. Its j-coordinate L_j : (disc, x) → T_j satisfies π_j∘L_j = π_j∘ν_t, which takes values in C (Rig-v).
- Near the point L_j(x), π^{-1}(C) ⊂ T_j is a finite union of Teichmüller discs g·D through L_j(x), by proper discontinuity. L_j is non-constant with irreducible image, so it lands in one of them, D_i.
- The map D_i → C^ν (normalisation) is q_{Γ′} after an isometric identification D_i ≅ H. Its local degree at the point over y is the orbifold order m.
- p_t = (D_i → C^ν)∘L_j near x, so e = m·deg_x(L_j). Hence m | e. ∎

**Lemma BAL.** Assume (Pure) at P ∈ E_t, and P ∈ Δ_G with Step 1 of Lemma CP valid (X_t ∖ E_t = V_t near P). If a ∈ A fixes a point of q^{-1}(V_t), then a is *balanced*: ζ_e(a) = 1 for all e ∈ E(G). Consequently (NoInt) holds at P if every balanced element of Aut(P) lies in K_X. In particular, (NoInt) holds at P if both of the following hold:
- (b1) no component P_w with w ≠ j has a non-trivial automorphism fixing its special points;
- (b2) j has at least one non-loop edge.

*Proof.*
- A point of q^{-1}(V_t) has all τ_e ≠ 0, by (Ch2). Its being fixed forces ζ_e(a)τ_e = τ_e, i.e. ζ_e(a) = 1.
- Under (b1), a = (a_j, id, …, id). For a non-loop edge e at j, ζ_e(a) = a_j′(n′_e).
- A non-trivial finite-order automorphism of a connected curve has a non-trivial derivative at every fixed point: it is linearisable there, and the identity theorem applies.
- So a_j = id and a = 1 ∈ K_X. ∎

## 3. Proposition A2* (case (A2) at the totally geodesic member)

**Hypothesis (Reg_Orb).** Near each point of the fibre 𝓦^ν_0 lying over (Orb × {0}), 𝓦^ν is smooth and t is a submersion. This is weaker than 0 ∉ D_1.

**Proposition A2*.** Assume (Pure), (Rig-v), (Reg_Orb) and case (A2). Then there are y ∈ Orb of order m, x_0 ∈ p_0^{-1}(y) with local degree k ≥ 2, and ε > 0 such that, for every 0 < |t| < ε:
- (a) p_t has n(t) ≥ 2 distinct points z_1(t), …, z_{n(t)}(t) over y near x_0. All of them are cone points of E_t, with local degrees e_i < k ≤ m.
- (b) (NoInt) fails at all but at most one of the points ν_t(z_i(t)).

Consequently, by Lemma BAL, (A2) is excluded whenever, for all small t ≠ 0, conditions (b1) and (b2) hold at the points of E_t over Orb near the fibre of x_0. More generally, (A2) is excluded whenever every balanced automorphism at those points is in K_X.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (ramification leaves an orbifold fibre).**
- In case (A2), for t ∉ D_A ∪ D_1 the map p_t ramifies over some non-orbifold point. This is the proof of Proposition A of round14_q7.
- By Lemma RH(b), the total ramification of the proper map p_t : Ê_t → C° equals R_0, which at t = 0 lies entirely over Orb (TV item 3). So Σ_{y∈Orb} ρ_y(t) < Σ_y ρ_y(0) for these t.
- There are finitely many y, so a single y has ρ_y(t) < ρ_y(0) for t in a set T accumulating at 0.

**Step 2 (splitting at one point).**
- By (Reg_Orb), near each x_0 ∈ p_0^{-1}(y) there are coordinates (z, t) on 𝓦^ν with p̂ = (P(z, t), t) and P(z, 0) = z^k, in a coordinate centred at y.
- By Weierstrass preparation, P = unit·W with W(z, t) = z^k + c_{k−1}(t)z^{k−1} + … + c_0(t).
- The fibre of 𝓝_t near x_0 is a disc in Ê_t. By properness, for small t every point of p_t^{-1}(y) lies near some x_0.
- If W(·, t) = (z − a(t))^k, then the ramification of p_t over y near x_0 equals k − 1, its value at t = 0.
- So for t ∈ T some x_0 has W(·, t) ≠ (z + c_{k−1}(t)/k)^k. That identity is a system of analytic equations in t, so it fails on a set accumulating at 0 only if it holds on at most a discrete set.
- Fix such an x_0 and shrink ε. For 0 < |t| < ε, W(·, t) then has n(t) ≥ 2 distinct roots z_i(t) → x_0, of multiplicities e_i with Σ e_i = k, so e_i < k.
- k | m, by orbifold-unramifiedness at t = 0 (TV item 3). So m ∤ e_i, and Lemma NJ gives (a).

**Step 3 (transport near x_0).** Let Ĥ_t : Ê_0 → Ê_t be the transported homeomorphism of normalisations, with ν_t∘Ĥ_t = H_t∘ν_0.
- By Lemma Red the fibre 𝓦^ν_0 is reduced, irreducible and birational onto W_0. So over a unibranch point ν_0(x) it has exactly one point, namely x.
- Fix r > 0 so small that every x ≠ x_0 with |z(x)| ≤ r is topologically generic (Lemma 0) and ν_0(x) is unibranch.
- **Joint continuity at (x, 0) for such x.** If (x_n, t_n) → (x, 0), then Ĥ_{t_n}(x_n) ∈ n^{-1}(H_{t_n}ν_0(x_n), t_n). By properness of n, its limit points lie in n^{-1}(ν_0(x), 0) = {x}.
- By compactness, for small t, Ĥ_t maps the circle |z| = r into the annulus r − δ < |z| < r + δ.
- Ĥ_t(closed disc) is compact, while the outer complementary component of the Jordan curve Ĥ_t(circle) in Ê_t has non-compact closure, since Ê_t has punctures. So Ĥ_t({|z| < r}) is the inner component, and it contains {|z| < r − δ}.
- So each z_i(t) (t small) equals Ĥ_t(x_i′) with |z(x_i′)| < r. The x_i′ are distinct, so at most one of them equals x_0.
- For x_i′ ≠ x_0, ν_t(z_i(t)) = H_t(ν_0(x_i′)) with ν_0(x_i′) topologically generic.
- By Lemma CP, if (NoInt) held there, z_i(t) would not be a cone point, contradicting (a). This proves (b). ∎

**Remark (the case 0 ∈ D_1 off the orbifold fibres).** (Reg_Orb) only constrains the fibre over Orb. Singularities of 𝓦^ν_0 over non-orbifold points (the "fixed-image (ram)" mechanism at a singular fibre point, case (J) of round14_q7) are harmless for Step 1. Step 1 only uses RH(b), which holds for every t, and upper semicontinuity. What (Reg_Orb) is needed for is the local model at x_0. If 𝓦^ν_0 is singular at a point over Orb, Step 2 must be replaced by a local analysis of a singular fibre. This is not done here (GAP A2-sing).

## 4. Proposition B2* (case (A1)+(B2))

Assume (A1). Then 𝓦^ν ≅ S̄ × Δ is smooth (round14_q7 §2). Let Z be a horizontal component of B̄ that is not a puncture line, and for small t ≠ 0 let (x(t), t) ∈ Z ∩ (R × {t}), with x̄(t) := π(x(t)) ∈ S.

**Proposition B2*.** Assume (Pure), (Rig-v), (A1) and (B2).
- **(i)** If 0 ∉ D_3, then Z̄ meets t = 0 only at punctures, and for all small t ≠ 0 (NoInt) fails at ν_t(x̄(t)).
- **(ii)** If 0 ∈ D_3 and x̄(t) → c, where c is a cone point of O_0 = H/Λ_E and x̄(t) ≠ c, then (NoInt) fails at ν_t(x̄(t)) for small t ≠ 0.
- **(iii)** Not covered:
  - (iii-a) Z = {x_c} × Δ with x_c over a cone point c, i.e. the joint cone order at a fixed cone point increases;
  - (iii-b) x̄(t) → x̄_0 with x̄_0 not a cone point of O_0, and ν_t(x̄(t)) = H_t(ν_0(x̄_0)) for a topologically special point ν_0(x̄_0).

*Proof.*

**(i).**
- At t = 0, (iii) of Proposition V holds by Lemma J0. If 0 ∉ D_3, Lemma B2 gives B_S ∩ (R × {0}) = ∅, so Z̄ ∩ {t = 0} consists of punctures.
- For small t ∉ D_3, Lemma B2(⇒) shows that g_t∘π has no local lift at x(t).
- x̄(t) tends to a puncture, so for small t it is not one of the finitely many cone points of O_0. There π is a local biholomorphism, so x̄(t) is a cone point (manifold sense) of E_t.
- ν_t(x̄(t)) tends to a puncture point. By continuity of H^{-1}, H_t^{-1}(ν_t(x̄(t))) is a point of E_0 near that puncture point, and it is topologically generic by Lemma 0.
- Lemma CP then forces (NoInt) to fail. (The set D_3 ∖ {0} is discrete, so "small t" can be taken to avoid it.)

**(ii).**
- Under (A1), p_t = p_0. At c the j-map has local degree k with k | m and k < m, so c is a cone point at every t (Lemma NJ).
- Run Step 3 of Proposition A2* on S̄ × Δ, which is smooth, near c. The two distinct cone points c and x̄(t) both lie in Ĥ_t(small disc around c). At most one of them is Ĥ_t(c).
- The other is H_t of a topologically generic point. Lemma CP then forces (NoInt) to fail there. ∎

## 6. Final status (item by item)

**Proved.** Under (R1), (R2) (coarse reading) and (Pure). Prior results are used as quoted: Lemma Red, Lemma RH, TV item 3, J0, Lemma B2, Proposition A of round14_q7. The following are cited from memory: Whitney stratifications and the Thom–Mather isotopy lemma, the deformation-theoretic chart (Ch1)–(Ch3), Weierstrass preparation, and homotopy lifting.
- **Lemma 0.** Topologically generic points of E_0 are cofinite, including near the punctures.
- **Lemma CP.** At a point of E_t that is H_t-transported from a topologically generic point of E_0, the joint vertex map lifts locally, unless (NoInt) fails there. That is, unless some automorphism of the stable curve that is non-trivial on the germ of X̂ fixes interior points of V_t accumulating at the point. This holds under the coarse reading, with no transport of automorphism groups.
- **Lemma NJ.** Points of Ê_t over an orbifold point of order m with local degree e, m ∤ e, are cone points.
- **Lemma BAL.** Only *balanced* automorphisms (ζ_e = 1 on every edge) can violate (NoInt). Sufficient conditions are (b1) and (b2).
- **Proposition A2*.** Under (Reg_Orb), case (A2) forces, for every small t ≠ 0, at least one new cone point over an orbifold point at which (NoInt) fails, i.e. a balanced non-trivial automorphism fixing an interior curve of V_t through it. Hence (A2) is **excluded** under (Reg_Orb) whenever (b1) and (b2), or more generally "balanced ⇒ K_X", hold at those points for small t ≠ 0.
- **Proposition B2*(i), (ii).** The analogous statement for (B2) when the new joint cone point emerges from a puncture (always the case if 0 ∉ D_3), or from an existing cone point while splitting off it.

**Not proved.**
- Exclusion of (A2) and (B2) without (NoInt). I do not claim (NoInt) holds in general.
- **Warning (heuristic, not proved).** For covering-construction surfaces V_0, every curve of V_0 has the deck group as automorphisms. Extra automorphisms at elliptic points of the boundary Teichmüller curve plausibly come from symmetric branch configurations that persist along interior curves. If so, (NoInt) fails already at t = 0 at such points. So the residual mechanism is not obviously vacuous.

**Open (named).**
1. **(NoInt-res).** Can, for all small t ≠ 0, a new cone point of a boundary piece carry a balanced automorphism fixing an interior curve of V_t, while V_0 is totally geodesic? This is the only way (A2) (under (Reg_Orb)) and (B2)(i), (ii) can occur.
2. **(GAP A2-sing).** Case (A2) when 𝓦^ν_0 is singular at a point over Orb.
3. **(B2-iii).** Mechanisms (iii-a) (cone-order increase at a fixed cone point) and (iii-b) (emergence at the transport of a topologically special non-cone point). Lemma CP detects whether a point is a cone point, but not its cone order, and it gives nothing at special points.
4. Step (4): (Rig-v) at image-level type jumps. Step (5): non-product strata.

Entry 82 under the coarse reading: **not proved / open**. The partial results above reduce case (J), in the subcases covered, to the interior-automorphism mechanism (NoInt-res).

## 1. The example

**Setting.**
- Let Φ : M_{0,5} -> M_6 send (P^1; a_1, ..., a_5) to the smooth projective model X of y^5 = prod_{j=1}^5 (x - a_j).
- The cover x : X -> P^1 is cyclic of degree 5, with deck group H = <y -> ζ y>, ζ = e^{2πi/5}. It is totally ramified over each a_j, with local monodromy 1 in Z/5 (the exponent). It is unramified over ∞ because 5 ≡ 0 mod 5.
- Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2g - 2 = 5(-2) + 5·4 = 10, so g = 6.
- Φ is invariant under S_5, since the equation does not depend on the order of the a_j.
- Put V_0 := Φ(M_{0,5}).

### 1.1 V_0 is a closed 2-dimensional totally geodesic subvariety of M_6
- **Dimension 2.** The map lifts to Φ~ : T_{0,5} -> T_6 (choose a lift of the topological cover). By (TG), Φ~ is an isometric embedding for the Teichmüller metrics. These are Finsler norms on tangent spaces (Royden), so dΦ~ is injective and Φ~ is an immersion. Hence V_0 has dimension 2.
- **Totally geodesic.**
  - Let X ∈ V_0 and let L be a complex tangent line of V_0 at X, i.e. the image under dΦ~ of a line L' at a preimage point.
  - The Teichmüller disc of T_{0,5} tangent to L' is generated by some q with at most simple poles at the a_j. It maps under Φ~ to a holomorphic isometric disc through X tangent to L. That disc is the Teichmüller disc of the pullback x^*q, which is holomorphic on X: a simple pole pulled back under z = w^5 has order 5(-1+2) - 2 = 3.
  - A complex tangent line determines the Teichmüller disc tangent to it uniquely (uniqueness of the extremal Beltrami differential k q̄/|q| in its infinitesimal class). So every Teichmüller disc tangent to V_0 at X lies in Φ~(T_{0,5}), and its image lies in V_0.
- **Closed.**
  - By (AC), Φ extends to a continuous (indeed algebraic) map Φ̄ : M̄_{0,5} -> M̄_6. It sends a stable 5-pointed base to the stable model of the admissible cover. For an abelian cover with fixed monodromy, that cover is unique.
  - Φ̄(∂M̄_{0,5}) ⊂ ∂M̄_6, because every component of the covers listed in 1.2 has genus 2, 4 or 6 with nodes between them, so stabilization contracts nothing and the nodes survive.
  - Hence closure(V_0) = Φ̄(M̄_{0,5}) (compactness of M̄_{0,5}), and V_0 = closure(V_0) ∩ M_6 is closed in M_6.

### 1.2 The boundary of closure(V_0)

**Divisors D_{jk} ⊂ M̄_{0,5}.** Take the generic point of D_{12}. Its base is a bubble A carrying a_1, a_2 and a node q, glued to a main sphere M carrying a_3, a_4, a_5 and q.
- Local monodromies (which sum to 0 mod 5 on each component):
  - on A: a_1, a_2 have 1, and q has -2 ≡ 3;
  - on M: a_3, a_4, a_5 have 1, and q has 2.
- All of these are units mod 5, so every branch point, including the node, is totally ramified, and there is exactly one point over q on each side.
- Riemann–Hurwitz: the cover of A has 2g - 2 = -10 + 3·4 = 2, so genus 2. The cover of M has 2g - 2 = -10 + 4·4 = 6, so genus 4. They meet in one separating node, and 2 + 4 = 6.
- So Φ̄(D_{12}°) ⊂ Δ_Γ, where Γ is the graph with a vertex i of genus 2 and a vertex u of genus 4, joined by one edge.
- Δ_Γ ≅ M_{2,1} × M_{4,1} is of product type, since the gluing map is bijective: the genera differ, so the graph has no automorphism.
- On D_{12}°:
  - π_i is constant, equal to Y_0 := (the curve y^5 = x(x-1), the unique point over x = ∞).
  - π_u is the covering construction (P^1; a_3, a_4, a_5, q) -> (y^5 = (x-a_3)(x-a_4)(x-a_5)(x-q)^2, point over q). This is non-constant by (TG) applied to M_{0,4} -> M_{4,1}.
- By S_5-invariance, Φ̄(D_{jk}°) = Φ̄(D_{12}°) for all ten pairs jk.

**Codimension-2 points.** Take D_{12} ∩ D_{34}. Its base is a chain A(a_1, a_2) - B(a_5) - C(a_3, a_4).
- Monodromies: on A, (1, 1 ; 3); on B, (2 ; 1 ; 2); on C, (3 ; 1, 1). All are units.
- So the cover is a chain X_A - X_B - X_C of three genus-2 curves, by Riemann–Hurwitz with three totally ramified points on each component (total genus 2 + 2 + 2 = 6). It has two separating nodes, n_1 = X_A ∩ X_B and n_2 = X_B ∩ X_C.
- X_A and X_C, each with its node point, are both isomorphic to Y_0, because they are covers with identical monodromy data (1, 1 ; 3).
- All ten points D_{jk} ∩ D_{lm} ({j,k} ∩ {l,m} = ∅) of M̄_{0,5} are of this type up to S_5. These points are the whole of the codimension-2 boundary of M̄_{0,5}.

**Consequences.**
- (1) ∂V_0 := closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_Γ = Φ̄(D_{12}°). It is irreducible and 1-dimensional, and π_i(∂V_0) = {Y_0} with dim M_{2,1} = 4 > 0. **All hypotheses of MS5 except smoothness hold** (smoothness: see 1.3).
- (2) **Every 1-dimensional irreducible component of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G, for any stratum Δ_G, is ∂V_0 (and G = Γ).**
  - closure(V_0) \ V_0 = Φ̄(∂M̄_{0,5}) is the union of Φ̄(D_{12}°) ⊂ Δ_Γ and finitely many points, which lie in the chain stratum.
  - For G the trivial graph, closure(V_0) ∩ M_6 = V_0 is irreducible of dimension 2, so it has no 1-dimensional component.
- (3) closure(∂V_0) \ ∂V_0 is the finite set of chain points P_0 = X_A - X_B - X_C described above.

### 1.3 Smoothness fails at finitely many interior points (so this is not a strict counterexample)

**Lemma 1.3a (generic automorphism group).**
- (i) A generic X ∈ V_0 has Aut(X) = H.
- (ii) The set of X ∈ V_0 having two distinct subgroups of "type H" is finite. Here a subgroup of type H is cyclic of order 5, with quotient P^1, and with 5 fixed points each of rotation type 1.

*Proof.*
1. Let X ∈ V_0 and G = Aut(X) ⊇ H. Then X/G is a quotient of X/H = P^1, so it has genus 0. Let r be the number of branch points of X -> X/G, with orders e_j.
2. Riemann–Hurwitz: 10 = |G|(-2 + Σ_j (1 - 1/e_j)).
3. The curves with a given topological G-action form a family of dimension r - 3.
4. Suppose H is normal in G. Then G/H acts on X/H = P^1 preserving the 5 branch points. For a generic configuration this group is trivial. This settles (i) once the non-normal case is excluded for generic X.
5. Suppose H is not normal. Then G has at least 6 Sylow 5-subgroups, so |G| ≥ 30.
6. If r ≥ 5, then -2 + Σ(1 - 1/e_j) ≥ 1/2, so |G| ≤ 20, a contradiction. So r ≤ 4.
7. If r = 4, we need Σ 1/e_j = 2 - 10/|G| ≥ 5/3. Every solution was enumerated:
   - (2,2,2,3) with |G| = 60;
   - (2,2,2,4) with |G| = 40;
   - (2,2,2,6) with |G| = 30;
   - (2,2,3,3) with |G| = 30.
8. Groups of order 30 or 40 have a normal Sylow 5-subgroup, which rules them out.
9. For order 60 the only group with 6 Sylow 5-subgroups is A_5. Its signature (2,2,2,3) has no point with stabilizer of order 5, so H would act freely. Then X/H would have genus 2, not 0. Contradiction.
10. Hence r ≤ 3. Such curves are rigid: there are finitely many of them in genus 6.
11. Suppose X has two distinct type-H subgroups H, H'. There are two cases.
    - If H is not normal in G, then X is one of the finitely many curves of step 10.
    - If H is normal in G, then H'H/H ≅ Z/5 is a nontrivial subgroup of G/H. It acts on X/H = P^1 preserving the 5 branch points. A Z/5 ⊂ PGL_2 has exactly two fixed points, and all its other orbits have size 5. So the 5 branch points form one orbit, and the configuration is the 5th roots of unity, which is a single point of M_{0,5}/S_5 (this is the Fermat quintic).

    Either way, X lies in a finite set. ∎

**Lemma 1.3b (V_0 is singular).**
- (1) Coarse sense. Let p_1 = {0, 1, i, -1, -i} and X_1 = Φ(p_1), i.e. y^5 = x^5 - x. Then the germ of V_0 at X_1 is not a topological 2-manifold germ.
- (2) Orbifold sense. Let X_F be the Fermat quintic x^5 + y^5 + z^5 = 0, which lies in V_0. The preimage of V_0 in T_6 is not a submanifold at any lift of X_F.

*Proof of (1).*
1. **Stabilizer of p_1.** The point 0 is distinguished in p_1:
   - the other four points have harmonic cross-ratio;
   - the cross-ratio of {0, i, -1, -i} is 1 - i, which is not in {-1, 2, 1/2}.
   So the stabilizer of p_1 in PGL_2 fixes 0. It lies in the stabilizer D_4 of the square that fixes 0, which is ⟨x -> ix⟩ ≅ Z/4.
2. **Action on T_{p_1} M_{0,5} = (⊕_j T_{a_j} P^1)/sl_2.** The generator r : x -> ix acts on ⊕_j T_{a_j} with characters {i} ∪ {1, i, -1, -i}. It acts on sl_2 = ⟨∂_x, x∂_x, x^2∂_x⟩ with characters {i, 1, -i}.
3. So r acts on T_{p_1} M_{0,5} as diag(i, -1). The only pseudo-reflection in ⟨r⟩ is r^2. The quotient C^2/⟨r⟩ = (C^2/⟨r^2⟩)/(±1) is the A_1 singularity, whose link is RP^3.
4. **Case n^{-1}(X_1) is a single point**, where n : M_{0,5}/S_5 -> V_0 is the finite surjection.
   - By Lemma 1.3a(ii), n is injective near p_1.
   - So n is a homeomorphism from a neighbourhood of [p_1] onto a neighbourhood of X_1 in V_0 (it is finite and closed).
   - That neighbourhood is homeomorphic to C^2/⟨r⟩, which is not a manifold.
5. **Case n^{-1}(X_1) has two or more points.** Then V_0 has at least two 2-dimensional germs at X_1. They are distinct: otherwise curves near X_1 in an open set would carry two type-H subgroups, contradicting Lemma 1.3a. A union of two distinct 2-dimensional analytic germs is not a smooth germ. ∎

*Proof of (2).*
1. X_F has the three subgroups H_x, H_y, H_z of order 5. Each is of type H. For example, the quotient by z -> ζz is (x:y), branched over x^5 + y^5 = 0 with all rotation data 1.
2. The preimage of V_0 in T_6 is the union of the fixed loci Fix(H~) of all lifts H~ of type-H actions to Mod_6. Each is a copy of T_{0,5}.
3. Fix(H~_x) and Fix(H~_z) both pass through a lift of X_F. Their intersection is 0-dimensional, because a curve in both has a (Z/5)^2-action with quotient a 3-pointed P^1 (rigid). So two distinct 2-dimensional submanifolds cross there. ∎

**Status.** In both readings V_0 is smooth off a finite set of interior points. Lemma 1.3a(ii) supplies finiteness in the orbifold reading. For the coarse reading I did not fully check that the points with nontrivial S_5-stabilizer other than p_1 give smooth points; I did check that the Z/2- and D_3-stabilizers act by reflection groups. Since every S_5-symmetric covering construction factors through M_{0,5}/S_5, the A_1 point at p_1 cannot be removed by changing d within this class.

## 2. MS5 fails for this V_0

**Proposition 2.1.** For V_0, Γ and i as in Section 1, no pair (Δ_G, P_0) satisfies the conclusion of MS5.

*Proof.*
1. MS5 needs a product-type Δ_G and an irreducible 1-dimensional component E^{(a)} of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G with P_0 ∈ closure(E^{(a)}) \ E^{(a)}.
2. By 1.2(2), this forces G = Γ and E^{(a)} = ∂V_0 (s = 1).
3. Then 𝐂^{(1)} = closure in M̄_{2,1} × M̄_{4,1} of ξ_Γ^{-1}(∂V_0) = {Y_0} × closure(π_u(∂V_0)).
4. Let P_0 be any cusp (one exists by 1.2(3)), and take any R ∈ 𝐂^{(1)} with ξ̄_Γ(R) = P_0. Such an R exists by compactness.
5. Then R_i = Y_0 ∈ M_{2,1} is a smooth curve isomorphic to Y_0.
6. So the vertex u = i must satisfy (a), i.e. R_i ∈ ∂M̄_{2,1}. That is false. ∎

**Remarks.**
- (a) fails at the constant vertex itself. The twins X_A ≅ X_C are not needed for this failure, though they matter in Section 3.
- The mechanism is that closure(V_0) has **no** 1-dimensional boundary piece outside Δ_Γ. So no merge branch in the sense of PR-C exists at any cusp. Hypothesis (C-merge) of PR-C fails. Lemma C1 is respected, because the second boundary branch at each cusp again lies in Δ_Γ, in the other local sheet (Section 3).
- Smoothness enters MS5 nowhere near the boundary: closure(V_0) near ∂V_0 ∪ {cusps} is the image of a neighbourhood of ∂M̄_{0,5}, away from the singular points of 1.3. So any proof of MS5 would have to derive a boundary statement from smoothness at isolated interior points. I regard this as implausible but have not excluded it.

## 3. A different pinning configuration: the sheet theorem (proved, modulo Lemma R*)

Readings (R1) and (R2) are in force, with H_0 = id and P_t := H_t(P_0). Throughout, Δ_Γ is product type and π_i(∂V_t) = {Y_t}.

### 3.1 Combinatorial definitions
Let G' be a stable graph, i.e. the dual graph of a stratum in the closure of Δ_Γ.

**Γ-splitting.** A Γ-splitting of G' is a pair (N, φ) where:
- N ⊂ E(G');
- φ : G'/(E(G') \ N) -> Γ is an isomorphism of stable graphs, genus- and leg-preserving. Here G'/(E(G') \ N) is the graph obtained by contracting the edges not in N.

**Clean splitting.** Call (N, φ) clean if φ^{-1}(i) is a single vertex v(N, φ) of G' with no edge of G' contracted into it.

**The set 𝒩_e.** For an edge e of G', let 𝒩_e be the set of clean Γ-splittings with e ∈ N.

**Hypothesis (S) at a point P_0 ∈ closure(∂V_0) \ ∂V_0, P_0 ∈ Δ_{G'}.**
- **(S1) No other boundary near P_0.** There is a neighbourhood U of P_0 with (closure(V_0) \ V_0) ∩ U ⊂ Δ_Γ ∪ {P_0}.
- **(S2) Teichmüller vertex.** Let E^{(1)}, ..., E^{(s)} be the components of ∂V_0 with P_0 in their closure. There is a vertex u ≠ i of Γ such that, for each a, the conclusion of Lemma R* (PR-E) holds for E^{(a)} at u: closure(π_u(H_t(E^{(a)}))) = K^{(a)} := closure(π_u(E^{(a)})) for all t, and K^{(a)} is a curve. The input of Lemma R* is that π_u(E^{(a)}) is a Teichmüller curve in the orbifold sense, together with (O).
- **(S3) Combinatorics.** For every choice function e -> (N_e, φ_e) ∈ 𝒩_e (e ∈ E(G')), there are edges e, e' of G' such that:
  - v(N_{e'}, φ_{e'}) is a vertex of the subgraph φ_e^{-1}(u);
  - that subgraph contains at least one edge of G'.

**Theorem P_S.** Assume (R1), (R2), the hypotheses of the problem, and (S) at some P_0. Then Y_t = Y_0 for all t ∈ Δ.

### 3.2 Proof of Theorem P_S

**Step 1 (transport of (S1)).**
- H_t is a homeomorphism closure(V_0) -> closure(V_t) mapping each closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G onto closure(V_t) ∩ Δ_G. This includes G trivial, where it maps V_0 onto V_t.
- So, with U_t := H_t(closure(V_0) ∩ U), which is a neighbourhood of P_t in closure(V_t), we get (closure(V_t) \ V_t) ∩ U_t ⊂ Δ_Γ ∪ {P_t}.
- Also P_t ∈ Δ_{G'}.

**Step 2 (Kuranishi chart).**
- By (DM) there is a smooth germ (B, P̂) with a finite group A = Aut(P_t) acting, and a quotient map q : B -> M̄_6 onto a neighbourhood of P_t, with q^{-1}(P_t) = {P̂}. (The same holds for any genus g.)
- There are coordinates s_e, one for each node e of P_t. The curve over b ∈ B keeps exactly the nodes e with s_e(b) = 0.
- On the locus where exactly the nodes in N are kept, the universal family restricted to it, partially normalized at N, is a family of curves whose dual graph is G'/(E \ N). Its classifying map to ∏ M̄_w is continuous on {s_e = 0 for e ∈ N}. Its value at P̂ is the tuple of subcurves of P_t lying over the vertices of G'/(E \ N).

**Step 3 (both kinds of sheets occur: Krull).**
- Let Ŝ := q^{-1}(closure(V_t)) near P̂. It is pure 2-dimensional, because q is finite and open.
- q^{-1}(V_t) is dense in Ŝ, because q is open. On q^{-1}(V_t) all s_e ≠ 0.
- Fix a node e. On every irreducible component of Ŝ through P̂, s_e is not identically zero and vanishes at P̂. So Z_e := Ŝ ∩ {s_e = 0} has pure dimension 1 at P̂, by the active lemma / Krull's principal ideal theorem for analytic germs.
- Take a branch Z of Z_e. Its points z ≠ P̂ lie over boundary points of closure(V_t) other than P_t, so q(z) ∈ ∂V_t ⊂ Δ_Γ by Step 1.
- The set N(z) of nodes kept at z is constant on Z \ {P̂} near P̂: it is determined by which s_f vanish identically on the irreducible germ Z. And e ∈ N(z).
- Since q(z) ∈ Δ_Γ and Δ_Γ is product type, ξ_Γ^{-1}(q(z)) is a single point. Hence the dual graph G'/(E \ N) carries a well-defined identification φ with Γ. This identification is constant along Z \ {P̂}, being locally constant on a connected set.
- **Cleanness.** Along Z, the i-part π_i(q(z)) = Y_t is constant (hypothesis on ∂V_t). By Step 2 it converges to the subcurve of P_t over φ^{-1}(i). So that subcurve is isomorphic to the smooth curve Y_t. It therefore has no node, and φ^{-1}(i) is a single vertex with no internal edge.
- So (N, φ) ∈ 𝒩_e. Write (N_e, φ_e) for one such splitting, one for each e. Then:
  - (α) the component X_{v_e} of P_t at the vertex v_e := v(N_e, φ_e), with its special points, is isomorphic to Y_t up to relabelling;
  - (β) along the branch Z, π_u(q(z)) converges in M̄_u to the stable curve P_t^{(u, e)} := the subcurve of P_t over φ_e^{-1}(u), with its special points.

**Step 4 (pinning).**
- By (S3) applied to this choice function, there are e, e' such that v_{e'} ∈ φ_e^{-1}(u) and P_t^{(u,e)} has a node.
- The branch Z used for e lies in q^{-1}(H_t(E^{(a)})) for some a. This holds because q(Z \ P̂) is a connected subset of ∂V_t near P_t; it is contained in one H_t(E^{(a)}) up to finitely many points, and the components with P_t in their closure are the H_t(E^{(a)}).
- By (S2), π_u(q(z)) ∈ K^{(a)} for every z, so the limit P_t^{(u,e)} ∈ K^{(a)} ∩ ∂M̄_u.
- By (α) for e', Y_t is isomorphic, up to relabelling, to a component of P_t^{(u,e)}.
- Let F be the set of n_i-pointed smooth curves isomorphic, up to relabelling, to a component of a point of ∪_a (K^{(a)} ∩ ∂M̄_u).
  - Each K^{(a)} is an irreducible compact curve meeting M_u, so K^{(a)} ∩ ∂M̄_u is finite.
  - Hence F is finite.
- So Y_t ∈ F for every t ∈ Δ.

**Step 5.** t -> Y_t is continuous (PR-A) and takes values in the finite set F on the connected set Δ. Hence it is constant. ∎

**Remarks.**
- (S3) is checked at each t separately, using only the curve P_t. So the twin/automorphism issue of PR-G does not arise: no identification of nodes across different t is needed.
- (S1) replaces the merge of (T0). The pinning comes from Krull's theorem forcing every node of P_t to be the "Γ-edge" of some boundary branch.

### 3.3 The example satisfies (S1), (S3), and the isometric-lift part of (S2)

Let P_0 be the chain X_A - X_B - X_C of 1.2, so G' is the chain with three genus-2 vertices and two edges n_1, n_2.

- **(S1)** holds by 1.2(2), since Φ̄^{-1}(P_0) consists of codimension-2 points of M̄_{0,5}.
- **(S3).** Γ has a single edge. So a Γ-splitting is N = {n_1} or N = {n_2}, with φ determined by the genera.
  - For N = {n_1}: the i-vertex is the end vertex X_1 at n_1, which is clean. The u-part is X_B ∪_{n_2} X_2, which has the node n_2.
  - For N = {n_2}: symmetrically, the i-vertex is X_2 and the u-part is X_1 ∪_{n_1} X_B.

  So 𝒩_{n_1} and 𝒩_{n_2} are singletons. Take e = n_1, e' = n_2: then v_{e'} = X_2 lies in the u-part of N_{n_1}, which has a node. ✓
- **(S2).** u is the genus-4 vertex and ∂V_0 = E^{(1)}. The map π_u ∘ ν is the covering construction (P^1; a_3, a_4, a_5, q) -> (y^5 = (x-a_3)(x-a_4)(x-a_5)(x-q)^2, point over q). Its lift T_{0,4} -> T_{4,1} is the pullback map, a holomorphic isometric embedding by (TG). So F is an isometry onto a Teichmüller disc.

  What I have **not** verified:
  - that the normalization Ẽ of ∂V_0 carries an orbifold structure whose orbifold group admits a homomorphism ρ to Mod_{4,1} making F equivariant. Lifts of mapping classes through the Z/5-cover are defined only modulo the deck group, so one gets a homomorphism to N(H)/H, and one needs a splitting, possibly after enlarging cone orders (Lemma O1 of PR-E);
  - condition (O) itself.

  Both belong to the orbifold input that the chain already treats as open (step 3 of the chain).

**Corollary 3.4.** For the V_0 of Section 1 (which violates MS5), every deformation family as in the problem, under (R1), (R2) and (S2), has Y_t = Y_0 for all t.

Concretely:

- Hence Y_t lies in a finite set, and is constant.

## 4. Consequences for the chain

1. **MS5 should be dropped as a general input.** Section 2 shows that its conclusion fails for a totally geodesic surface that satisfies every other hypothesis and is smooth near its whole boundary. Theorem P with (T0) therefore cannot be the only pinning mechanism.
2. **Replacement.** The chain should use the disjunction "(T0) at some P_0, or (S) at some P_0". Each implies Y_t ≡ Y_0, by Theorem P and by Theorem P_S respectively, modulo the same orbifold input.
3. **Neither disjunct is proved for general V_0.** The natural dichotomy at a cusp P_0 is:
   - (I) some boundary branch of closure(V_0) at P_0 leaves Δ_Γ. This is the merge situation of (T0), PR-C and PR-F, and it still needs (PA) and the Teichmüller property at the merged vertex.
   - (II) all boundary branches at P_0 lie in Δ_Γ. This is (S1), and it still needs (S3) and (S2).

   (S3) can fail combinatorially. For example, if every Γ-splitting through every node has its i-vertex outside the u-parts with nodes, it fails; I have no example of a totally geodesic V_0 where this happens. Its failure would be a further pinning gap.
4. A hybrid of (T0) and (S) can be stated: at P_0, choose for every node e a branch Z_e (Krull), in whatever stratum it lies, and require that some branch provides a Teichmüller vertex whose limit contains, as a component, the i-component of another branch. This contains both Theorem P (one branch with a merge) and Theorem P_S. The proof is the same as Steps 3–5 above, with "∂V_t" replaced by the relevant 1-dimensional pieces and Lemma R* applied to each. I have not written out this hybrid in full.

## 5. What is proved, what is not, open questions

**Proved (modulo the classical results cited from memory: (AC), (TG), (DM), Royden, Riemann–Hurwitz, Sylow).**
- P1. V_0 = {y^5 = prod_{j=1}^5 (x-a_j)} ⊂ M_6 is a closed, 2-dimensional, totally geodesic subvariety, with the following properties:
  - ∂V_0 = closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_Γ (Γ = genus 2 — genus 4) is 1-dimensional;
  - ∂V_0 is constant equal to Y_0 = (y^5 = x(x-1), ∞) on the factor M_{2,1}, which has positive dimension;
  - every 1-dimensional boundary piece of closure(V_0) lies in Δ_Γ (Section 1).
- P2. For this V_0 the conclusion of MS5 fails for every candidate (Δ_G, P_0) (Proposition 2.1): G = Γ is forced, and (a) fails at the vertex i.
- P3. This V_0 is **not** smooth. It is topologically singular at y^5 = x^5 - x, and orbifold-singular at the Fermat quintic. It is smooth (orbifold sense) off a finite set, and smooth near its boundary (Section 1.3). So **P2 is not a counterexample to MS5 in the strict sense.**
- P4. Theorem P_S: under (R1), (R2) and hypothesis (S), Y_t ≡ Y_0.
- P5. The example satisfies (S1), (S3), and the isometric-lift part of (S2). Hence Corollary 3.4: the problem's claim holds for this V_0, under (R1), (R2) and the orbifold input ((O) plus the existence of the equivariance homomorphism ρ).

**Not proved / open.**
- Q1. **Is there a V_0 that is smooth everywhere and violates MS5?** Candidates must have no 1-dimensional boundary piece outside Δ_Γ through any cusp of ∂V_0, or else twins or bridges at every merge. I showed that among cyclic covering constructions from M_{0,5} of prime degree, the twin-at-every-cusp condition forces the exponent vector to be (1,1,1,1,1) mod 5 up to units. The coarse A_1 point at {0, ±1, ±i} is then unavoidable. Sketch of the computation, with d = p prime and the constant vertex over the bubble containing a_1, a_2:
  1. The twin condition at the three cusps of D_12 requires {k_3,k_4}, {k_3,k_5}, {k_4,k_5} each to equal w·{k_1,k_2} for some unit w.
  2. Normalize k_1 = 1, k_2 = a. If a ≠ 1, the three conditions force a^3 = 1 and k = (1, a, w, aw, a^2 w). The sum is then 1 + a + w(1 + a + a^2) ≡ 1 + a ≢ 0, so ∞ would be a sixth branch point. This is excluded.
  3. If a = 1, then k_3 = k_4 = k_5 = w. For π_i(∂V_0) to be a single point, D_13-type pieces must carry the same constant, which forces w = 1, and then p = 5.

  This sketch covers only the twin mechanism at prime degree. Non-cyclic covers, M_{1,2}-constructions and the exotic (gothic / flex) surfaces were not examined.
- Q2. **Does MS5 hold for smooth V_0?** Not settled. Any proof must use smoothness at interior points, which Section 2 makes implausible.
- Q3. **Is "(T0) or (S) or the hybrid of Section 4.4" always true for a totally geodesic V_0?** Not settled. This is now the real open input of the chain, together with (O) and (PA).
- Q4. The orbifold bookkeeping in (S2) for the example: the existence of ρ with values in Mod_{4,1}, and (O).

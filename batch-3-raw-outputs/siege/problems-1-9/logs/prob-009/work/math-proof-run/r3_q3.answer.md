## 3. A global tool: Hodge determinants of monodromy-invariant isotropic lattices

### 3.0 Notation
- Σ_g is a reference surface, H := H_1(Σ_g, Z) with the intersection form, and π : T_g → M_g the quotient by Mod_g.
- A point of T_g is (X, m) with m : Σ_g → X a marking.
- For v ∈ H ⊗ R and (X, m) ∈ T_g, let η_v be the real harmonic 1-form on X that is Poincaré dual to m_*v (∫_γ η_v = m_*v · γ for all cycles γ). The **Hodge norm** is N_v(X,m) := ∫_X η_v ∧ *η_v, which is > 0 for v ≠ 0.
- For a primitive isotropic sublattice I ⊂ H of rank r ≥ 1 with basis v_1, …, v_r, put G_I := (⟨η_{v_i}, η_{v_j}⟩) and d_I := det G_I > 0. Since the basis is changed by GL_r(Z), d_I does not depend on the basis.
- With γ ∈ Mod_g acting by γ·(X,m) = (X, m∘γ^{-1}), we get d_I(γ·x) = d_{γ^{-1}_* I}(x).

**Cited standard facts (from memory).**
- (HF1) Every primitive isotropic sublattice I of rank r is spanned by a_{g−r+1}, …, a_g for some symplectic basis (a_i, b_i) of H.
- (HF2) Let ω_j be the normalised holomorphic forms (∫_{a_i} ω_j = δ_ij), τ = (∫_{b_i} ω_j) and Y = Im τ > 0. Then ⟨η_{a_k}, η_{a_l}⟩ = (Y^{-1})_{kl}.
  - Check: η_{a_k} has zero a-periods and b-periods δ_{kj}. Writing η = Re(Σ c_j ω_j) gives c = −iY^{-1}e_k. Then ∫ η∧*η = (i/2)∫ω∧ω̄ = c^T Y c̄ = (Y^{-1})_{kk}.
  - Hence, for I as in (HF1), G_I = (Y^{-1})_{II}.
- (HF3) The period map T_g → 𝔥_g (Siegel space) is holomorphic.
- (HF4) **(continuity at the boundary).** Let R ∈ ∂M̄_g and let B_R ⊂ Def(R) be a small ball with plumbing coordinates s_f, f ∈ E_R. Mark a base fibre C_b, b ∈ B_R ∖ ∂. The nodes give disjoint vanishing curves f on C_b. Put V_R := span{[f]} (isotropic) and V_R^⊥ ⊃ V_R.
  - π_1(B_R ∖ ∂, b) is generated by the loops around {s_f = 0}. Their monodromies are the Dehn twists T_f, acting on H_1 by v ↦ v + (v·[f])[f] (Picard–Lefschetz). Hence a lattice I ⊂ V_R^⊥ is fixed pointwise by the local monodromy, and d_I is a well-defined function on B_R ∖ ∂.
  - The cited fact: **d_I extends continuously to B_R**, for I ⊂ V_R^⊥. At p ∈ B_R with vanishing span V_p ⊂ V_R, the value d_I(p) is the Gram determinant of the image of I in V_p^⊥/V_p ≅ H_1(normalisation of C_p), taken with the Hodge norm of the normalisation. So d_I(p) = 0 iff I ∩ V_p ≠ 0.
  - This follows from the classical expansion of the period matrix near a stable curve, τ(s) = Σ_f (log s_f / 2πi)·A_f + Ψ(s) with Ψ holomorphic on B_R and A_f the rank-one matrices of the vanishing classes (Fay; Masur 1976; Yamada). Equivalently it follows from Schmid's nilpotent-orbit norm estimates. I use it as stated and do not re-derive it.
- (HF5) **(only vanishing classes are short).** For R as in (HF4) there are a neighbourhood U ⊂ B_R of 0 and c > 0 such that: if x ∈ U ∖ ∂ and v ∈ H_1(C_x, Z) has N_v(x) < c, then v ∈ V_R. (V_R is well defined in H_1(C_x) because it is invariant under the local monodromy.) This is the uniform form of the norm estimates (weights −1 / 0 / +1 for W_{−1} = V_R, W_0 = V_R^⊥, the rest). Cited from memory, same sources.
- (HF6) Minkowski's second theorem: for a positive definite Gram matrix G of a rank-r lattice J, det G ≥ γ_r^{−r} λ_1(J)^{2r}, where λ_1(J) is the minimal norm (not squared) of a non-zero vector of J and γ_r is Hermite's constant.

### 3.1 Lemma H1 (plurisubharmonicity) — proved (from (HF1)–(HF3))
For every primitive isotropic I, the function u_I := log d_I is continuous and plurisubharmonic on T_g.

*Proof.*
- By (HF1) and (HF2), after a symplectic change of basis (which acts on 𝔥_g by a biholomorphism and does not change d_I) we have d_I = det((Y^{-1})_{II}), where I is the last r indices and J the first g − r.
- By the block-inverse formula, (Y^{-1})_{II} = S^{-1}, where S := Y_{II} − Y_{IJ} Y_{JJ}^{-1} Y_{JI} is the Schur complement. Hence u_I = −log det S.
- **S is matrix-concave in Y.** For w ∈ R^r, w^T S w = min_{y ∈ R^{g−r}} (y, w)^T Y (y, w), which is the minimum of a quadratic form in y with positive definite y-part. As a minimum of linear functions of Y, it is concave in Y. So S(λY_1 + (1−λ)Y_2) ⪰ λS(Y_1) + (1−λ)S(Y_2) in the Loewner order.
- log det is monotone for the Loewner order and concave on positive definite matrices. Hence log det S(λY_1 + (1−λ)Y_2) ≥ log det(λS_1 + (1−λ)S_2) ≥ λ log det S_1 + (1−λ) log det S_2. So Y ↦ log det S is concave, and **u_I is a convex function of Y** on the open convex cone of positive definite matrices.
- A continuous convex function F on an open convex set is the supremum of its affine minorants. For a holomorphic disc ζ ↦ τ(ζ) in 𝔥_g, ζ ↦ A(Im τ(ζ)) is harmonic for every affine A. So F(Im τ(ζ)) is a continuous supremum of harmonic functions, hence subharmonic. Therefore u_I is psh on 𝔥_g.
- Compose with the holomorphic period map (HF3). ∎

### 3.2 Lemma H3 (invariant isotropic lattices are orthogonal to vanishing cycles) — proved
Let T = ∏_{f∈E_R} T_f^{n_f} with all n_f ≥ 1. Let I be an isotropic sublattice of H_1(C_b) with T(I) ⊂ I. Then v·[f] = 0 for all v ∈ I and f ∈ E_R, i.e. I ⊂ V_R^⊥.

*Proof.* The curves f are disjoint, so the T_f commute and T v = v + Σ_f n_f (v·[f])[f]. Since Tv ∈ I and I is isotropic, 0 = v·Tv = v·v + Σ_f n_f (v·[f])(v·[f]) = Σ_f n_f (v·[f])². Since all n_f ≥ 1, v·[f] = 0 for every f. ∎

### 3.3 Proposition H (constancy and non-vanishing) — proved (modulo (HF4))
Let C ⊂ M̄_g be a compact irreducible curve with C ⊄ ∂M̄_g, and put C° := C ∩ M_g. Let C̃ be an irreducible component of π^{-1}(C°) ⊂ T_g, and Γ_C := Stab_{Mod_g}(C̃). Let I ⊂ H be a non-zero primitive isotropic sublattice with

  **(Mon)  γ_*(I) = I for every γ ∈ Γ_C.**

Then:
1. u_I is constant and finite on C̃.
2. For every point R ∈ C ∩ ∂M̄_g and every local branch of C at R, transport I along that branch into Def(R) (as in step 3 of the proof). Then I ⊂ V_R^⊥ and I ∩ V_R = 0. In words: **no non-zero class of I is a vanishing class at any point of C.**

*Proof.*
1. **The function on C^ν.**
   - The components of π^{-1}(C°) form one Mod_g-orbit. This is the orbit argument of round14_q5 Lemma 2, applied locally, since Mod_g acts properly discontinuously with π locally the quotient by the finite stabiliser.
   - Let C^ν be the normalisation of C and C^{ν°} := ν^{-1}(C°), a cofinite subset. Let C̃^ν be the normalisation of C̃. The induced map C̃^ν → C^{ν°} is surjective, and two points have the same image iff they differ by an element of Γ_C. Indeed, if branches at x and x′ = γx have the same image branch, then γC̃ and C̃ share a 1-dimensional germ, so γC̃ = C̃.
   - Near a point of C̃^ν, this map is the quotient by a finite cyclic group, i.e. w = z^m in local coordinates.
   - u_I restricted to C̃ is psh (Lemma H1), so u_I∘(normalisation) is subharmonic on C̃^ν. It is Γ_C-invariant, since d_I(γx) = d_{γ^{-1}_* I}(x) = d_I(x) by (Mon).
   - So U(ξ) := u_I(ξ̃), for any lift ξ̃, is a well-defined continuous function on C^{ν°}. It is subharmonic: at w = 0, the mean of U over |w| = r equals the mean of u_I over |z| = r^{1/m}, which is ≥ u_I(0) = U(0); elsewhere the map is locally biholomorphic. U is finite on C^{ν°}, since d_I > 0 on smooth curves (the Gram matrix of independent classes for a positive definite form).
2. **Boundary points.** Let ξ ∈ C^ν with R := ν(ξ) ∈ ∂M̄_g, and let β be the branch of C at R given by a small disc Δ_ξ.
   - Choose a lifted branch β̃ ⊂ B_R through 0, parametrised by its normalisation ζ ∈ D. Then β̃(D ∖ 0) ⊂ B_R ∖ ∂, because C ∩ ∂ is finite. The map D → Δ_ξ is ζ ↦ ζ^{m′} in suitable coordinates.
   - Mark a fibre over β̃(ζ_0) so that the marked curve lies on the branch of C̃ over β. Continuing the marking along D ∖ 0 lifts the universal cover of D ∖ 0 into π^{-1}(C°). Its image is an irreducible curve sharing a germ with C̃, hence lies in C̃. The deck transformation of the loop around 0 is therefore an element γ_β ∈ Γ_C.
   - Its action on homology is the monodromy of the plumbing family along ζ ↦ β̃(e^{iθ}ζ_0), i.e. ∏_f T_f^{n_f} with n_f := ord_0(s_f∘β̃).
   - n_f ≥ 1 for every f ∈ E_R: s_f(0) = 0 because f is a node of R, and s_f∘β̃ ≢ 0 because β̃ ∖ 0 is interior.
   - By (Mon), γ_β(I) = I, so Lemma H3 gives I ⊂ V_R^⊥.
   - By (HF4), d_I extends continuously to B_R. So U(ζ^{m′}) = log d_I(β̃(ζ)) → log d_I(0) ∈ [−∞, ∞) as ζ → 0, and U is bounded above near ξ.
   - A subharmonic function on a punctured disc that is bounded above extends subharmonically across the puncture (points are polar; cited). Set U(ξ) := log d_I(0).
3. **Maximum principle.** U is subharmonic, with values in [−∞, ∞), on the compact connected Riemann surface C^ν. It attains its maximum, so it is constant. The constant is finite because U is finite on C^{ν°}. This proves 1.
   - At boundary points, log d_I(0) equals this finite constant, so d_I(0) > 0. By (HF4) this means I ∩ V_R = 0. This proves 2. ∎

**Remarks.**
- Proposition H is a lattice version of "flat sections of a polarised VHS over a compact base have constant Hodge norm" (theorem of the fixed part). The proof above is self-contained, apart from (HF4).
- Proposition H is consistent with complete curves meeting Δ_0. For example, a general complete-intersection curve has large monodromy, so (Mon) fails for every I ≠ 0.

## 4. Application to the detaching mirror (NRT-Z): Corollary H4

**Setting.**
- Case II at a generic p_0 ∈ c̃, with (Sm), as in §1. Coordinates (z, σ, t) on X near (p_0, 0) as in Lemma LN, and D = {σ = 0}.
- Let U_0 be a small neighbourhood of (p_0, 0). For small t ≠ 0, D_t ∩ U_0 = {(z, 0, t) : |z − z_0| < ε} is a connected curve germ consisting of interior points (generically; case II). So q(D_t ∩ U_0) lies in a unique irreducible component Z_t of the compact curve 𝒟_t, and Z_t ⊄ ∂M̄_g.
- Near p_0, the Kuranishi family over Def(Q) is a deformation of the curve C_{p_0}, whose nodes are E_c. V_{p_0} := span{[e] : e ∈ E_c}, computed in H_1 of a smooth fibre over U_0 ∖ ∂. It is isotropic and fixed pointwise by the local monodromy (Picard–Lefschetz, since V_{p_0} ⊂ V_{p_0}^⊥).
- **(NS)** Some e ∈ E_c is non-separating. Then I := (V_{p_0} ⊗ Q) ∩ H_1 is a non-zero primitive isotropic lattice, well defined on every smooth fibre over U_0.
- **(Mon_t)** (Mon) of Proposition H holds for C = Z_t and the lattice I, transported from a point of D_t ∩ U_0 to a lift on the branch of Z_t through it.

**Corollary H4.** Assume case II, (Sm), (NS), and (Mon_t) for a sequence of t → 0 (or for all small t ≠ 0). Then:
- (a) d_I ≡ e^{f(t)} on Z_t, with f(t) finite;
- (b) Z_t contains no stable curve at which a non-zero class of (the transport of) I vanishes;
- (c) f(t) → −∞ as t → 0;
- (d) (uses (HF5), (HF6)) every point R_∞ ∈ M̄_g that is a limit of points x_n ∈ Z_{t_n} with t_n → 0 is a stable curve with a **non-separating node**. In particular the limit of the curves Z_t as t → 0 lies in ∂M̄_g, inside the closure of the locus of curves with a non-separating node. Among the components of 𝒟_0, the alternative "closure of a Teichmüller curve of V_0 ∩ Z_h" of round13_q4 Prop. Z(4) does not occur in that limit.

*Proof.*
- (a), (b): Proposition H.
- (c): Fix z_0 and take the points x(t) := (z_0, 0, t) ∈ D_t ∩ U_0. As t → 0 they tend to (p_0, 0). By (HF4) in the chart at the stable curve C_{p_0} (where I ⊂ V_{p_0} ⊂ V_{p_0}^⊥), d_I(x(t)) → d_I(p_0) = 0. This is because I ⊂ V_{p_0} maps to 0 in H_1 of the normalisation. So f(t) = log d_I(x(t)) → −∞.
- (d): Since Z_{t_n} ∩ ∂ is finite, we may replace x_n by smooth curves x′_n ∈ Z_{t_n} within distance 1/n of x_n.
  - Let J_n ⊂ H_1(C_{x′_n}) be the transport of I along Z_{t_n}. By (a) it is well defined, and det G_{J_n}(x′_n) = e^{f(t_n)} → 0.
  - By (HF6), some non-zero v_n ∈ J_n has N_{v_n}(x′_n) → 0.
  - For large n, x′_n lies in the neighbourhood U of (HF5) in a chart at R_∞. So v_n ∈ V_{R_∞}, and in particular V_{R_∞} ≠ 0.
  - A node has non-zero vanishing class iff it is non-separating. ∎

**What H4 does and does not give.**
1. It is a genuinely global (t ≠ 0, compactness) constraint, of the kind Lemma NG says is needed. It forces the following picture:
   - the mirror family is a level set of the I-Hodge determinant;
   - it never touches boundary points where an I-class pinches;
   - in the limit it collapses entirely into ∂_irr.

   Together with (d), the homological route of round13_q6 Prop. G / round14_q6 §4.1 is now needed only for cycles supported on boundary curves with a non-separating node.
2. **It does not exclude case II.** In the chart model of round13_q6 §3 (s_e = s_{e′} = σ² − t², so s_e ≡ −t² on D), d_I on D_t depends to leading order on t only, as (a) requires. (b) and (c) are also satisfied. No contradiction is visible at chart level, and I have no global one.
3. **(Mon_t) is not verified.**
   - **Sufficient condition (SKETCH, not proved).** Suppose that, for small t, Z_t stays in a neighbourhood of c̄ (i.e. lim Z_t = c̄). Suppose also that at every puncture Q′ of c, every a ∈ Aut(Q′) stabilising a lifted component of 𝒟 at (Q′, 0) preserves span{[e] : e ∈ E_c}.
     - Then a van Kampen decomposition of the smooth part of Z_t applies. The pieces are: a tube along c ∖ {punctures}, where the monodromy preserves the multicurve E_c because it is carried along the stratum; and small balls at the punctures, where the monodromy lies in ⟨T_f : f ∈ E_{Q′}⟩ · Stab(lifted component). This would give (Mon_t).
     - The second condition fails exactly for **pattern-moving** elements (aE_c ≠ E_c), the phenomenon behind (A3). So (Mon_t) carries the same difficulty as the rest of the problem. I could not verify it.
   - (NS) can fail: if every e ∈ E_c is separating, then V_{p_0} = 0 and Proposition H gives nothing. A replacement would use H_1 of the normalisation pieces, but separating pinches are invisible to Hodge norms. **GAP.**

**Status of (NRT-Z).** NOT PROVED. Reduced, under (Sm), (NS), (Mon_t), to excluding a compact family Z_t with the following properties:
- constant I-Hodge determinant e^{f(t)}, with f(t) → −∞;
- its limit lies in ∂_irr ∩ closure(V_0) and contains c̄. (The limit contains points of c near P, it is a pure 1-dimensional compact limit cycle, and c̄ is irreducible.)

## 6. Summary: proved / not proved / open

**Proved** (modulo the cited facts (HF1)–(HF6): symplectic bases, Riemann bilinear relations, holomorphy of the period map, the boundary expansion of the period matrix / Schmid norm estimates, Minkowski's second theorem; plus removable singularities for subharmonic functions):
- Lemma H1: log det of the Hodge Gram matrix of a primitive isotropic lattice is a convex function of Im τ, hence plurisubharmonic on T_g.
- Lemma H3: a lattice-preserving positive multitwist about disjoint curves forces the isotropic lattice to be orthogonal to those curves.
- Proposition H: on a compact irreducible curve C ⊄ ∂M̄_g whose monodromy (stabiliser of a component of its preimage in T_g) preserves a non-zero primitive isotropic lattice I, the I-Hodge determinant is constant and finite, and no non-zero class of I vanishes at any boundary point of C.
- Corollary H4, under case II, (Sm), (NS) and (Mon_t): (a)–(d) of §4. The mirror component Z_t of the detaching-mirror family 𝒟_t has constant I-determinant e^{f(t)} with f(t) → −∞. It avoids all I-pinching boundary points. Its limit lies in the closure of the locus of curves with a non-separating node, so it contains no Teichmüller curve of V_0.

**Sketched only:** Remark 2.1 (flat reading of (M1') under (TD)); the van Kampen sufficient condition for (Mon_t) in §4.

**Not proved:**
- (M1');
- (NRT-Z), and hence case (A1), even given (Sm);
- (M3) for non-central a;
- (A2), (A3), (B);
- therefore (NRT_P), and the problem's claim along this chain.

No counterexample was constructed.

**Open questions (named):**

2. (Mon_t) for the mirror component Z_t. By §4 it fails only through pattern-moving elements of Aut at the punctures that are realised by loops of Z_t.
3. Excluding the residual family of Corollary H4: a compact Z_t with constant I-determinant degenerating into ∂_irr ∩ closure(V_0) ⊃ c̄. Possibly via the homological route of round13_q6 Prop. G, now restricted to ∂_irr-curves.
4. (NS) failing (all pinched curves along c separating).
5. (M3) for non-central a; (A2)/(A3)/(B); (Rig).

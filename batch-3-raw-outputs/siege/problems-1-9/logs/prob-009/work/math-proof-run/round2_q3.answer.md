# round2_q3: what the constant factor's rigidity amounts to, and why it may not transport

## Interpretations
- "Smoothly trivial": there is a diffeomorphism 𝒱 ≅ V_0 × Δ that commutes with the projections to Δ.
- (i): for each stratum Δ_G, the set closure(V_t) ∩ Δ_G is the fibre over t of a smoothly trivial family.
- (ii): the compatibility of closures stated in the problem.

## Proved (relying on the context items, which I re-checked)
**P1.** The map t ↦ Y_t is holomorphic Δ → M_{g_i,n_i}. Proof: this is context item 1, via Remmert's proper mapping theorem applied to closure(𝒱) ∩ (Δ_Γ × Δ). One caveat: that closure must be analytic. This holds if closure(𝒱) in M̄_g × Δ is analytic, for instance if 𝒱 is algebraic over Δ. Remmert–Stein does not give it in general, so I take it as part of what "holomorphic family" means here.
Consequently the claim is equivalent to Y_t taking only countably many values.

**P2 (hypotheses (i) and (ii) alone cannot decide the claim).** Near ∂V_t, use coordinates (y, z, s): y on M_{g_i,n_i}, z on the other factors, s the plumbing parameters. Any germ y = Y_t + O(|s|) satisfies every constraint that (i) and (ii) impose at ∂V_t (context items 2–4, which I re-checked). The same reasoning applies at the deeper points {Y_t} × (closure(C_t) \ C_t). There, (ii) only records incidences between boundary curves. Moving Y_t holomorphically preserves those incidences, because factor i never degenerates along closure(∂V_t). So any proof has to use some global property of V_t that is preserved for t ≠ 0. The local boundary hypotheses do not supply one.

**P3 (t = 0, cited from memory).** By Wright's theorem, V_0 is the projection of a GL(2,R)-orbit closure. By the countability theorem of Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi, totally geodesic subvarieties form a countable set. Hence, if every V_t were totally geodesic, Y_t would be constant. Heuristically (via Chen–Wright WYSIWYG and BCGGM multi-scale differentials), along ∂V_0 the component Y_0 sits at a lower level, so its top-level differential is zero. I have not proved that Y_0 is rigid in that setting, and I do not claim it.

## The missing lemma (open)
**Lemma (needed).** There is a property P of a point Y ∈ M_{g_i,n_i} with the following three features:
1. P is determined by data that a smoothly trivial family satisfying (i) and (ii) preserves. Examples: the conjugacy class of the monodromy of the universal curve around ∂V_t and around the incidence points, or the intersection numbers of boundary curves on closure(V_t).
2. Only countably many Y satisfy P.
3. Y_0 satisfies P.

Candidates I considered:
- **Monodromy.** Monodromy around ∂V_t is generated by Dehn twists about the nodes of Γ. These twists act trivially on the subsurface carrying Y_t, so they impose no condition on Y_t. A finite-order monodromy that fixes Y_t would come only from orbifold points, and generic V_0 does not have them.
- **Intersection numbers.** Self-intersection numbers of boundary curves involve the degrees of ψ-classes restricted to ∂V_t. Along {Y_t} × C_t these depend only on C_t and on the normal-bundle data, not on Y_t. So they are invariant under moving Y_t and cannot pin it down.

Both candidates therefore fail for the same reason as P2: the dependence on Y_t is trivial. This is strong evidence that the claim does not follow from (i), (ii) and smooth triviality without a further assumption, such as V_t being algebraic and totally geodesic, or some rigidity of the family. I could not construct a counterexample either: that would need a genuine holomorphic deformation of V_0 in M_g with a moving constant factor, and I have no construction of such a deformation.

## Status
- **Proved:** P1 (conditional on closure(𝒱) being analytic) and P2.
- **Not proved:** the claim itself.
- **Open:** the lemma above, and whether the claim is true under the stated hypotheses.

## 0. Status (read this first)

- **No complete proof and no counterexample.** MS4 is **not** settled here. What is below is a **partial result**: it is a conditional theorem, and the condition is a property (T0) of the totally geodesic surface V_0 alone.
- **New and proved, given cited classical theorems (§2): Lemma R.** Take a boundary curve E_0 of closure(V_0) whose projection to some vertex factor is a Teichmüller curve, and let E_t be its transport by (R2). Then the projection of E_t is the same curve for all t.
  - This holds even though the complex structure of E_t is allowed to vary.
  - The proof uses only Royden's theorem, Bers' theorem on translation lengths of pseudo-Anosov maps, Thurston's theorem on minimal stretch maps, and the Imayoshi–Shiga rigidity theorem.
  - It replaces PR-3 Proposition D, which needed Hodge theory plus McMullen's (K) and the countability statement (Ct). The hypothesis (H2′) of PR-3 is no longer needed: being a Teichmüller curve is enough.
- **Proved (§3): Theorem P.** (R1) + (R2) + (T0) imply MS4. Here (T0) is either
  - (T0-a): a merging boundary branch whose merged-vertex projection is a Teichmüller curve, or
  - (T0-b): two transverse Teichmüller-curve projections through Y_0 at vertex i.
- **Remark (§4).** MS4 contains a non-trivial rigidity statement. Example: inside the image W of a covering construction M_{0,6} → M_g, the fibres V_c of the forgetful map M_{0,6} → M_{0,4} form a family that satisfies every hypothesis of the problem except total geodesy, and the constant factor *does* move with c. So MS4 implies that no such fibre is totally geodesic. Any proof must therefore use total geodesy of V_0 in an essential, global way.
- **Open (§5):** (T0) for an arbitrary totally geodesic surface V_0.

## 1. Conventions

- **Level covers.** Fix ℓ ≥ 3. For each factor M_{h,m}, the level-ℓ cover M_{h,m}[ℓ] is a complex manifold. It is the quotient of T_{h,m} by a torsion-free finite-index subgroup Mod[ℓ] of the mapping class group, and the map M_{h,m}[ℓ] → M_{h,m} is finite.
- **Metric normalization.** On T_{h,m}, d_T is the Teichmüller metric, normalized so that d_T = ½ log K. **[cited] Royden's theorem**, in its extension to finite-type surfaces by Earle–Kra / Gardiner, says that d_T equals the Kobayashi distance of T_{h,m}. With this normalization, a Teichmüller disc is an isometric embedding of the unit disc 𝔻 with the Poincaré metric of curvature −4, which is also the Kobayashi metric of 𝔻.
- **Teichmüller curves.** A *Teichmüller curve* in M_{h,m} means the following. There is a nonconstant holomorphic map f : S → M_{h,m}, where S = 𝔻/Γ_S is a finite-type Riemann surface. f lifts to an isometric holomorphic embedding f̃ : 𝔻 → T_{h,m} whose image is a Teichmüller disc 𝔻_q generated by a quadratic differential q (abelian or not). It is equivariant for a homomorphism ρ : Γ_S → Mod_{h,m}, and ρ(Γ_S) lies in the stabilizer of 𝔻_q, which is the Veech group. Finite covers of such maps are allowed.
- **Normalizations of curves.** A 1-dimensional analytic set E is a union of irreducible components, and its normalization Ẽ is a disjoint union of Riemann surfaces.
  - Topologically, Ẽ is obtained by separating the local branches at each singular point. These are the connected components of the punctured link neighbourhoods.
  - Hence a homeomorphism E → E′ of 1-dimensional analytic sets lifts to a homeomorphism Ẽ → Ẽ′. It maps irreducible components to irreducible components, since these correspond to connected components of the normalization.
  - If E is a quasi-projective curve, every component of Ẽ is a compact Riemann surface minus finitely many points.

## 2. Lemma R (rigidity of Teichmüller-curve projections of transported boundary curves)

**Setting.**
- H : closure(V_0) × Δ → closure(𝒱) is the stratified homeomorphism of (R2), and H_t = H(·, t).
- Δ_G is a non-quotient stratum, Δ_G ≅ ∏_{w} M_{g_w,n_w}, and v is a vertex of G with factor M_{h,m}.
- E_0 is an irreducible component of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_G of dimension 1, and E_t := H_t(E_0).
  - By (R2), E_t ⊂ Δ_G.
  - E_t is a closed analytic subset of Δ_G, because closure(V_t) is closed and analytic by (R1).
  - E_t is irreducible, by §1.
  - E_t is algebraic: closure(V_t) is a closed analytic subset of the projective variety M̄_g, hence algebraic by **[cited] Chow's theorem**, and Δ_G is locally closed and algebraic.
- Write f_t := π_v|_{E_t}, and F_t := π_v(E_t) ⊂ M_{h,m}. F_t is constructible, because π_v : Δ_G → M_{h,m} is an algebraic morphism.

**Lemma R.** Assume the composite Ẽ_0 → E_0 → M_{h,m} is (a finite cover of) a Teichmüller curve, in the sense of §1. Then F_t = F_0 for all t ∈ Δ, and also closure(F_t) = closure(F_0) in M̄_{h,m}.

*Proof.*

**Step 0 (level structure and marking).**
- Pull back the level cover along π_v. Choose a component E_0′ of the preimage of Ẽ_0, a finite unramified cover, such that f_0 lifts to f_0′ : E_0′ → M_{h,m}[ℓ].
- The homeomorphisms h_t := (H_t)~ : Ẽ_0 → Ẽ_t lift to E_0′ → E_t′, where E_t′ is the corresponding cover of Ẽ_t. The cover is defined by the same subgroup of π_1, transported by h_t; see the next bullet.
- The map (x, t) ↦ π_v(H(x, t)) is continuous. So for every t, f_t ∘ h_t is homotopic to f_0, through f_s ∘ h_s, s ∈ [0, t]. Hence the lifts to M_{h,m}[ℓ] can be chosen continuously in t, and f_t′ ∘ h_t is homotopic to f_0′.
- Since M_{h,m}[ℓ] = T_{h,m}/Mod[ℓ] with Mod[ℓ] acting freely, f_t′ lifts to a holomorphic map f̃_t : 𝔻 → T_{h,m}. It is equivariant for ρ_t := ρ_0 ∘ (h_t)_*^{-1} : π_1(E_t′) → Mod[ℓ], up to conjugation.
- From now on I drop the primes and write E_t for E_t′.
- E_0 = 𝔻/Γ_0, where Γ_0 is torsion free (it is a finite-index subgroup of a Fuchsian group, mapping injectively to Mod[ℓ] because f̃_0 is an embedding equivariant under ρ_0 and the action is free).
- Since π_1(E_0) is non-abelian, E_0 is hyperbolic of finite type, i.e. it has finite area. E_t is homeomorphic to E_0 via h_t, so it is also a hyperbolic finite-type surface. Give both their complete hyperbolic metrics of curvature −4, which are their Kobayashi metrics.

**Step 1 (equality of lengths at t = 0).**
- Let γ be a non-peripheral simple closed curve on E_0. In the torsion-free lattice Γ_0, γ is hyperbolic: in a finite-area hyperbolic surface the parabolic elements are exactly the peripheral classes.
- Its image ρ_0(γ) acts on the Teichmüller disc 𝔻_q as a hyperbolic isometry, with translation length ℓ_{E_0}(γ) along the image A of the hyperbolic axis. A is a Teichmüller geodesic.
- **[cited] (Veech; Thurston.)** An element of the Veech group acting hyperbolically on 𝔻_q is an affine pseudo-Anosov map. Its stretch factor λ is the larger eigenvalue (in absolute value) of its derivative, and its translation along the invariant Teichmüller geodesic A is log λ.
- **[cited] (Bers, "An extremal problem for quasiconformal mappings and a theorem by Thurston", Acta Math. 1978.)** For a pseudo-Anosov φ with stretch factor λ, inf_{x ∈ T} d_T(x, φx) = log λ, attained exactly on its axis.
- Hence τ_T(ρ_0(γ)) := inf_x d_T(x, ρ_0(γ)x) = ℓ_{E_0}(γ).

**Step 2 (inequality of lengths at time t).**
- f̃_t is holomorphic, so it does not increase Kobayashi distances. By Royden's theorem, d_T(f̃_t(x), f̃_t(y)) ≤ k_𝔻(x, y).
- Take x on the axis of γ_t := (h_t)_*γ in 𝔻. Then
  τ_T(ρ_0(γ)) = τ_T(ρ_t(γ_t)) ≤ d_T(f̃_t x, ρ_t(γ_t) f̃_t x) = d_T(f̃_t x, f̃_t(γ_t x)) ≤ k_𝔻(x, γ_t x) = ℓ_{E_t}(γ_t).
  The first equality holds because translation length is a conjugacy invariant.
- With Step 1: ℓ_{E_t}(h_t(γ)) ≥ ℓ_{E_0}(γ) for every non-peripheral simple closed curve γ of E_0.

**Step 3 (Thurston).**
- **[cited] (Thurston, "Minimal stretch maps between hyperbolic surfaces", 1986 preprint, arXiv math/9801039; Thm 3.1 / Prop. 3.5 there, as I recall it).** Let S be a surface of finite type, and let X, Y be marked complete finite-area hyperbolic structures on S. Then sup_γ ℓ_Y(γ)/ℓ_X(γ) ≥ 1 (sup over simple closed non-peripheral γ), with equality iff X = Y in T(S).
- Apply this with X = (E_t, marked by h_t) and Y = E_0. Step 2 gives sup_γ ℓ_{E_0}(γ)/ℓ_{E_t}(γ) ≤ 1. So equality holds, and X = Y in Teichmüller space.
- That is, there is a biholomorphism φ_t : E_0 → E_t isotopic to h_t.
- (The scaling of the metric is the same on both sides, so it does not matter. Caveat: I am citing Thurston's theorem in the finite-area, cusped case from memory. I believe it is stated there for finite-area surfaces, and it is also proved in that generality in Papadopoulos–Théret's survey; if one only trusts the closed case, this step is a gap.)

**Step 4 (Imayoshi–Shiga).**
- g_t := f_t ∘ φ_t : E_0 → M_{h,m}[ℓ] is holomorphic and homotopic to f_0.
- It is non-constant. Otherwise its monodromy would be trivial, but the monodromy is conjugate to ρ_0, which is injective on the non-trivial group Γ_0.
- **[cited] Rigidity theorem of Imayoshi–Shiga (1988)**, in the map form recorded in PR-4. Its hypotheses: S is of finite type, and the target is a fine moduli space (a level cover), so families correspond to maps. Its conclusion: a homotopy class of maps S → M_{h,m}[ℓ] contains only finitely many nonconstant holomorphic maps, say g^1, …, g^N.
- So g_t ∈ {g^1, …, g^N} for every t, and F_t = f_t(E_t) = g_t(E_0) is the image in M_{h,m} of one of these maps. (I am now back on the original curves E_t, without primes; images are unchanged by finite covers.)

**Step 5 (constancy by connectedness).**
- Let A_1, …, A_M be the distinct sets among the closures in M_{h,m} of the images of g^1, …, g^N, projected to M_{h,m}. Each closure(F_t) is one of the A_j.
- Each A_j is an irreducible closed algebraic curve. Indeed, closure(F_t) is the closure of the image of the irreducible algebraic curve E_t under an algebraic morphism.
- Put T_j := {t : closure(F_t) = A_j}. These sets are disjoint and cover Δ.
- **Claim: each T_j is closed.** Let t_n → t with t_n ∈ T_j. For x ∈ E_0, the point π_v(H(x, t_n)) lies in A_j and converges to π_v(H(x, t)), because H(x, t) ∈ Δ_G and π_v is continuous on Δ_G. A_j is closed in M_{h,m}, so π_v(H(x, t)) ∈ A_j. Hence F_t ⊂ A_j and closure(F_t) ⊂ A_j. Both are irreducible 1-dimensional closed analytic sets, so closure(F_t) = A_j.
- Δ is connected and is a finite disjoint union of the closed sets T_j. Hence a single T_j equals Δ, and closure(F_t) = closure(F_0) for all t.
- The equality F_t = F_0 needs one more remark, since F_t could a priori be closure(F_0) minus a finite set. The cleanest statement, and the one I use, is closure(F_t) = closure(F_0). This holds in M_{h,m}, and then also in M̄_{h,m} by taking closures. ∎

**Remarks on Lemma R.**
- No Hodge theory is used. Neither Möller's characterization, nor McMullen's isometric-disc theorem (K), nor countability (Ct) is needed: Thurston's theorem replaces the "Toledo invariant plus (K)" step of PR-3.
- The hypothesis is only that the t = 0 projection is a Teichmüller curve, for an abelian or a quadratic differential.

## 3. Theorem P (MS4 under (T0))

**Hypothesis (T0-a).** There exist:
- a point P_0 ∈ closure(∂V_0) \ ∂V_0, lying in a stratum Δ_{G′};
- a 1-dimensional local branch b of closure(V_0) at P_0 with the merge property of PR-3 §C. That is, along b \ {P_0} the curve has dual graph G_b, where G_b is G′ with a set of edges contracted that includes at least one edge at vertex i, and v is the vertex of G_b containing i;

such that Δ_{G_b} is a non-quotient stratum, and the projection π_v of the irreducible component E_0 of closure(V_0) ∩ Δ_{G_b} containing b \ {P_0} is a Teichmüller curve F_0 ⊂ M_{h,m}, in the sense of §1.

**Theorem P.** Assume (R1), (R2) and (T0-a). Then Y_t = Y_0 for all t ∈ Δ.

*Proof.*

1. **Transport.** Put E_t := H_t(E_0), P_t := H(P_0, t) and b_t := H_t(b).
   - By PR-3 Lemma B′: E_t ⊂ Δ_{G_b}, P_t ∈ closure(E_t) ∩ closure(∂V_t) ∩ Δ_{G′}, and t ↦ P_t is continuous.
   - b_t is a branch at P_t with b_t \ {P_t} ⊂ E_t.
   - The vertex-i component of P_t is Y_t, by P-b Lemma 1 (closure of {Y_t} × C_t).

2. **Local sheets.** Work in an orbifold chart of M̄_g near P_0 with plumbing coordinates s_e, e ∈ E(G′). For small t the chart also contains P_t.
   - Locally, Δ_{G_b} is a disjoint union of the sets {s_e = 0 for e ∈ S, s_e ≠ 0 for e ∉ S} (away from deeper strata), one for each edge set S ⊂ E(G′) whose contraction gives G_b.
   - The punctured branch b_t \ {P_t} is connected, so it lies in one of them, S_t. By continuity of H, S_t is locally constant in t, hence equal to S_0.
   - On the closure of this sheet, the factor of the merged vertex v extends continuously to a map into M̄_{h,m}: the sheet is locally a product of neighbourhoods in the factors M̄_{g_w,n_w}, and v corresponds to the sub-graph K of G′ spanned by the contracted edges at v.
   - Hence Q_t := lim π_v along b_t at P_t is well defined. It is the stable curve with dual graph K formed by the components of P_t at the vertices of K. So it is continuous in t, and its vertex-i component is Y_t.
   - Q_t ∈ ∂M̄_{h,m}, because K has an edge. This is PR-3 Lemma C2.

3. **Rigidity.** By Lemma R, closure(F_t) = closure(F_0) in M̄_{h,m}. Since Q_t ∈ closure(F_t), we get Q_t ∈ closure(F_0) ∩ ∂M̄_{h,m}.

4. **Finiteness.** closure(F_0) is a compact irreducible curve not contained in ∂M̄_{h,m}. So its intersection with ∂M̄_{h,m} is a proper analytic subset, hence finite.

5. **Constancy of Q_t.** t ↦ Q_t is continuous from the connected set Δ into a finite set, hence constant: Q_t = Q_0.

6. **Constancy of Y_t.** Y_t is a component of the fixed stable curve Q_0, with its special points, viewed as a point of M_{g_i,n_i} after ordering the special points. So it lies in a finite set, by the finitely many choices of component and ordering. It is continuous in t (PR-1), hence constant: Y_t = Y_0 for all t. ∎

**Hypothesis (T0-b), the non-merging variant (PR-3 Lemma F with Lemma R in place of Prop. D).** There are two cusps P_0, P_0′ of ∂V_0, possibly equal, and branches b, b′ through them without the merge property, contained in components E_0, E_0′ of strata Δ_G, Δ_{G″} (non-quotient). The projections F_0 = π_i(E_0) and F_0′ = π_i(E_0′) to the vertex-i factor are Teichmüller curves, and closure(F_0) and closure(F_0′) have no common component through Y_0.

**Corollary P′.** Assume (R1), (R2) and (T0-b). Then Y_t = Y_0 for all t.

*Proof.*
1. The limits of π_i along b_t and b_t′ at P_t and P_t′ equal Y_t, because the vertex-i component of P_t and of P_t′ is Y_t, as in step 1 above.
2. So Y_t ∈ closure(F_t) ∩ closure(F_t′) = closure(F_0) ∩ closure(F_0′), by Lemma R twice.
3. Near Y_0 this intersection is a finite set. Y_t is continuous, so Y_t is constant for t near 0. By PR-1 and the identity theorem, Y_t is constant on all of Δ. ∎

## 4. A test family: MS4 is a genuine rigidity statement

**Construction.**
- Fix a cyclic covering datum: degree d, and monodromies a_1, …, a_6 ∈ ℤ/d, all non-zero, with Σ a_k = 0, such that the cover has genus g ≥ 2.
- Let c : M_{0,6} → M_g send (P^1; x_1, …, x_6) to the cyclic cover branched over the x_k with this monodromy.
- **[cited]** Covering constructions lift to holomorphic isometric embeddings T_{0,6} → T_g. So a subvariety of M_{0,6} is totally geodesic iff its image is, at least where c is an immersion.
- For λ ∈ M_{0,4}, let V^λ ⊂ M_{0,6} be the fibre of the forgetful map (x_1, …, x_6) ↦ (x_1, …, x_4) over λ. So V^λ = {(x_5, x_6) ∈ X_λ² minus the diagonals and the 4 points}, where X_λ = (P^1; 0, 1, ∞, λ).

**What happens at the boundary.**
- In M̄_{0,6}, consider the boundary stratum where x_5 and x_6 collide with x_1. A generic point of closure(V^λ) there is a nodal curve A ∪ B with A = (x_2, x_3, x_4, node) and B = (x_1, x_5, x_6, node). The cross-ratio of A equals λ. So closure(V^λ) meets this stratum ≅ M_{0,4} × M_{0,4} in {λ} × M_{0,4}: a curve constant on the first factor, with constant value λ.
- In M̄_g the corresponding boundary piece of c(V^λ) consists of covers. The part of the stable curve lying over A is a fixed cyclic cover Ã_λ of X_λ, branched over its four special points with monodromy (a_2, a_3, a_4, −a_2−a_3−a_4). It is constant along the piece, and it varies with λ.

**Conclusion.**
- Taking V_t := c(V^{λ(t)}) gives a holomorphic family of surfaces in M_g with a 1-dimensional boundary piece constant on a factor, whose constant value moves.
- Granting that the fibration is smoothly trivial with its boundary strata, which is the expected situation for a fibration of a smooth 3-fold restricted to generic boundary strata (I have not checked every stratum, nor that the relevant M̄_g-stratum is a product stratum for a given covering datum), the hypotheses (i) and (ii) hold for this family. The only hypothesis that fails is that V_0 is totally geodesic.
- So, read as a statement, **MS4 implies that no fibre V^λ (with any covering datum for which the hypotheses hold) is totally geodesic.** I could not decide this independently. It is consistent with what I remember of Eskin–McMullen–Mukamel–Wright: the only primitive totally geodesic surfaces known are the flex and gothic loci, and these are not forgetful fibres.
- This, together with PR-6, explains why every argument that is local near ∂V_t fails. A proof must use the total geodesy of V_0 globally. In Theorem P this enters through (T0).

## 5. What is proved and what is open

**Proved, given the cited classical theorems:**
- Lemma R. Its inputs are Royden, Bers, Veech affine pA, Thurston's minimal stretch theorem in the finite-area case, Imayoshi–Shiga, and Chow.
- Theorem P and Corollary P′: MS4 holds whenever V_0 satisfies (T0-a) or (T0-b).

**Partial: these are not a proof of MS4.** They cover only surfaces V_0 with (T0).

**Open:**
1. **(T0) for an arbitrary totally geodesic surface V_0.** Does every Γ-boundary curve ∂V_0 = {Y_0} × C_0 have a cusp with a merging branch whose merged projection is a Teichmüller curve? Or else two transverse Teichmüller-curve projections through Y_0?
   - Heuristically, boundary pieces of totally geodesic surfaces are complex geodesics for the sup-metric on the product strata, which would make some factor projection a Teichmüller curve. The theory of boundaries of orbit closures (Mirzakhani–Wright, Chen–Wright) would locate the relevant vertices.
   - I could not make this rigorous. Minsky's product-region theorem only gives the product structure up to an additive error, and that is not enough for exact isometry.
   - Moreover, components with identically zero limiting differential are invisible to the WYSIWYG compactification, and Y_0 may be such a component.
2. **Whether totally geodesic surfaces are rigid as compact subvarieties of M̄_g.** For example: is H^0 of the normal sheaf of closure(V_0) zero? By PR-5 this would settle MS4. A two-dimensional analogue of Lemma R would require a "Thurston theorem" for Kobayashi length spectra of quasi-projective surfaces, which I do not know to exist.
3. **Whether some fibre V^λ as in §4 (or any totally geodesic surface) admits a stratum-preserving deformation moving Y_0.** That would give a counterexample. None is known to me.

# round14_q5: answer

## Status in brief
- (SC) is **not proved** in general, and I found **no counterexample**.
- **The invariance-principle route does not apply to (SC) as posed.** Every invariance principle I know needs an invariant fibration (a centre foliation, or a skew-product structure) and continuous s/u holonomies between its fibres. A general smooth flow has neither. Even where the principle does apply, its output (holonomy-invariant conditional measures on the centre fibres) does not decide the closing condition (E). Section 2 makes this precise with explicit models, where the conclusion of the invariance principle holds trivially while closure is governed by one real equation that the principle says nothing about.
- **Proved sub-case (Theorem H, Section 3).** If mu is hyperbolic (no zero exponent other than the flow direction), (SC) holds with finite chains (two legs, then constant). This rests on cited horseshoe theorems. Under GH this case is already excluded by Theorem 5.1 of R6-a, so Theorem H is only a consistency check: it adds nothing to the chain.
- **Proved sub-case (Lemma S, Section 2.4).** In a skew product with continuous su-holonomies whose measure mu sits on an su-invariant invariant section (the "atomic" branch of the planner's dichotomy), chains close automatically. So that branch reduces to the base.
- **Open:** (SC) in general. The precise missing ingredient is stated in Section 4 as (PHC).

## 1. Which invariance principle, and whether it applies

### 1.1 Statements (all cited from memory; hypotheses as I recall them)
- **[Led] Ledrappier (1986), "Positivity of the exponent for stationary sequences of matrices", LNM 1186.**
  - Setting: a measurable linear cocycle A over an invertible system (f, mu), and a sigma-algebra B with f^{-1}B ⊂ B such that A is B-measurable.
  - Conclusion: if the extremal exponents coincide (lambda_- = lambda_+), then every A-invariant probability on the projectivised bundle that projects to mu has a B-measurable disintegration.
  - Taking B to be the sigma-algebra generated by local unstable sets turns this into u-invariance of the disintegration.
- **[AV] Avila–Viana (2010), "Extremal Lyapunov exponents: an invariance principle and applications", Invent. Math.**
  - Setting: a smooth cocycle F(x, xi) = (f(x), F_x(xi)) on a fibre bundle with compact fibres N, over a hyperbolic homeomorphism f (uniformly hyperbolic, with local product structure), and an F-invariant probability m projecting to mu.
  - Assumption: F admits stable and unstable holonomies. These are continuous (Hölder) families of homeomorphisms h^s_{x,y}: N_x -> N_y for y in W^s(x) (and likewise for u), e.g. from fibre bunching or partial hyperbolicity.
  - Conclusion: if lambda_-(F, m) >= 0, m has an s-invariant disintegration; if lambda_+(F, m) <= 0, a u-invariant one. If mu has local product structure and both hold, there is an su-invariant disintegration that depends continuously on the base point on supp mu.
- **[ASV] Avila–Santamaria–Viana (2013), Astérisque 358.**
  - Versions for linear cocycles and smooth cocycles over partially hyperbolic bases, with s/u holonomies.
  - Also an "invariance principle" for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms whose centre foliation forms a fibre bundle with compact fibres.
- **[TY] Tahzibi–Yang (2019), "Invariance principle and rigidity of high entropy measures", Trans. AMS.**
  - A version phrased with partial entropies instead of exponents, for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with a compact centre fibration (e.g. circle fibres).
  - I do not remember its exact hypotheses. I do not use it.
- **Non-uniform versions.** These replace the uniformly hyperbolic base by a non-uniformly hyperbolic one with Pesin blocks, but still require the fibre bundle structure and holonomies on Pesin blocks. I recall such statements for linear cocycles (Viana 2008, Annals, "Almost all cocycles over any hyperbolic system have nonvanishing Lyapunov exponents", stated for Hölder cocycles over hyperbolic bases). I cannot cite a precise non-uniform version for smooth (non-linear) cocycles.

**Common structural hypothesis.** All of these need an invariant fibration (a skew product, or a centre foliation with compact leaves forming a fibre bundle) and s/u holonomies mapping fibres to fibres. The conclusion is about conditional measures of an invariant measure on those fibres.

### 1.2 The three candidate substitutes in (SC), and why each fails
(SC) concerns an arbitrary smooth flow and an arbitrary ergodic mu with h_mu > 0 and int J_1 dmu = 0. There is no dominated splitting, no centre foliation, and no fibration.

**(i) The projective cocycle Dg_1 on the measurable zero-exponent bundle E^c_0.**
- Something like [Led] can be set up. For the derivative cocycle on E^{cs}/E^s along Pesin stable manifolds, linear holonomies can plausibly be defined on Pesin blocks: the centre growth is e^{o(n)} while stable distances contract like e^{-chi n}, so the products telescope. I have not checked the regularity of E^{cs} along W^s that this needs.
- **Even granting it, the conclusion is about tangent directions, and condition (E) is about positions.**
  - (E) asks whether the endpoint of an su-chain lies *exactly* on the weak-stable set of x_0.
  - This is a statement about the nonlinear position holonomy in the centre direction. The projectivised derivative cocycle does not see it.
  - Model 2.1 below makes this explicit: there the derivative cocycle on E^c is the identity, so every projective invariance statement holds trivially and carries no information. Yet closure is governed by a real equation (Theta) that can hold or fail.
- **Verdict: dead end for (SC).**

**(ii) Pesin centre plaques / fake foliations.**
- Burns–Wilkinson fake foliations need a *continuous* dominated splitting E^s ⊕ E^c ⊕ E^u on an open set. Here only a measurable Oseledets splitting is available.
- Non-uniform centre manifolds (Pesin–Kelley type) exist locally, but they are not unique, not invariant, and do not form a lamination. So there are no centre fibres and no holonomies between them for [AV] to act on.
- **Verdict: no substitute available.**

**(iii) A measurable centre-unstable / centre-stable lamination.**
- W^{cs}(x) := {y : limsup (1/t) log d(g_t x, g_t y) <= 0} is defined, but in general it is neither a manifold nor a lamination.
- With zero exponents, the "slow" sets are not controlled by Pesin theory.
- **Verdict: no substitute available.**

**Conclusion of Section 1 (a dead end, with reason).** No version of the invariance principle that I know applies to (SC) in the generality stated. Section 2 shows that applying it inside the class where it does apply (skew products) still does not give (SC). The planner's step (a) does not follow from holonomy invariance alone.

## 2. Models: what the invariance principle gives and why it does not decide (E)

### 2.1 Model (rotation skew products over a hyperbolic base)
**Setting.**
- Let h_t be a smooth flow on a compact manifold N, generated by Y, with an ergodic hyperbolic measure mu_N of positive entropy. Example: a volume-preserving Anosov flow, with mu_N its smooth volume.
- Let phi ∈ C^∞(N). Put M = N × T^1 and X = Y + phi(y) d/dtheta.
- Then g_t(y, theta) = (h_t y, theta + Phi_t(y)), where Phi_t(y) = int_0^t phi(h_s y) ds.

**Basic properties.**
- **Derivative cocycle on the centre.** Dg_t(d/dtheta) = d/dtheta. So E^c = R d/dtheta is invariant and Dg_t restricted to E^c is the identity. Every invariant measure has a zero centre exponent, and the projectivised centre cocycle is trivial.
- **Divergence.** With nu = nu_N × dtheta, div_nu X = div_{nu_N} Y, because d phi / d theta = 0. In the volume-preserving example, int J_1 dm = 0 for every invariant m.
- **The measure.** m = mu_N × Leb is invariant. Its g_1-ergodic components project to mu_N because mu_N is h_1-ergodic.
- **Entropy.** By Abramov–Rokhlin (cited), fibre entropy vanishes for isometric fibres. So every such component has entropy h_{mu_N}(h_1) > 0.
- **Invariance principle.** Here [AV] holds trivially: the fibre maps are rotations, and the conditionals of m (Lebesgue) are invariant under all rotations.

**Strong stable and unstable sets.** Assume the base is Anosov, so that for the base, exponential forward asymptoticity means strong stable.
- For y' ∈ W^{ss}_N(y), put beta^s(y, y') := int_0^∞ (phi(h_s y) - phi(h_s y')) ds. This converges exponentially.
- W^{ss}_M(y, theta) = {(y', theta + beta^s(y, y')) : y' ∈ W^{ss}_N(y)}.
- *Check.* The difference of the theta-coordinates of g_t(y, theta) and g_t(y', theta') is theta' - theta + Phi_t(y') - Phi_t(y). This tends to 0 exponentially iff theta' = theta + beta^s(y, y'), and the error is int_t^∞ (...) ds = O(e^{-ct}).
- Similarly W^{uu}_M(y, theta) = {(y', theta - beta^u(y, y'))}, with beta^u(y, y') := int_{-∞}^0 (phi(h_s y) - phi(h_s y')) ds.

**The closing condition.** A chain in M projects to a chain in N, and each leg shifts theta by the corresponding beta. Let x_0 = (y_0, theta_0) and let p = (p_N, theta_p) be the limit point. Then (E) holds with shift tau iff both of the following hold:
- p_N ∈ W^{ss}_N(h_tau y_0);
- (Theta): Hol(chain) ≡ Phi_tau(y_0) + beta^s(h_tau y_0, p_N) (mod 1), where Hol(chain) is the sum of the leg shifts.

**What the invariance principle contributes.** Exactly one real condition mod 1, (Theta), is added to the base closing problem. The invariance principle (Lebesgue conditionals are su-invariant) holds for every phi, so it cannot distinguish phi's for which (Theta) is solvable from phi's for which it is not.

### 2.2 The planner's dichotomy in this model
The su-loop holonomies form a group of rotations of T^1.
- **Finite group.** The planner's "some power of an su-loop returns exactly" is not well defined.
  - An su-loop at x_0 ends at p ∈ W^{ss}(g_tau x_0), not at x_0.
  - "Iterating" the loop means starting a new chain at p. That is a different loop based at a different point, with a different holonomy.
  - So finiteness of a holonomy group (defined, say, at the fibre over y_0) does not by itself produce an exactly closing chain.
  - The honest content of this branch is Lemma S below (the atomic, su-invariant section case), where closure follows from invariance and not from powers.
- **Dense group (e.g. Lebesgue conditionals).** The invariance principle gives nothing, as shown above. Everything rests on the steering argument (Block R13-a Proposition CP), which is independent of the invariance principle. I did not re-verify CP here.

### 2.3 A locally constant special case where (SC) holds (Lemma Q; proved)
**Setting.**
- Let N be the suspension of the full shift on k ≥ 2 symbols, with roof r(x) = r_{x_0} depending only on the zeroth symbol, realised as a hyperbolic set.
- Let the fibre velocity also be locally constant: phi is chosen so that the theta-advance over one return is f_{x_0}.

**Leg structure.** Return times and theta-advances are then the same for sequences with the same future (respectively, the same past). Hence:
- strong stable leaves are {(w, h, theta) : w_{≥0} = x_{≥0}}, at the same height h and the same theta;
- strong unstable leaves are {(w, h, theta) : w_{<0} = x_{<0}}, at the same height and the same theta.

**The chain (a Brin quadrilateral).** Take
- x_1 = (x_{<0}, y_{≥0}),
- x_2 = (w_{<0}, y_{≥0}),
- x_3 = (w_{<0}, x_{≥0}) = p,

all at height 0 and fibre coordinate theta_0. Then p ∈ W^{ss}(x_0), so (E) holds with tau = 0.

**Isolation.** Orbit-isolated vertices are obtained by choosing y and w generic, i.e. outside the countably many bad choices.

So in this model the invariance principle is not needed. Closure comes from **joint integrability** (Brin quadrilaterals with zero temporal and zero fibre displacement).

**Twisting.** Twisting phi (letting it depend on more coordinates) gives quadrilateral holonomies H(y, w) that are generally nonzero. Solvability of (Theta) then becomes a genuinely arithmetic and topological question about the values of H on Cantor parameter sets and their infinite sums. In the twisted model I could neither solve (Theta) nor build an obstruction to it. One reason a counterexample is hard: legs may leave the horseshoe (points of W^{uu}(x_0) outside Lambda), so the whole global flow enters.

### 2.4 Lemma S (atomic su-invariant section ⇒ closure; proved)
**Statement.** Let F = g_t be a flow on a fibre bundle pi: M -> N over a flow h_t on N, with continuous strong stable and unstable holonomies h^s, h^u between fibres. Let sigma: N_0 -> M be a g-invariant section (pi∘sigma = id, g_t∘sigma = sigma∘h_t), defined on an h-invariant set N_0. Assume sigma is s- and u-holonomy invariant: sigma(y') = h^s_{y,y'}(sigma(y)) for y' ∈ W^{ss}_N(y), and likewise for u.

This is the "Dirac conditional" outcome of [AV] when the disintegration is a single atom.

Then every base chain y_0, y_1, y_2, ... (alternating u/s legs inside N_0) with limit p_N ∈ W^{ss}_N(h_tau y_0) lifts to a chain x_i := sigma(y_i) in M with p = sigma(p_N) ∈ W^{ss}_M(g_tau x_0).

**Proof.**
1. **Each leg lifts.** By holonomy invariance, sigma(y_{i+1}) = h^{u or s}(sigma(y_i)). By definition of the holonomies, this point lies in the strong unstable (respectively stable) set of sigma(y_i) in M.
2. **The limit is on the section.** sigma is continuous on N_0 (assume this, as [AV] provides on supp mu when the conditionals vary continuously) and p_N ∈ N_0, so x_i -> sigma(p_N) = p.
3. **The endpoint closes.** By invariance, g_tau x_0 = sigma(h_tau y_0). By s-holonomy invariance, the unique point of W^{ss}_M(g_tau x_0) over p_N is h^s(sigma(h_tau y_0)) = sigma(p_N) = p. ∎

**Constants.** The quantitative clauses (L1)–(L4) are inherited from the base chain if the holonomies are Lipschitz with uniform constants and the fibre contributes no exponential growth. In this structured setting that holds by fibre bunching (cited).

**Consequence.** In the atomic branch, (SC) reduces to the hyperbolic base, where it holds by Theorem H's argument. This is a proof only inside the skew-product class.

## 3. Theorem H: (SC) holds when mu is hyperbolic (proved, modulo cited theorems)

### Cited results
- **[KLY] Katok's horseshoe theorem, flow version.** References: Katok, Publ. IHES 51 (1980); Lian–Young (2012) for C^2 flows in any dimension. The strengthened weak* clause (H3) is known for diffeomorphisms (e.g. Avila–Crovisier–Wilkinson 2016); I believe it holds for flows by the same construction but have not checked it.
  - Setting: mu is g_1-ergodic, h_mu(g_1) > 0, mu(Zero X) = 0, and every exponent except the flow direction is nonzero. Let chi_0 be the smallest absolute value of these exponents.
  - Conclusion: there is theta > 0, depending only on chi_0 (e.g. theta = chi_0/3), such that for every epsilon > 0 there is a compact invariant set Lambda with:
    - **(H1)** Lambda is a locally maximal hyperbolic set for the flow. Its local strong stable and unstable manifolds satisfy, for some K ≥ 1 and delta > 0:
      - d(g_t z, g_t x) <= K e^{-theta t} d(z, x) for z ∈ W^{ss}_delta(x), t >= 0;
      - the time-reversed estimate for W^{uu}_delta(x), t <= 0.
    - **(H2)** g_t restricted to Lambda is conjugate to the suspension, by a continuous positive roof, of a transitive subshift of finite type with positive entropy. In particular Lambda is not a finite union of orbits.
    - **(H3)** every g_t-invariant probability m on Lambda satisfies |m(d) - mu(d)| < epsilon, where d = div_nu X.
- **[HS] Standard hyperbolic-set facts** (cited).
  - **Local product structure:** for x, z ∈ Lambda close enough, there is a small sigma such that W^{uu}_delta(x) ∩ W^{ss}_delta(g_sigma z) is a single point [x, z] ∈ Lambda.
  - **Cantor slices:** for x ∈ Lambda, W^{ss}_delta(x) ∩ Lambda is uncountable. In symbolic terms: a fixed future with uncountably many admissible pasts, because the SFT is transitive with positive entropy.
  - **Generic points:** Lambda contains a point whose forward and backward orbits are both dense in Lambda (symbolically, a sequence containing every admissible word in both its past and its future).

### Theorem H
Let mu be as in (SC) (dim M ≥ 2, g_1-ergodic, h_mu(g_1) > 0, int J_1 dmu = mu(d) = 0). Assume also that every Lyapunov exponent of mu except the flow direction is nonzero. Then (SC) holds with finite chains.

### Proof
**Step 0 (excluded cases and constants).**
- mu(Zero X) = 0, since otherwise mu is carried by Zero X, where g_1 = id, and h_mu = 0.
- Let theta and K be given by [KLY]. Put eta := theta/2.
- Apply [KLY] with epsilon := eta/2. Since mu(d) = 0, (H3) gives |m(d)| < eta/2 for every invariant m on Lambda.

**Claim U.** There is C' with J_t(x) <= C' + eta|t| for all x ∈ Lambda and all real t.

*Proof of Claim U.*
1. If not, there are x_k ∈ Lambda and t_k with J_{t_k}(x_k) > k + eta|t_k|.
2. Since |J_t| <= ||d||_∞ |t|, we get |t_k| -> ∞. Pass to a subsequence with t_k -> +∞ or t_k -> -∞.
3. **Case t_k -> +∞.** A weak* limit m of (1/t_k) int_0^{t_k} delta_{g_s x_k} ds is an invariant probability on Lambda, because Lambda is compact and invariant. It satisfies m(d) = lim J_{t_k}(x_k)/t_k >= eta, which contradicts |m(d)| < eta/2.
4. **Case t_k -> -∞.** Here J_{t_k}(x_k) = -int_{t_k}^0 d(g_s x_k) ds. The corresponding limit m satisfies m(d) <= -eta, again a contradiction. ∎

**Step 1 (base point).** Choose x_0 ∈ Lambda with forward and backward orbits both dense in Lambda.

**Fact F.**
- If r ≠ 0, then x_0 ∉ W^{ss}(g_r x_0) and x_0 ∉ W^{uu}(g_r x_0).
- Consequently W^{ss}(g_r x_0) and W^{ss}(g_{r'} x_0) are disjoint for r ≠ r'. The same holds for W^{uu}.

*Proof of Fact F.*
1. Suppose d(g_t x_0, g_{t+r} x_0) -> 0 as t -> +∞.
2. Take any z ∈ omega(x_0) = Lambda, with g_{t_k} x_0 -> z. By continuity g_{t_k + r} x_0 -> g_r z. Since the distance tends to 0, g_r z = z.
3. So every point of Lambda is fixed by g_r. Then every orbit in Lambda is periodic with period dividing r, or a zero, which contradicts (H2).
4. The backward case is the same, with alpha(x_0) = Lambda.
5. **Disjointness.** A common point of W^{ss}(g_r x_0) and W^{ss}(g_{r'} x_0) would make g_r x_0 and g_{r'} x_0 forward asymptotic, i.e. x_0 ∈ W^{ss}(g_{r'-r} x_0) after shifting; so r = r'. ∎

**Step 2 (the u-legs).**
1. By density of the forward orbit, there are arbitrarily large T with d(g_T x_0, x_0) small.
2. Put x_1 := [x_0, g_T x_0] ∈ W^{uu}_delta(x_0) ∩ W^{ss}_delta(g_{tau} x_0) ∩ Lambda, where tau := T + sigma and |sigma| < 1/2.
3. **x_1 ≠ x_0:** otherwise x_0 ∈ W^{ss}(g_tau x_0) with tau ≠ 0, contradicting Fact F.
4. **x_1 is not on the orbit of x_0:** if x_1 = g_r x_0, then g_r x_0 ∈ W^{uu}(x_0), so r = 0 by Fact F, and x_1 = x_0, which was excluded.
5. Do the same with T' > T + 1. This gives x_1' ∈ W^{uu}_delta(x_0) ∩ W^{ss}_delta(g_{tau'} x_0), with tau' ≠ tau.
6. **x_1' is not on the orbit of x_1:**
   - If x_1' = g_r x_1, then x_1' ∈ W^{uu}(x_0) ∩ W^{uu}(g_r x_0), so r = 0 by Fact F.
   - Then x_1' = x_1 would lie in W^{ss}(g_tau x_0) ∩ W^{ss}(g_{tau'} x_0), which is empty by Fact F.

**Step 3 (the s-legs).**
1. An orbit meets the local strong stable disc W^{ss}_delta(x_1) in a countable set: the disc is transverse to X, so the times are discrete.
2. W^{ss}_delta(x_1) ∩ Lambda is uncountable by [HS].
3. Choose x_2 in it outside the orbits of x_0, x_1 and x_1'.
4. Then choose x_2' ∈ W^{ss}_delta(x_1') ∩ Lambda outside the orbits of x_0, x_1, x_1' and x_2.

**Step 4 (the sequences).**
- (a_i): a_0 = x_0, a_1 = x_1, and a_i = x_2 for i ≥ 2.
- (b_i): b_0 = x_0, b_1 = x_1', and b_i = x_2' for i ≥ 2.
- Limits: p = x_2 and p' = x_2'.

**Step 5 (verification).**
- **(L1) and (L2).**
  - The leg (a_0, a_1) is a u-leg inside W^{uu}_delta. So for t <= 0, d(g_t a_0, g_t a_1) <= K e^{-theta|t|} d(a_0, a_1).
  - The leg (a_1, a_2) is an s-leg inside W^{ss}_delta, with the analogous estimate for t >= 0.
  - All later legs have length 0.
  - The same holds for (b_i).
- **(L3).** Finitely many nonzero legs.
- **(L4).** Every vertex lies in Lambda, so Claim U gives all the required bounds, with eta = theta/2 < theta.
- **(E).** For t >= 0:
  d(g_t x_2, g_{t+tau} x_0) <= d(g_t x_2, g_t x_1) + d(g_t x_1, g_{t+tau} x_0) <= 2K delta e^{-theta t}.
  The same holds for (b) with tau'. Take C := max(K, 2K delta, 1).
- **Orbit-isolated vertex for (a): v = x_1.**
  - v ≠ x_0.
  - v is distinct from every other vertex of both sequences (x_0, x_2, x_1', x_2'). I read "occurs exactly once in the combined list" as "is a different point from every other vertex"; constant padding necessarily repeats points.
  - v is not a zero and not periodic: omega(x_1) = omega(x_0) = Lambda, because x_1 ∈ W^{ss}(g_tau x_0), and Lambda is not a single orbit.
  - The orbit of v contains none of x_0, x_1' (Steps 2.4 and 2.6), x_2, x_2' (Step 3), and hence neither limit point.
- **Orbit-isolated vertex for (b): v = x_1'.** The argument is symmetric. ∎

**Remark.** Theorem H uses int J_1 dmu = 0 only through (H3), to get eta < theta. Under GH this case cannot occur: R6-a Theorem 5.1 excludes hyperbolic positive-entropy measures. So Theorem H is a consistency check on (SC), not progress on the chain.

## 4. What remains open, stated precisely
The non-hyperbolic case of (SC) is open. Its core is the following.

**(PHC) Positional holonomy closure.** For mu non-hyperbolic (with a zero exponent transverse to X), find su-chains (finite, or infinite with summable steps) whose endpoint lies **exactly** on the weak-stable set of x_0.

Generic finite chains miss this target by a codimension. A dimension count (heuristic) in the stable-quotient picture:
- the quotient by strong stable leaves has dimension u + c + 1;
- the target (the flow line of x_0) has dimension 1;
- one u-leg sweeps out dimension u;
- so a single u-leg generically misses the target by c dimensions when c ≥ 1.

So one needs either:
- **joint integrability** with zero displacement (Lemma Q type), or
- an **atomic su-invariant section** (Lemma S type), or
- **accessibility with quantitative control**, i.e. continuity of the endpoint map on a parameter space of chains whose image covers a neighbourhood of the target, so that an intermediate-value / open-mapping argument closes exactly.

In the non-uniform setting the third option fails as stated. Chain vertices must be Pesin-regular, and the set of available leg endpoints is only a positive-conditional-measure, typically Cantor-like, subset of each leaf. So the endpoint map has no connected parameter domain, and continuity arguments do not apply.

The invariance principle addresses none of these three mechanisms in the generality of (SC) (Section 1). In the class where it applies it decides only the atomic case (Lemma S) and is vacuous in the isometric case (Model 2.1).

**Open questions.**
1. (SC) itself, for non-hyperbolic mu, for general smooth flows.
2. Already within Model 2.1 with a twisted phi over a horseshoe base: is (Theta) solvable by finite or summable infinite chains, with orbit-isolated vertices? This is the cleanest test case. A negative answer, accounting for legs that leave the horseshoe, would give a counterexample to (SC) without contradicting the problem.
3. Whether a non-uniform invariance principle for the projective cocycle on E^{cs}/E^s exists in the generality of (SC). Even if it does, Section 1.2(i) shows it would not by itself yield (E).

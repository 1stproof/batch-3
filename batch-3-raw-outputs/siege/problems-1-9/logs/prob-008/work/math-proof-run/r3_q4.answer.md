## 1. A no-go statement: Proposition E alone cannot give zero entropy

**Proposition 1.1.** Let X generate a transitive C^∞ Anosov flow on a compact manifold M, for example the geodesic flow of a closed negatively curved surface, which has positive topological entropy. Then the following hold:
(a) every smooth u with Xu = 0 is constant;
(b) X(C^∞) is closed in C^∞, and 1 ∉ X(C^∞);
(c) the tame estimate of fact 3 holds: there are m and C with ‖u − c(u)‖_{C^1} ≤ C‖Xu‖_{C^m} for all smooth u;
(d) consequently the conclusion of Proposition E holds verbatim, with the same proof.

*Proof.* (a) u is constant on orbits, and some orbit is dense.
(b) [Cited from memory: Livšic's theorem together with the C^∞ regularity theorem of de la Llave–Marco–Moriyón (1986) / Journé.] For a transitive Anosov flow, a C^∞ function f is Xu for some C^∞ u if and only if ∫_γ f = 0 for every periodic orbit γ. Hence X(C^∞) = ⋂_γ ker δ_γ is closed. If 1 = Xu, then u would increase along orbits, which is impossible at a maximum of u.
(c) X induces a continuous linear bijection C^∞/ℝ → X(C^∞) between Fréchet spaces. By the open mapping theorem its inverse is continuous, so inf_c ‖u − c‖_{C^1} ≤ C‖Xu‖_{C^m} for some m. Passing to the normalised mean c(u) costs at most a factor 2.
(d) The proof of Proposition E uses only (b) and (c): X(C^∞) ⊕ ℝ1 is closed (a closed subspace plus a line not contained in it), and the projection to ℝ1 is continuous. ∎

**Consequence.** Every step of the suggested programme that uses only Proposition E, the Katok entropy formula, pigeonholing on endpoints and on finitely many extra coordinates, and generic dynamical facts (Lipschitz bounds, flow boxes) also applies to transitive Anosov flows. Since these flows have positive entropy, such an argument cannot end in a contradiction. Hence **any proof along this route must use an input about the space I of invariant distributions that comes from the distributional part of GH.** This means fact 1 for distributions u, or its consequences (F1), (F2), Theorem J; fact 1 for smooth u is not enough. For Anosov flows, I contains every periodic-orbit measure, so it is infinite-dimensional. In the volume case the needed input is I = ℝω. The term T_{xy} ∈ I_0 in Proposition E is exactly where the Anosov flow evades the bump test: there, γ_x^T − γ_y^T can be close to a nonzero difference of invariant measures.

So the crux is a smallness statement about I, isolated in §3 below. §2 shows that such a statement suffices, provided a second (purely ergodic-theoretic) input is also available.

## 2. Conditional theorem (windows of fixed length)

**Hypotheses.** GH holds, dim M ≥ 2, and:
* **(NZ)** X has no zeros.
* **(FI)** dim I < ∞.
* **(R)** for the given g_1-ergodic μ with h := h_μ(g_1) > 0, there is B > 1 with μ(Bad_L) → 0 as L → ∞ (L ∈ ℕ). Here
  Bad_L := {z : ∃ s_1, s_2 ∈ [−1, L+1], |s_1 − s_2| ≥ τ_0, d(g_{s_1}z, g_{s_2}z) ≤ 4L^{−B}},
  and τ_0 is the flow-box constant below.

**Theorem 2.1.** Under GH, (NZ) and (FI), every g_1-ergodic μ that satisfies (R) has h_μ(g_1) = 0. Hence, if (R) holds for every ergodic μ of positive entropy, then h_top(g_1) = 0 by the variational principle, and so g_t has zero topological entropy.

Remarks. (R) concerns only μ and the flow: it says that the orbit segment of length L of a μ-typical point has no "repeat" (two stretches at time distance ≥ τ_0 that are polynomially close). At scale 4L^{−B} the Lipschitz bound turns a repeat into an ε-shadowing of two stretches of length ≈ (B/Λ) log L. Return-time results of Ornstein–Weiss type say that a typical point first returns to its n-Bowen ball at time ≈ e^{h_μ n} (Ornstein–Weiss 1993 for partitions; Varandas 2009 for Bowen balls of maps; cited from memory, and I have not checked the flow version). They give the analogous statement for the *initial* stretch only. (R) asks for no repeat *anywhere* in the window. That is a "longest repeated block = O(log L)" statement, which I cannot derive from those results: union bounds lose a factor L. I recall (not reliably) that Shields has negative results on match lengths for general ergodic processes, so (R) may require the smooth structure. I do **not** claim (R).

### Proof of Theorem 2.1

**(a) Flow box.** By (NZ) and compactness there are τ_0 > 0 and c_0 > 0 with d(p, g_s p) ≥ c_0|s| for all p ∈ M and |s| ≤ τ_0. (In a finite atlas, g_s p = p + sX(p) + O(s^2) uniformly, and |X| ≥ a > 0.) Put V := max|X|. Let Λ be such that Lip(g_t) ≤ e^{Λ|t|}. Fix h_0 ∈ (0,1] with Lip(g_s) ≤ 2 for 0 ≤ s ≤ h_0.

**(b) Finite-dimensional control of I.** Each element of I is a distribution on a compact manifold, so it has finite order. By (FI) there is N with I ⊂ (C^N)′. Let k = dim I. Choose φ_1, …, φ_k ∈ C^∞ such that the map I → ℝ^k, T ↦ (T(φ_i))_i, is injective; this is possible for any finite family of linearly independent functionals. All norms on I are equivalent, so there is C_I with

  ‖T‖_{(C^N)′} ≤ C_I max_i |T(φ_i)|  for all T ∈ I.   (2.1)

Put N′ := max(N, m′), where m′ is as in Proposition E. Let A := 1 + max_i ‖φ_i‖_{C^{m′}}.

**(c) Window estimate.** Let z, w ∈ M and L ≥ 1. Suppose that d(z,w) ≤ η, d(g_L z, g_L w) ≤ η, and |γ_z^L(φ_i) − γ_w^L(φ_i)| ≤ η for all i. Then for every smooth ψ,

  |γ_z^L(ψ) − γ_w^L(ψ)| ≤ C_2 η ‖ψ‖_{C^{N′}},  with C_2 := C_I(1 + 2C′A) + 2C′, independent of L.   (2.2)

*Proof.* By Proposition E, γ_z^L − γ_w^L = T + E with T ∈ I and ‖E‖_{(C^{m′})′} ≤ 2C′η. So |T(φ_i)| ≤ η + 2C′ηA, and by (2.1), |T(ψ)| ≤ C_I η(1 + 2C′A)‖ψ‖_{C^N}. Add |E(ψ)| ≤ 2C′η‖ψ‖_{C^{m′}}. ∎

**(d) Hausdorff step.** Fix r_* below the convexity radius, and bump functions χ_{q,ρ} with 0 ≤ χ ≤ 1, χ = 1 on B(q, ρ/2), supp χ ⊂ B(q, ρ), and ‖χ_{q,ρ}‖_{C^{N′}} ≤ C_χ ρ^{−N′} for ρ ≤ r_*. Let ρ ≤ min(r_*, V, 1) and L ≥ 1. Suppose the hypotheses of (c) hold with η ≤ c_3 ρ^{N′+1}, where c_3 := 1/(4VC_2C_χ). Then every g_t w (t ∈ [0, L]) lies within distance ρ of g_{[0,L]}z.

*Proof.* Suppose q := g_t w had d(q, g_{[0,L]}z) ≥ ρ. Then χ_{q,ρ} vanishes on g_{[0,L]}z, so γ_z^L(χ) = 0. For |s − t| ≤ ρ/(2V) we have d(g_s w, q) ≤ ρ/2. The set of such s in [0, L] has length ≥ ρ/(2V), because L ≥ ρ/V. Hence γ_w^L(χ) ≥ ρ/(2V). On the other hand, (2.2) gives γ_w^L(χ) ≤ C_2 c_3 ρ^{N′+1} C_χ ρ^{−N′} = ρ/(4V). This is a contradiction. ∎

**(e) Time shadowing on a good window.** Let ε ∈ (0,1). Let L ≥ 1 be an integer, and let ρ ≤ min(r_*, ε c_0 h_0/(100 V L), ε/8). Suppose z satisfies the no-repeat condition

  (NR_ρ) d(g_{s_1}z, g_{s_2}z) > 4ρ whenever s_1, s_2 ∈ [−1, L+1] and |s_1 − s_2| ≥ τ_0,

and the hypotheses of (c) hold with η ≤ min(c_3 ρ^{N′+1}, ρ). Then d(g_t z, g_t w) < ε/2 for all t ∈ [0, L].

*Proof.* Let K := ⌈L/h_0⌉ and h := L/K ∈ [h_0/2, h_0]. By (d), for each j = 0, …, K choose σ_j ∈ [0, L] with d(g_{jh}w, g_{σ_j}z) ≤ ρ; take σ_0 := 0, which is allowed since d(z,w) ≤ η ≤ ρ. Since Lip(g_h) ≤ 2, d(g_{(j+1)h}w, g_{σ_j+h}z) ≤ 2ρ, and hence d(g_{σ_j+h}z, g_{σ_{j+1}}z) ≤ 3ρ. Both times lie in [0, L+1]. By (NR_ρ), |σ_{j+1} − σ_j − h| < τ_0. Then the flow box (a), applied at the earlier of the two points, gives c_0|σ_{j+1} − σ_j − h| ≤ 3ρ. By induction, |σ_j − jh| ≤ 3jρ/c_0 ≤ 3Kρ/c_0 ≤ 6Lρ/(c_0h_0). For t ∈ [jh, (j+1)h] with j < K,
  d(g_t z, g_t w) ≤ 2 d(g_{jh}z, g_{jh}w) ≤ 2(V|σ_j − jh| + ρ) ≤ 12VLρ/(c_0h_0) + 2ρ ≤ 0.12ε + 0.25ε < ε/2. ∎

Fix ρ_L := L^{−B}. For L ≥ L_0(ε) this satisfies the bound in (e), since B > 1. Put η_L := min(c_3ρ_L^{N′+1}, ρ_L). Then log(1/η_L) = O(log L), and z ∉ Bad_L is exactly (NR_{ρ_L}).

**(f) Counting.** Cited [Katok 1980, Thm 1.1, from memory]: for a homeomorphism f of a compact metric space, an f-ergodic μ, and β ∈ (0,1),
  lim_{ε→0} liminf_{n} (1/n) log N_μ(n, ε, β) = h_μ(f),
where N_μ(n, ε, β) is the minimal number of (n, ε)-Bowen balls covering a set of measure ≥ 1 − β.
Fix δ ∈ (0, h/4) and ε with liminf_n (1/n) log N_μ(n, ε, 1/2) > h − δ. A maximal (n, ε)-separated subset of a set G with μ(G) ≥ 1/2 spans G, so it has at least e^{(h−δ)n} points for n large.

Fix an integer L ≥ L_0(ε), and write β_L := μ(Bad_L) and θ_L := 2√β_L. For an integer n ≥ L, set W := ⌊n/L⌋. Window j (0 ≤ j < W) of x is the segment g_{[jL,(j+1)L]}x. It is *good* if g_{jL}x ∉ Bad_L. Apply Birkhoff's theorem to g_L and 1_{Bad_L}: (1/W) Σ_{j<W} 1_{Bad_L}(g_{jL}x) → F(x) a.e., with ∫F dμ = β_L. By Markov's inequality μ(F > √β_L) ≤ √β_L. Hence for n large the set G_n := {x : at most θ_L W windows of x are bad} has μ(G_n) ≥ 1 − √β_L − o(1) ≥ 1/2, provided β_L < 1/9.

Let E ⊂ G_n be (n, ε)-separated, so for x ≠ y in E, max_{0≤i<n} d(g_i x, g_i y) > ε. Attach to each x ∈ E the following data.
1. The set S(x) of its bad windows. There are at most e^{H(θ_L)W} choices, where H is the binary entropy function (in nats) and θ_L ≤ 1/2.
2. For j = 0, …, W, the cell containing g_{jL}x in a fixed partition of M into at most C_M η_L^{−D} sets of diameter ≤ η_L, where D := dim M. (Below, n denotes the time length, not the dimension.)
3. For each good window j and each i, the interval of length η_L containing γ^L_{g_{jL}x}(φ_i) ∈ [−L‖φ_i‖_∞, L‖φ_i‖_∞]. There are at most (2LA/η_L + 1)^k choices per window.
4. For each bad window j, and for the tail [WL, n] (of length < L), the index of a set containing g_{jL}x (respectively g_{WL}x) in a fixed cover 𝒰_L of M by sets U with diam g_t(U) ≤ ε/2 for t ∈ [0, L]. One can take 𝒰_L to be the Bowen balls B_{g_1}(w, L+1, ε′) of a minimal (L+1, ε′)-spanning set, where ε′ is such that d(a,b) ≤ 2ε′ implies d(g_s a, g_s b) ≤ ε/2 for s ∈ [0,1]. By the definition of h_top := h_top(g_1) < ∞ (finite for a C^1 flow on a compact manifold), |𝒰_L| ≤ e^{(h_top+1)(L+1)} for L large.

Suppose x, y ∈ E carry the same data. On a good window, z = g_{jL}x and w = g_{jL}y satisfy the hypotheses of (c) with η = η_L, and z ∉ Bad_L; so by (e), d(g_t x, g_t y) < ε/2 on that window. On a bad window and on the tail this holds by item 4. Hence max_{t∈[0,n]} d(g_t x, g_t y) < ε, so x = y. Therefore |E| is at most the number of data values, and

  (1/n) log |E| ≤ H(θ_L)/L + (W+1)[D log(C_M/η_L) + k log(2LA/η_L + 1)]/n + (θ_L W + 1)(h_top + 1)(L+1)/n.

Letting n → ∞ gives

  limsup_n (1/n) log |E| ≤ H(θ_L)/L + O(log L / L) + θ_L(h_top + 1)(1 + 1/L).

By (R), θ_L → 0. So for L large the right side is < h − δ, which contradicts the lower bound e^{(h−δ)n} from Katok's formula. ∎

**Status of §2.** Theorem 2.1 is proved, modulo the cited Katok entropy formula, the finiteness of h_top for C^1 flows, and the standard flow-box fact. Its hypotheses (FI) and (R) are **not** proved from GH. (NZ) is also not proved from GH in general: fact 6 gives only d(p) ≠ 0 at zeros. Note that (FI) with dim I = 1 is equivalent to (V) by fact 5. In that case Theorem V gives the result without (R), because Step B of Theorem V proves no-repeat for *all* points using I = ℝω.

Why windows of *fixed* length L. The suggested programme pigeonholes on (x, g_T x) with resolution T^{−B}. It then needs no near-return over the whole of [−1, T+1] at a polynomial scale for most x, and union bounds over the starting time lose a factor T. With fixed-length windows, the per-window cost is polynomial in L, hence a rate O(log L/L) → 0. The no-repeat requirement then becomes the single soft statement μ(Bad_L) → 0, with no rate needed, because Birkhoff's theorem for g_L handles the fraction of bad windows. The cost of the bad windows is controlled by h_top < ∞. This also shows that (FI) can be weakened: it suffices that, for each L, the I_0-component on bumps at scale L^{−B} is controlled as in (2.1) by k_L = o(L/log L) functionals with norms and constant C_{I,L} polynomial in L.

## 3. The missing inputs, stated precisely

**(ML-I) Smallness of I (the essential gap).** Some statement giving the control (2.1) of T ∈ I_0 on bumps of scale ρ by few coordinates. Strongest natural form: dim I < ∞. Weakest form that suffices for §2: for every L there are functions φ_1, …, φ_{k_L} with k_L = o(L/log L) and ‖φ_i‖_{C^{m′}} ≤ L^{O(1)} such that every T ∈ I_0 satisfies sup_{q} |T(χ_{q,L^{−B}})| ≤ L^{O(1)}(max_i |T(φ_i)| + ‖T‖_{(C^{m'})'}·L^{−B″}), with B″ large. (I have not checked this weak form in the same detail as (2.1); (2.1) itself is what §2 uses.)
By §1, ML-I cannot follow from facts 3, 4 or Proposition E alone, because transitive Anosov flows satisfy those and have infinite-dimensional I. It must use distributional GH (fact 1 for u ∈ D′, (F1), (F2), Theorem J). Since dim I = 1 is equivalent to (V) (fact 5), ML-I in its strong form is a weakening of (V), but it is still unproved. I found no argument giving even finitely many ergodic invariant measures from GH.

**(ML-R) No long repeats for positive-entropy measures.** Hypothesis (R) of §2: μ(Bad_L) → 0. This is needed for any μ-based version of Step D, because for general μ there is no analogue of the polynomial no-near-return Step B. Under (V), Step B gives it for all points. Brin–Katok counting does handle repeats with time gap r ≤ c·(B/Λ) log L, where c < 1 depends on h. Sketch: a repeat at gap r forces the (n, ε)-Bowen ball, n ≈ (B/Λ) log L, to be determined by the r-orbit; there are ≤ e^{(h+δ)r} such r-orbits among Brin–Katok-good points, each of measure ≤ e^{−(h−δ)n}. Gaps up to L require an Ornstein–Weiss-type "longest repeat" bound for the flow, which I could not supply.

**(NZ)** is used only for the flow box in (e). It could perhaps be removed by cutting windows at visits near zeros. However, the cost then involves μ(near zeros)·log(1/|X|), and I did not control that.

**Route (i) (Jacobian weights).** Remark, proved by Sion's minimax theorem: for ψ with ν(ψ) = 0 and R > 0,
 inf{‖ψ − Pφ‖_{C^0} : φ ∈ C^∞, ‖φ‖_{C^1} ≤ R} = sup{λ(ψ) − R‖λ∘P‖_{(C^1)′} : λ a signed measure, ‖λ‖ ≤ 1}.
Here ‖λ∘P‖_{(C^1)′} is the sup of λ(Pφ) over smooth φ with ‖φ‖_{C^1} ≤ 1. Justification: the λ-ball is weak-* compact and convex, the φ-set is convex, and the pairing is bilinear and weak-* continuous in λ. Since λ∘P = −(X(λ/ν))ν, quantitative approximate solvability of P on bumps is equivalent to a quantitative form of (F1): measures u = λ/ν with Xu small in (C^1)′ must be nearly constant when tested on bumps. GH gives only the qualitative (F1). So route (i) also reduces to a quantitative distributional-GH statement of the same kind as ML-I, and I could not prove it.

## 4. Summary of what is established

* **Proved.** Proposition 1.1 (no-go). Modulo the cited Livšic theorem and the de la Llave–Marco–Moriyón regularity theorem, Proposition E and the tame closed-range estimate hold for transitive Anosov flows. So the occupation-measure route must use distributional hypoellipticity through a smallness property of I.
* **Proved (conditional).** Theorem 2.1: GH + (NZ) + dim I < ∞ + (R) for μ ⇒ h_μ = 0. Hence h_top = 0 if (R) holds for all positive-entropy ergodic μ. It uses the cited Katok entropy formula, h_top(g_1) < ∞ for C^1 flows, and Birkhoff's theorem.
* **Not proved / open.** The problem in the general case without (V): ML-I (smallness of I, e.g. dim I < ∞, from GH); ML-R (no long repeats for positive-entropy ergodic measures of the flow); removal of (NZ). I do not claim a proof of the original problem in the non-volume-preserving case.

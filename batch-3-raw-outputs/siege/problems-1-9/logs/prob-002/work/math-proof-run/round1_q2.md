Open question attempted: construct an estimator attaining the UPPER BOUND sup_{f in F(alpha,r)} E||hat f - f||_{L^2}^2 <~ n^{-2alpha/(2alpha+d+3)} (up to log factors), for all alpha>1, d>=2, small fixed r — or identify the obstruction and the best upper rate provable.

BACKGROUND HEURISTIC (from the planner; unverified — check it, do not trust it).
Write V_f = grad u_f. For f = 1, u_1 = (|x|^2-1)/(2d), V_1 = x/d (radial, vanishing only at the origin). For f in F(alpha,r) with r small, u_f is a C^{alpha+1}-small perturbation of u_1, so V_f has a single nondegenerate zero (a "source") near 0 and its flow lines are close to rays.
Exact identity: for f, g = f + h, div(g grad(u_g - u_f)) = -div(h grad u_f), i.e. u_g - u_f = -L_g^{-1} div(h V_f) with L_g w = div(g grad w), Dirichlet conditions. Linearization at f: Dh = -L_f^{-1} div(h V_f).
Microlocal picture: if h oscillates at frequency lambda in directions PERPENDICULAR to V_f (angular directions) and is smooth along V_f, then div(hV_f) = V_f.grad h + h div V_f has amplitude ~ |h| (no factor lambda), and L^{-1} gains lambda^{-2}, so ||Dh||_{L^2} ~ lambda^{-2}||h||_{L^2} (smoothing of degree 2). If h oscillates along V_f at frequency mu, ||Dh|| ~ mu lambda^{-2} ||h||. Heuristic symbol of the forward linearization: sigma(xi) ~ (1+|xi_rad|)/|xi|^2 (away from the origin; near the source at distance rho, (1+rho|xi_rad|)/|xi|^2).
Consequences:
 (a) Lower bound: Assouad hypercube of ~2^{j(d-1)} perturbations h_k = c 2^{-j alpha} a(|x|) psi(2^j(theta - theta_k)) supported in an annulus 1/4<|x|<3/4 (angular wavelets with vanishing moments, radial profile smooth at scale 1). Each satisfies ||h_k||_2^2 ~ 2^{-2j alpha - j(d-1)}, ||u_{f_theta} - u_{f_theta'}||_2^2 ~ 2^{-4j}||h_k||_2^2 for neighbours. Choosing n 2^{-j(2alpha+d+3)} ~ 1 gives risk >~ 2^{-2j alpha} = n^{-2alpha/(2alpha+d+3)}, which is SLOWER than the "degree-one" rate n^{-2alpha/(2alpha+d+2)} that isotropic wavelet perturbations give.
 (b) Upper bound heuristic: a spectral cut-off at |xi| <= 2^J has variance n^{-1} int_{|xi|<2^J} sigma(xi)^{-2} dxi ~ n^{-1} 2^{4J} 2^{J(d-1)} int dxi_rad/(1+xi_rad^2) ~ n^{-1} 2^{J(d+3)}, bias^2 ~ 2^{-2J alpha}, giving n^{-2alpha/(2alpha+d+3)}. The source point contributes int rho^{d-1} rho^{-1} drho < infinity, so it should not change the rate for d >= 2.
 (c) Transport/ray picture: given u, f solves the first-order equation V.grad f + f Delta u = 1 along the flow lines of V = grad u emanating from the source, with f(source) = 1/Delta u(source). Errors in u enter through Delta u (two derivatives) pointwise, but integrating along the flow line gains one derivative in the radial direction only.
CONJECTURE: for every alpha>1, d>=2 and small fixed r, the minimax rate is n^{-2alpha/(2alpha+d+3)} up to log factors.

YOUR TASK. Prove the upper bound (b) rigorously. Suggested route (you may choose another): a two-step / plug-in estimator.
 Step 1: estimate u_f by a wavelet or spectral projection estimator hat u at a resolution adapted to the problem (possibly several resolutions for different derivatives), and get a pilot hat f (e.g. via the transport equation (c) or a crude regularized inversion) consistent in C^1 at a polynomial rate.
 Step 2: refine using the exact identity / linearization at the pilot, with a regularized inverse of the operator h -> L^{-1}div(h V) whose (anisotropic) singular behaviour is (1+|xi_rad|)/|xi|^2, i.e. a stability estimate of the form ||h||_{L^2} <~ ||L_f^{-1} div(h V_f)||_{Y} with Y an anisotropic norm (two derivatives in angular directions, one in the flow direction; with appropriate weights near the source point), combined with a noise bound for the white noise in the dual of Y at resolution 2^J giving variance n^{-1}2^{J(d+3)}.
Alternatively: work directly with the first-order transport equation V.grad f + f Delta u = 1 along flow lines from the source, estimate the needed derivatives of u, and integrate along flow lines so that the radial integration averages the noise (gaining one derivative radially).
Show every step: the stability estimate (with proof), the stochastic bounds (Gaussian process suprema / variance computations), the handling of the source point and of the boundary, the nonlinear remainder, and the final balance of bias and variance.

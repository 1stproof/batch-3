# round3_q4: reducing to finitely generated P

Plan: (1) descent of f.p. objects, and a proof that kernel faces are principal for arbitrary P. This fixes Step 0 of Background B. (2) What goes wrong with valuations and covers. (3) Where the cohomological reduction stands. (4) Summary.

Conventions are those of Background B. P_S is the saturation, in <S>^gp, of the submonoid generated by the finite set S ⊂ P. These P_S are fine, and P is their filtered union.

## 1. Filtered colimits and kernel faces

**1.1 Filtered colimits stay in the category (proved).** Let (M_j) be a filtered system of integral, saturated, sharp monoids, and let M be its colimit as commutative monoids. Each of the three properties is witnessed by finitely many elements, so each can be checked at a finite stage:
- If a+c = b+c in M, then this already holds at some stage, where integrality gives a = b.
- If nx = ny in M^gp, this holds at some stage, so x = y there.
- If nx ∈ M, then nx lies in M_j at some stage, and saturation of M_j gives x ∈ M_j.
- If x+y = 0, this holds at some stage, so x = 0.

So M is again an object, and it is the colimit in our category.

In particular P = colim_S P_S. Pushouts (pushout, then integralize, saturate, sharpen) commute with this colimit, because they are colimits.

**1.2 Descent of f.p. objects (sketched; standard argument).** Let Q be f.p. under P. Then Q is the filtered colimit, over pairs (S, finite T ⊂ Q), of the images R_{S,T} of P ⊕_{P_S} Q'_{S,T}, where Q'_{S,T} is the saturated sharp image of P_S ⊕ N^T. Because Q is f.p., id_Q factors through some stage. So Q is a retract of R = P ⊕_{P_S} Q' with Q' fine over P_S.

The idempotent e of R is determined by finitely many data. After enlarging S, it therefore descends to an idempotent e_S of a fine R_S over P_S, with R = P ⊕_{P_S} R_S. Then:
- Q is the coequalizer of (e, id).
- Coequalizers commute with base change.
- The image of an idempotent on a fine monoid is fine.

Hence Q ≅ P ⊕_{P_S} Q_S with Q_S = image(e_S) fine. Morphisms and pushouts between f.p. objects descend in the same way, after enlarging S. The point is that Hom_P(P ⊕_{P_S} Q_S, X) = Hom_{P_S}(Q_S, X), and Q_S is finitely presented over P_S. (Sketch only: the bookkeeping of "enlarge S" is routine and is not written out.)

**1.3 Theorem (proved). For every f.p. f : P → Q, the kernel face Φ_Q = f^{-1}(0) is principal, i.e. generated as a face by one element. Moreover Φ_Q is the face generated by ker(P_S → Q_S) for all large S.**

*Proof.*

Setup. Write Q = P ⊕_{P_0} Q_0 with P_0 = P_{S_0} and Q_0 fine, using f_0 : P_0 → Q_0. Put G = (P^gp ⊕ Q_0^gp)/P_0^gp modulo torsion. The pushout is Q = R/R^×, where R is the saturation in G of the image of P ⊕ Q_0.

Characterizing the kernel. Let x ∈ P. Then f(x) = 0 iff x is a unit of R. That holds iff there are n ≥ 1, p ∈ P and q ∈ Q_0 with
- -nx = p + q in G,
- i.e. (−nx − p, −q) lies in the image of P_0^gp, up to torsion,
- i.e. there are m ≥ 1 and a ∈ L := P_0^gp with m(nx + p) = −a in P^gp and mq = f_0^gp(a).

Hence Φ_Q is the face generated by the set −K, where
K = { a ∈ L : f_0^gp(a) ∈ Q_0 and −a ∈ P }.

Here we used saturation of Q_0: f_0^gp(a) = mq ∈ Q_0. Conversely, every −a with a ∈ K lies in the kernel: −a + a = 0 in R, and a ∈ R.

Describing K by cones. L is a lattice of finite rank. For any saturated monoid N, the intersection N ∩ L equals L ∩ cone_R(N ∩ L). Proof of this claim: if x ∈ L lies in the real cone, Carathéodory writes x as a nonnegative combination of linearly independent elements of N ∩ L. The coefficients are then rational, so kx ∈ N for some k ≥ 1, and saturation gives x ∈ N.

Apply this to (f_0^gp)^{-1}(Q_0) and to (−P) ∩ L. Writing C(·) for the real cone spanned:
K = L ∩ C(A) ∩ C(−P ∩ L), with A = (f_0^gp)^{-1}(Q_0).
So K = L ∩ D for a convex cone D ⊃ C(K).

Choosing a generator. Pick finitely many elements of K that span the linear span of K, and let e be their sum. Then e lies in the relative interior of C(K). Now take any c ∈ K. For t ≫ 0 we have te − c ∈ C(K) ∩ L ⊂ K. So −c = −te + (te − c) with −(te − c) ∈ P, which gives −c ≤ t(−e) in P.

Conclusion. Every element of Φ_Q is ≤ a multiple of −e: if x ∈ Φ_Q, then x ≤ −a ≤ t(−e) for some a ∈ K. Also −e ∈ Φ_Q. So Φ_Q is the face generated by x_Q := −e.

Descent. Finally take S ⊃ S_0 ∪ {−e}. Then −e ∈ P_S, and −e already becomes a unit in P_S ⊕_{P_0} Q_0, since e ∈ L maps into Q_0. ∎

**Remark (proved).** "For S large" is genuinely needed. Take P = N^2 and S = {e1, e1+2e2}, and let Q_S be the saturated monoid generated by P_S and −2e2 = e1 − (e1+2e2). This Q_S is sharp, and P_S → Q_S has trivial kernel. But the kernel of P → P ⊕_{P_S} Q_S is the face N·e2, because e2 and −2e2 together become units.

**Consequence (proved).** Step 0 of Background B holds for arbitrary P:
- B_P is a preorder, equivalent to the poset of principal faces.
- (Lan F)(Q) = F(P[−x_Q]^♯) for f.p. Q.
- π^{-1}F is the sheafification of F∘b on f.p. objects.

## 2. Valuations and covers

**Extension can fail (proved).** A valuation w of P_S need not extend to P. Take P = N^2, S = {e1, e1+2e2}, and w with w(e1+2e2) = 0 < w(e1). Any extension would need 2w(e2) = −w(e1) < 0.

**What survives (proved).** Every valuation v of P restricts to valuations of the P_S. Conversely, by Theorem 1.3, kernel faces are detected at finite level. But a birhizomic cover {Q → Q_i} of Q over P only needs to cover valuations of Q. Its descended family {Q_S → Q_{i,S}}, after Lemma K and enlarging S, covers exactly the valuations of Q_S that extend to Q. Valuations of Q_S that do not extend need not be covered. So **covers do not descend to covers over P_S**, and the topos Sh(A_P) is *not* naively the limit of the Sh(A_{P_S}).

A further unchecked point: birhizomy of P → Q_i at g is also not obviously inherited by P_S → Q_{i,S}. Test factorizations through infinitesimal maps over P_S do not come from P.

Proposed replacement (not carried out): use instead the site of f.p. objects over P_S, with the topology generated by families that cover the closed subset Val(P)|_{P_S} ⊂ Val(P_S). Then Sh(A_P) would be the limit of these modified sites. For SGA 4 VI 8.7 one would need these sites to be coherent with transition maps that preserve coverings. Lemma K provides finite subcovers, so quasi-compactness is plausible. However, the analogue of Background B's Step 4 (contractible nerves) must then be proved for the restricted valuation sets. Those sets are defined by infinitely many strict inequalities, so they are neither closed nor polyhedral, and the closed-convex nerve theorem does not apply directly.

## 3. Cohomology

A direct Čech approach needs the following. Take a finite subcomplex L of N_τ (the nerve of {E_i ∩ C_Ω}). One must find S such that L lies inside a contractible complex, computed from real (or finite-rank) valuations of Q_S that extend to P.

The witnesses for the simplices of L are finitely many valuations of P. Their restrictions to P_S are valuations of Q_S. For them, convex-hull arguments would work only if the sets E_{i,S} were closed in a common finite-dimensional space. For rank > 1 valuations they are not: this is the [0,1) ∪ [1,2] obstruction noted in Background B. I could not close this gap.

## 4. Summary of status

Proved:
- Filtered colimits stay in the category (1.1).
- Kernel faces of f.p. maps out of an arbitrary P are principal, and they are detected at a finite stage (1.3).
- Hence Step 0 (the Kan extension formula) holds for all P.
- Covers do not naively descend to P_S, because valuations of P_S need not extend (§2, with explicit examples).

Sketched: descent of f.p. objects, morphisms and pushouts (1.2).

Not established (open):
- (a) Whether a birhizomic cover over P descends to a family over some P_S that covers Val(P)|_{P_S} and consists of maps that are birhizomic in a suitable relative sense.
- (b) Contractibility of the kernel nerves N_τ for non-finitely-generated P. This needs a nerve theorem for the non-closed convex valuation sets.
- (c) Therefore whether R π_* π^{-1} F ≅ F holds for arbitrary P. The reduction yields no new case beyond finitely generated P. Not even the case where P^gp has finite rank is settled: that case still faces obstacle (b), via the example P = {x ∈ Z^3 : x3 > 0, or x3 = 0 and x1, x2 ≥ 0}.

Used without proof: the Background B result for fine P (taken as the black box).

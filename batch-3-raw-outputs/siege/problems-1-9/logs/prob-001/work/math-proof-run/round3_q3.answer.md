## Checks of Background B (P fine)
- **Step 0.** A map P[-x]^sharp -> Q under P exists iff f(x) = 0, and it is then unique. A face of a fine monoid is generated by finitely many generators, so it is the face of their sum x_Q. So b(Q) = P[-x_Q]^sharp is terminal in the comma category, and Lan F(Q) = F(Phi_Q). Correct.
- **Step 1.**
  - A fine, saturated, sharp monoid Q has a strictly positive functional: its dual cone is full-dimensional, so it has an interior point.
  - Pushouts: given w in the intersection of the T_i, pick lifts w_i : Q_i -> R_{>=0}. These lifts all restrict to w on Q. Because R_{>=0} is integral, saturated and sharp, the universal property of the pushout in this category gives a map from Q_sigma. So the image of Q_sigma^v_R is exactly the intersection T_sigma. Correct.
- **Step 3.**
  - Hom(F, I_{tau,A}) = Hom(F(tau), A). A natural transformation has components F(b) -> A for faces b contained in tau. Naturality forces each component to be F(b) -> F(tau) -> A. Correct.
  - The equivalence "Phi_{Q_sigma} is contained in Phi_tau iff T_sigma meets C":
    - (<=) If w is in T_sigma and in C, then Phi_{Q_sigma} lies in ker(w) on P, which lies in Phi_tau.
    - (=>) A relative-interior point w of T_sigma has kernel on P exactly Phi_{Q_sigma}, so w lies in C.
  - Correct.
- **Step 4.**
  - Each witness lies in some T_sigma with sigma in N_tau, so it lies in C. C is convex, so D is a compact convex subset of C.
  - The nerve of {T_i cap D} is N_tau(J'):
    - If sigma is in N_tau(J'), its witness lies in T_sigma cap D, so that set is nonempty.
    - Conversely, T_sigma cap D lies inside T_sigma cap C, so if it is nonempty then sigma is in N_tau.
  - The sets T_i cap D with i in J_0 cover D, because J_0 is contained in J'.
  - The nerve theorem for finite covers by compact convex sets then applies. Correct.
- **Steps 5–6.**
  - Pushouts of finitely presented objects are finitely presented, so the class B of f.p. objects is closed under fibre products in A_P^op. Covers of objects in B consist of objects in B. So Cartan's criterion applies on B.
  - Each P[-x]^sharp is f.p. Hence R^p pi_* is computed by the H^p(b, -) with b in B.
  - I found no gap.

**Conclusion for fine P:** YES, modulo the cited theorems and Lemma K. Lemma K relies on quantifier elimination for ordered divisible abelian groups, which I accept from memory; I did not check the reduction of "v factors through Q_i" to a finite linear system in detail.

## The counterexample hunt (non-finitely-generated P)
1. **Sign splits cannot give a disconnected N_{0}.** Let a be in P^gp and suppose injective valuations exist with v(a) > 0 and with v(a) < 0. Then the order-extension argument gives an injective valuation with v(a) = 0, or a coarsening that kills a. Either way the overlap sat(P + <a, -a>) has kernel face {0} in P. So the two vertices are joined (as already noted in the task).
2. **Splits along a segment ("t < 1" versus "t >= 1") are not available as covers.** For a valuation v sitting at the boundary point t = 1, a member that contains only the closed side behaves like the closed charts of R3. The test monoid M = {x : v(x) > 0} cup {0} (Lemma A) shows that such a member is not birhizomic at v: in rank 1 by Proposition B, and in higher rank by the same proof, since Lemma A does not use rank one. So v must factor birhizomically through a member whose locus contains an open-cone-type neighbourhood of v. This is the mechanism that made the nerves in the fine case behave like nerves of convex sets.
   - **Status of the general claim:** this is a heuristic, not proved. The precise claim I could not prove is: *every member Q_i that is birhizomic at v has a valuation locus E_i that contains every valuation w for which w|_{supp} ≺-dominates v (an "openness under ≺" property).* If this holds, a refinement argument suggests the nerves in Background B are contractible for every P.
3. **The specific candidates suggested in the task** (V × N, the Z^3 example, Q_{>=0}^2, N^(∞)): I built no covering that I could verify to be birhizomic and that also has a disconnected N_tau. For each attempted split, Lemma A rules out the closed-side member at the boundary valuation.

Open question attempted: ledger entries 1 (headline goal) and 7: proving that an explicit Dehn twist (power) acts on π_0(D_f) with an infinite orbit. Below is a planner's sketch. This query asks for an INDEPENDENT, more structural proof of the core criterion, plus a second family of answers, so that the final document does not depend on one formulation.

Task.
(1) Covering-space formulation. Prove that Ψ = (i_*, f^*) maps Teich(B) onto U := {(p,q) ∈ H×H : q ∉ Λ(p)}, where Λ(p) ⊂ H is the set of lifts under λ of the 7 points of C∖{0,1} that are images of the w-values of Step 1 lying over a_0, a_2, a_3 and of w = 1−t (t = λ(p)), i.e. of 1 ± √t, ±√(1−t), the roots of w² + 2(t−1)w + 1 − t, and 1 − t; and that Ψ: Teich(B) → U is a covering map (in fact the universal covering, with deck group H = {φ ∈ F : f^*∘φ = f^*}). Prove that these 7 points are pairwise distinct and distinct from 0, 1 for t ∈ C∖{0,1}, so that U → H is a fibration. Deduce: π_0(D_f) ≅ π_1(U)/K where K is the image of π_1(Δ ∩ U), Δ the diagonal.
(2) The general criterion. Each point of Λ(p) moves along a "graph" (a connected component of the preimage in H×H of one of the algebraic curves {(t, q_i(t))}). Let S be the set of graphs that meet Δ. Prove: K lies in the normal closure of the meridians of the graphs in S (fill in those graphs: Δ becomes contractible in the complement of the remaining graphs), hence for every element T ∈ π_1(U) that is a conjugate of a nonzero power of the meridian of a graph NOT in S, no nonzero power of T lies in K, so ⟨T⟩ has an infinite orbit on π_0(D_f). Identify which elements of π_1(U) are Dehn twists (or twist powers) of curves in (S², B): twists about curves around {a_1, v} with the 3/0 split ↔ meridians of graphs of the curve q = 1 − t (graphs of Möbius maps g ∈ Γ(2)S; in S iff g elliptic); squares of twists about curves around {v, a_j}, j ∈ {0,2,3} ↔ meridians of graphs of the other three curves.
(3) Second family (k = 2). Using (2), exhibit an explicit curve γ' around {v, a_j} for some j ∈ {0,2,3} (at t0 = 1/4) such that T_{γ'}² preserves D_f and has an infinite orbit: you must decide which graphs of, say, the curve (q−1)² = t (points over a_0) meet the diagonal (they do so over the roots of t² − 3t + 1), find a graph that does not, and translate its meridian back into an explicit arc from v to a_j in the dynamical plane via the map V_{1/4} of Step 1. Prove everything.
(4) Report clearly which steps of the planner's sketch you confirmed, which you corrected, and give a complete written proof of whatever you establish.

## Proposed complete solution (planner's sketch — algebra machine-checked, but every step must still be checked and written out rigorously)

Notation. a_0 = c_1, a_{i+1} = f(a_i) (indices mod 4), A = {a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3}, B = A ∪ {v}, v = f(c_2). The critical values are a_1 and v. i: PMod(S²,B) → PMod(S²,A) forgets v; its kernel F = π_1(S²∖A, v) (point pushes of v). For φ ∈ PMod(S²,B), a lift φ~ (f∘φ~ = φ∘f) always exists (both critical values are marked); f^*(φ·τ) = φ~·f^*(τ), i_*(φ·τ) = i(φ)·i_*(τ). Teich(A) ≅ upper half plane H; Teich(B) is a 2-dimensional complex cell, fibred over Teich(A) by i_* with fibre over p the Teichmüller space of the extra point v, which is the universal cover of S²∖A_p (A_p the configuration of A determined by p); point pushes act on that fibre as deck transformations (Birman).

Step 0 (explicit model; checked symbolically). Normalize a_0 = 0, a_1 = ∞, a_2 = 1, a_3 = t. Then f_t(z) = 1 + β/z + γ/z² with β = (t²+t−1)/(1−t), γ = t(1−2t)/(1−t); c_2 = −2γ/β; with D = 4t(1−2t)(1−t): v(t) = −(t²−3t+1)²/D, v−1 = −(t²+t−1)²/D, v−t = −(3t²−3t+1)²/D. The image W of D_f in M_{0,5} is {(0,∞,1,t,v(t))}, t ∈ C∖{0, 1, 1/2, roots of t²−3t+1, t²+t−1, 3t²−3t+1}. Base example t0 = 1/4: f(z) = 1 − 11/(12z) + 1/(6z²), c_2 = 4/11, v = −25/96, and on the real line v = −25/96 < a_0 = 0 < a_3 = 1/4 < a_2 = 1 (a_1 = ∞).

Step 1 (fibrewise formula for f^*). Fix t ∈ C∖{0,1} (the A-configuration (0,∞,1,t)) and let the fifth point v' range over C∖{0,1,t}. The degree-2 cover F(z) = v' + z² has critical points ∞ ↦ ∞ = a_1 and 0 ↦ v'. The pulled-back marked points are c_1 = ∞ (playing a_0), a_1' ∈ F^{-1}(1) = {±s_2}, a_2' ∈ F^{-1}(t) = {±s_3}, a_3' ∈ F^{-1}(0) = {±s_0}, where s_0² = −v', s_2² = 1−v', s_3² = t−v'. The pulled-back modulus of A, in the same normalization (a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3) ↦ (0,∞,1,w), is w = (s_3 − s_2)/(s_0 − s_2) (Möbius map z ↦ (s_3−s_2)/(z−s_2)). Putting u = s_2 − s_0 (so s_2 + s_0 = 1/u) and U = u², one finds s_3 = s_2 − wu and then U = (w+t−1)/(w(w−1)), v' = −(1−U)²/(4U), i.e.
   V_t(w) := v' = −((w−1)² − t)² / (4 w (w−1)(w+t−1)).
(Checked with sympy: V_t(t) = v(t), i.e. the dynamical point has w = t; numerically, for random complex t, w, some sign choice of (s_0,s_2,s_3) for v' = V_t(w) gives cross-ratio exactly w.) V_t: P¹_w → P¹_v has degree 4 and is Galois with deck group {id, g_0, g_2, g_3} ≅ (Z/2)², g_0(w) = (w+t−1)/(w−1), g_3(w) = (1−t)(w−1)/(w+t−1), g_2 = g_0∘g_3 (these correspond to changing the signs of s_0, s_3, s_2). Fibres: V_t^{-1}(0) = {1 ± √t} (each double), V_t^{-1}(1) = {±√(1−t)} (double), V_t^{-1}(t) = roots of w² + 2(t−1)w + 1 − t (double), V_t^{-1}(∞) = {0, 1, ∞, 1−t} (simple; also V_t−1 = −(w²+t−1)²/(4w(w−1)(w+t−1)) and V_t − t = −(w²+2(t−1)w+1−t)²/(4w(w−1)(w+t−1))). So the w-sphere minus these 10 points is a 4-sheeted unbranched cover of S²∖A_t, the sheet records which preimages are marked, and the pulled-back modulus is the coordinate w itself. The three points 0, 1, ∞ of the w-sphere lie over a_1 = ∞ and are where w hits the punctures of M_{0,4}; the fourth point over a_1 is w = 1−t.
Key consequence: if v' ≠ a_1 then w ≠ 1−t. Hence for every τ' ∈ Teich(B), with t' = π(i_*τ'): π(f^*τ') ≠ 1 − t'.

Step 2 (the map Ψ and the graphs). Let π: H → C∖{0,1} be the universal covering given by the moduli coordinate; one may take π = λ, the modular lambda function, with λ(τ+2) = λ(τ), λ(−1/τ) = 1 − λ(τ), λ maps the positive imaginary axis decreasingly onto (0,1), λ → 0 as τ → i∞; deck group Γ(2)/±1. Put Ψ := (i_*, f^*): Teich(B) → H × H. By definition D_f = Ψ^{-1}(Δ), Δ = diagonal. The preimage in H × H of {(t, 1−t)} is the union of the graphs Γ_g = {(p, g(p))}, g ranging over the coset Γ(2)·S (S(τ) = −1/τ), all Möbius maps; distinct graphs are disjoint (Γ(2)/±1 acts freely on H). By Step 1, Ψ(Teich(B)) is disjoint from every Γ_g. Moreover Γ_g ∩ Δ ≠ ∅ iff g has a fixed point in H iff g is elliptic.

Step 3 (linking number). For g ∈ Γ(2)S non-elliptic, X_g := (H×H)∖Γ_g ≅ H × (H∖{point}) (via (p,q) ↦ (p, (q − Re g(p))/Im g(p))), so H_1(X_g) ≅ Z, generated by a small loop around g(p) in a fibre {p}×H, and Δ ⊂ X_g is contractible. Let T ∈ PMod(S²,B) with i(T) = 1 and f^*∘T = f^*; then Ψ∘T = Ψ and T preserves D_f. Let τ ∈ D_f, p0 := i_*τ = f^*τ, D_0 the component of D_f through τ. Suppose T^m D_0 = D_0, m ≠ 0. Take a path σ in D_0 from τ to T^mτ: Ψ∘σ is a loop in Δ ⊂ X_g, hence null-homologous in X_g. Take a path σ' in the fibre i_*^{-1}(p0) from τ to T^mτ. Teich(B) is contractible, so σ ≃ σ' rel endpoints, hence Ψσ ≃ Ψσ' rel endpoints inside Ψ(Teich(B)) ⊂ X_g. Ψσ' = (p0, f^*∘σ') is a loop in the fibre; its class in H_1(X_g) is the winding number of f^*∘σ' around g(p0). If this winding number is ±m, contradiction. So: the ⟨T⟩-orbit of D_0 is infinite provided the fibre loop of T winds ±1 around g(p0) for a non-elliptic g.

Step 4 (the curve). On the fibre over p0 (≅ universal cover of S²∖A_{t0}, the position of v'), f^* is the lift through π, normalized by f^*(τ) = p0, of the map (point of the universal cover) ↦ w-coordinate of its image in the 4-sheeted cover of Step 1, where τ sits over w = t0. Let α be a simple arc from v to a_1 in S² with interior in S²∖B, c = ∂(regular neighbourhood of α) (a simple closed curve enclosing exactly v and a_1), T = T_c^{±1} = point push of v around the lasso η of α. Let β be the lift of α to the w-sphere starting at w = t0 (V_{t0}∘β = α). If β ends at w = 1−t0, then the fibre loop f^*∘σ' for T^m is the lift from p0 of (β · small circle around 1−t0 · β^{-1})^{±m}, which is closed, and it winds ±m around q* := (endpoint of the lift of β starting at p0) = g(p0) for a unique g ∈ Γ(2)S. Closedness for m = 1 means f^*∘T = f^* on the fibre, so the lift T~ acts trivially on Teich(A), so T~ is trivial in PMod(S²,A) = i(T), i.e. T preserves D_f (consistent with the known criterion: the outer preimage disks of the complement of the disk bounded by c contain a_1, a_2, a_3 on one side — the "3/0 split").
Choice at t0 = 1/4, with p0 = i·y0 on the imaginary axis: let β be homotopic in C∖{0,1} (rel endpoints) to (a loop around 0 based at 1/4) followed by the segment [1/4, 3/4]. The loop lifts to a path from p0 to p0 ± 2, the segment lifts along the imaginary axis to S(p0) = i/y0, so q* = T^{±2}S(p0) (in this formula T denotes the translation τ ↦ τ+1, not a twist) with g = T^{±2}S = [[±2, −1],[1, 0]], trace ±2: parabolic, not elliptic. Hence the orbit is infinite. (For comparison: the straight segment alone gives g = S, elliptic with fixed point i over t = 1/2 — that is the cusp curve of the puncture t = 1/2 of W, whose twist stabilizes a component.)
Explicit β at t0 = 1/4: lower half of the circle |w| = 1/4 from 1/4 to −1/4; then upper half of |w − 5/32| = 13/32 from −1/4 to 9/16; then lower half of |w − 21/32| = 3/32 from 9/16 to 3/4. It avoids all points of V^{-1}(B): {0, 1, ∞, 3/4, 1/2, 3/2, ±√3/2, 3/4 ± (√3/4)i} and V^{-1}(v) = {1/4, 2/3, 9/8, 3}. Numerically β is disjoint from g_0(β), g_2(β), g_3(β) (minimum distance ≥ 0.05; for t = 1/4: g_0(w) = (w − 3/4)/(w − 1), g_3(w) = (3/4)(w−1)/(w − 3/4)), so α = V_{1/4}∘β is a simple arc. Numerically α starts at v = −25/96, runs in the lower half-plane, crosses the real axis upward at ≈ 1.378 ∈ (a_2, a_1) = (1, ∞), runs in the upper half-plane, crosses downward at ≈ 0.088 ∈ (a_0, a_3) = (0, 1/4), runs in the lower half-plane, crosses upward at ≈ −0.019 ∈ (v, a_0), then stays in the upper half-plane and reaches a_1 = ∞ (along the direction +i∞).
Numerical sanity checks: f^{-1}(α) is a closed curve through c_1 = 0 and c_2 = 4/11 with a_1 = ∞, a_2 = 1, a_3 = 1/4 all on the same side (3/0 split, so T_c preserves D_f); the "real" arc from v to ∞ along (−∞, v] gives a 2/1 split (no power of its twist preserves D_f); the arc for the straight segment (g = S) gives a 3/0 curve (the cusp curve).

Proposed answer. For f = f_{1/4} (real points v = −25/96 < 0 = c_1 < 1/4 = f³(c_1) < 1 = f²(c_1), f(c_1) = ∞), γ is the boundary of a thin neighbourhood of the arc α just described; T_γ itself (power 1) preserves D_f and the ⟨T_γ⟩-orbit of the component of D_f containing the base point is infinite. For any other f in the family one transports γ along a path in W (any such transport conjugates by an element of the stabilizer of D_f, which preserves the property).

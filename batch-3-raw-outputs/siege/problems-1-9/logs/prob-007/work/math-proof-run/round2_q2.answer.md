# round2_q2 — partial answer

Status: the main goal is NOT settled. Below I separate what I checked from what is still open.

## Checked (short arguments)

A. Curves around {v, a_j}, j in {0,2,3} (item 5). Let Delta be a disk with Delta ∩ B = {v, a_j}. It contains exactly one critical value, v, so f^{-1}(Delta) is a single disk mapped 2:1. That disk meets A only in a_{j-1}, so its boundary is peripheral rel A. Hence T_gamma^2 lifts to a map that is trivial rel A. Also i(T_gamma) = 1. So T_gamma^2 ∈ G for every such curve. Explicit curve: for real t in (0, 1/2), the points are ordered on the real line. One can take gamma to be the boundary of a thin neighbourhood of the real segment joining v(t) to the nearest point of A, provided that segment contains no other marked point.

B. The quotient tests in item (8) cannot separate these curves.
- In F/<<l_1>> ≅ F_2, the image of K lies in the normal closure of {l_0^2, l_2^2, l_3^2}. The image of T_gamma^{2n} is a conjugate of l_j^{2n}, which also lies in that normal closure. So this quotient gives no obstruction.
- In Q = Z/2*Z/2*Z/2, the image of T_gamma^2 is trivial. So Q gives no obstruction either.

Any invariant that works must use the actual G_0-orbit structure of the generators of K, not only their normal closures.

C. A curve gamma around {a_1, v} with a 3/0 split has T_gamma ∈ G (item 6). In Q, its image is a parabolic conjugate of l_1. The image of K in Q is generated by G_0-translates of x_{1/2}. To show the orbit is infinite, one would need to show that the G_0-orbit of x_{1/2} in Q never generates a nonzero power of the parabolic element given by T_gamma. I did not complete this.

## Proposed answer (conjectural, not proved)

Take gamma to be a simple closed curve around {v, a_j} as in A, chosen not to be one of the six cusp curves of item (4) up to G_0-conjugacy. For example, take the curve around v and a_0 whose arc passes once around a_2. Take k = 2. Then T_gamma^2 preserves D_f (proved, part A). The claim that its orbit on pi_0(D_f) is infinite is NOT proved here.

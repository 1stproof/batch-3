Open question attempted: the "preserves D_f" half of the headline goal and the identification of candidate curves — determine exactly which Dehn twist powers T_γ^k (γ a simple closed curve in S^2 minus B) lie in the group G of mapping classes preserving D_f, and which of them have a power in the stabilizer G_0 of a component.

Task. Fix a concrete map f in the family (for instance a real quadratic map, or one whose 4-cycle combinatorics you can draw — describe it precisely) and compute the pullback of curves under f:
(a) For every essential simple closed curve γ in S^2∖B of "small" complexity — in particular all curves surrounding exactly two points of B, described by arcs joining those two points — compute f^{-1}(γ), the degrees of its components, which components are essential/peripheral rel A, and the lift of T_γ or T_γ^2 as an element of PMod(S^2,A). Determine the k ≥ 1 (if any) with T_γ^k ∈ G, i.e. lift = i(T_γ^k) in PMod(S^2,A). Prove the lifting criterion you use (including the condition that the lift fixes A pointwise).
(b) Explain which of these twists arise (up to power) as monodromies of small loops around punctures of the curve W of conjugacy classes (punctures where f(c_2) lands on the cycle, or where the maps degenerate), since those have FINITE orbits on components.
(c) Identify a curve γ with T_γ^k ∈ G that is not of that kind, and attempt to prove that no nonzero power of T_γ^k stabilizes a component of D_f (e.g. via an invariant of components such as a homomorphism from G that kills G_0 but not T_γ^k, or via the action on the fibers of i_* restricted to D_f).
Give complete proofs of whatever you can establish and flag clearly anything left unproved.

## Notation and background (shared context; claims marked "heuristic" are NOT verified)

Write the 4-cycle as a_0 = c_1, a_1 = f(c_1), a_2, a_3 (with f(a_3) = a_0), so A = {a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3}, B = A ∪ {v}, the critical values are a_1 and v, and Teich(B) has complex dimension 2, Teich(A) dimension 1, D_f dimension 1 (by Epstein transversality D_f is a smooth 1-dimensional complex submanifold).

Framework (standard, but you must justify anything you use):
* PMod(S^2,B) acts on Teich(B) by changing the marking. If φ ∈ PMod(S^2,B) has a lift φ~ (f∘φ~ = φ∘f) fixing A pointwise, then f^*(φ·τ) = φ~·f^*(τ), and i_*(φ·τ) = i(φ)·i_*(τ), where i : PMod(S^2,B) → PMod(S^2,A) forgets v. Hence if φ~ = i(φ) in PMod(S^2,A), then φ preserves D_f. Let G be the group of such φ (the "liftable-and-compatible" subgroup).
* Let W be the image of D_f in moduli space M_{0,5} = Teich(B)/PMod(S^2,B) (the curve of conjugacy classes of quadratic maps with a marked critical 4-cycle and marked other critical value v, v not in the cycle). If τ_1, τ_2 ∈ D_f lie over the same point of W then τ_2 = φ·τ_1 with φ ∈ G. So G acts transitively on fibers of D_f → W; if D_0 is a component and G_0 ⊂ G its stabilizer, then (when W is connected) π_0(D_f) ≅ G/G_0, and the ⟨φ⟩-orbit of D_0 is infinite iff no nonzero power of φ lies in G_0.
* PMod(S^2,B) is torsion free and acts freely, so D_0 → W is a covering and G_0 is the image of π_1(W) → π_1(M_{0,5}) = PMod(S^2,B) (monodromy of the inclusion W ⊂ M_{0,5}), up to conjugacy depending on base points.

Lifting computations (heuristic, check them):
* If γ surrounds v and a_j with j ∈ {0,2,3} (one critical value inside), then f^{-1}(γ) is a single curve δ mapping with degree 2, bounding a disk containing c_2, a_{j-1} and the other preimage a'_{j-1} of a_j, so rel A, δ is peripheral; also i(γ) is peripheral. Then T_γ^2 lifts to T_δ, which is trivial in PMod(S^2,A), so T_γ^2 ∈ G.
* BUT: W has punctures where f(c_2) = a_j (c_2 strictly preperiodic onto the cycle). A small loop in W around such a puncture has monodromy T_γ^m for a curve γ around {v,a_j} (the "short" one in that degenerating configuration, transported to the base point), m = local order of contact. So for those particular curves a power of T_γ lies in G_0 and the orbit is FINITE. So the answer curve cannot be one of those; it must be either a curve around {v,a_j} drawn along a "different" arc, or a curve around two cycle points, or around {v,a_1} (both critical values: f^{-1}(γ) is then two curves each mapping with degree 1, bounding an annulus containing c_1, c_2).
* A possible obstruction to T_γ^n ∈ G_0: abelianization. H_1(M_{0,5};Z) ≅ Z^5, spanned by the classes e_{xy} of twists about curves surrounding two marked points {x,y}. If the image of H_1(W) in H_1(M_{0,5}) ⊗ Q does not contain the class of T_γ, no power of T_γ lies in G_0. (Whether W has genus 0, its punctures, and where it meets the boundary divisors of M̄_{0,5} must then be computed.) Other possible obstructions: non-abelian quotients, or geometry (a parabolic element of G_0 acting on the disk D_0 must come from a cusp of W).
* This setup appears in the literature (Hironaka–Koch, "A disconnected deformation space of rational maps"), but do not rely on recollection: give a self-contained argument.

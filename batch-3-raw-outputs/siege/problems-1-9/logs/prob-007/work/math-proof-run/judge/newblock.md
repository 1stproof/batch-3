**Independent confirmation that $[\tilde T]=1$ (the "3/0 split").** This gives a second, purely topological proof of $f^*\circ T_\gamma=f^*$, independent of the fibre computation of §7.

- Put $s=1/z-\tfrac{11}4$. Then $f=v+s^2/6$: indeed $v+\tfrac16(1/z-\tfrac{11}4)^2=v+\tfrac{121}{96}-\tfrac{11}{12z}+\tfrac1{6z^2}$ and $v+\tfrac{121}{96}=1$. The critical points are $s=0$ ($z=c_2$, over $v$) and $s=\infty$ ($z=0=a_0$, over $a_1=\infty$).
- The points of $A$: $s(a_0)=\infty$, $s(a_1)=-\tfrac{11}4$, $s(a_2)=-\tfrac74$, $s(a_3)=\tfrac54$. Their images $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_0$... are irrelevant here; what matters is that the positive numbers $\tfrac54<\tfrac74<\tfrac{11}4$ satisfy $v+s^2/6=0,\tfrac14,1$ respectively.
- Put $Y=f-v=s^2/6$. The arc $\alpha$ meets $\hat{\mathbb R}$, apart from its endpoints, only at $x_1,x_2,x_3>v$, i.e. at positive $Y$. So the interior of $\alpha$ lies in $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$ in the $Y$-coordinate, where $s=\sqrt{6Y}$ has a branch with $\operatorname{Re}s>0$. Hence $f^{-1}(\alpha)=\alpha_+\cup(-\alpha_+)$ with $\alpha_+\subset\{\operatorname{Re}s>0\}$ a simple arc from $s=0$ to $s=\infty$, and $J=f^{-1}(\alpha)$ is a Jordan curve through $0$ and $\infty$. The involution $s\mapsto-s$ preserves $J$ and exchanges its two complementary discs $\Omega,\Omega'$ (it is a rotation by $\pi$ about the two fixed points $0,\infty\in J$).
- $J$ meets the positive real $s$-axis only in $\alpha_+$, at the three transversal crossings $s_i=\sqrt{6(x_i-v)}$, and $x_3<0<x_2<\tfrac14<1<x_1$ gives $s_3<\tfrac54<s_2<\tfrac74<\tfrac{11}4<s_1$. So $\tfrac54$ and $\tfrac74$ lie on opposite sides of $J$, and $\tfrac74,\tfrac{11}4$ on the same side. Say $\tfrac54\in\Omega$; then $\tfrac74,\tfrac{11}4\in\Omega'$, so $-\tfrac74,-\tfrac{11}4\in\Omega$.
- Hence $a_1,a_2,a_3\in\Omega$ and $a_0\in J$. Now $f^{-1}(N(\alpha))$ is a regular neighbourhood of $J$, and the collars $R_1,R_2$ of Lemma 2.3 are parallel to its two boundary curves $\gamma_1\subset\Omega$, $\gamma_2\subset\Omega'$. The curve $\gamma_1$ separates $\{a_1,a_2,a_3\}$ from $\{a_0\}$, so it is peripheral in $(S^2,A)$; $\gamma_2$ bounds a disc in $\Omega'$ containing no point of $A$. Twists about such curves are trivial in $\mathrm{PMod}(S^2,A)$, so $[\tilde T]=[T_{\gamma_1}T_{\gamma_2}]=1$, i.e. $f^*\circ T_\gamma=f^*$.

(Numerically, lifting the concrete arc through $f$ along both branches from $c_2$ gives a bounded closed curve with winding number $0$ about $1$ and about $\tfrac14$, consistent with $a_1=\infty,a_2,a_3$ lying on one side.)

## 10. Remarks (not used in the proof)

**A heuristic mapping-class description of $\gamma$ (not proved).** Let $c$ be the round circle $|z-\tfrac12|=\tfrac35$, which surrounds $c_1=0,\ f^3(c_1)=\tfrac14,\ f^2(c_1)=1$ but not $v$ or $\infty$, and let $\gamma_0=\partial N(\alpha_0)$, where $\alpha_0$ leaves $v$ into the upper half-plane, crosses $\mathbb R$ downward in $(0,\tfrac14)$ and then runs in the lower half-plane to $\infty$. Topologically $\alpha\simeq L\cdot\alpha_0$ as arcs from $v$ to the puncture $a_1$, where $L$ is a loop at $v$ around $\{0,\tfrac14,1\}$ whose regular neighbourhood has $c$ as its non-trivial boundary curve; with the push formula this suggests $\gamma=T_c^{\mp1}(\gamma_0)$, the sign depending on twist and push conventions which I have not pinned down. The same kind of computation suggests that $T_{\gamma_0}$ also preserves $\mathcal D_f$ but corresponds to the elliptic element $S$ (so the linking argument gives nothing for it), and that $T_c$ alone does not preserve $\mathcal D_f$. None of these statements is proved here and none is used.

## Other routes

- **Curves around $\{v,a_j\}$, $j\in\{0,2,3\}$.** For such a curve the lift of $T_\gamma^2$ is a twist about a curve peripheral rel $A$, and $T_\gamma^2$ projects trivially to $\mathrm{PMod}(S^2,A)$, so $T_\gamma^2$ preserves $\mathcal D_f$. No proof of an infinite orbit was found for these curves; the quotients of $\pi_1(S^2\setminus A)$ that were tried (killing a loop around $a_1$, or the squares of loops around $a_0,a_2,a_3$) provably cannot detect it.
- **Curves around the two critical values $\{a_1,v\}$.** By Lemma 2.3-type reasoning, $T_\gamma$ preserves $\mathcal D_f$ exactly when the two preimage discs of the outside of $\gamma$ split $\{a_1,a_2,a_3\}$ as $3/0$; with a $2/1$ split no nonzero power preserves $\mathcal D_f$ via the lifting criterion. Among $3/0$ curves some have finite orbits: the monodromy around the puncture $t=\tfrac12$ of $W$ (where $v$ collides with $a_1$) is a single twist about such a curve, lying in a component stabiliser. The curve of the Theorem is distinguished by the parabolic element $g=T^{-2}S$ in the linking criterion; a $3/0$ curve whose element is elliptic (such as $S$) is not detected by this criterion.
- **Abelian obstructions fail.** The image of $H_1(W)$ in $H_1(\mathcal M_{0,5})\cong\mathbb Z^5$ has full rank, so no homological invariant of $\mathcal M_{0,5}$ can show an orbit is infinite; the linking criterion of §5 instead uses the non-abelian information carried by the pair of maps $(i_*,f^*)$ into $\mathbb H\times\mathbb H$ and the forbidden graph of $g$.
- **Forgetting $a_1$.** The forgetful map $W\to\mathcal M_{0,4}(a_0,a_2,a_3,v)$ is $u(t)=(1-t)(t^2-3t+1)^2/(3t^2-3t+1)^2$, with $u-1=-t(t^2+t-1)^2/(3t^2-3t+1)^2$; it was not needed.

## Status

**Main claim: fully proved** (modulo the standard facts listed below). The main claim is the Theorem: for $f=f_{1/4}$, the explicit curve $\gamma=\partial N(\alpha)$ — the boundary of a thin neighbourhood of an arc $\alpha$ from $v$ to $f(c_1)$ with the stated crossing pattern with $\hat{\mathbb R}$ (concretely, three semicircles and a vertical ray) — is a simple closed curve in $S^2\setminus B$ enclosing exactly $v$ and $f(c_1)$; the Dehn twist $T_\gamma$ itself (power $1$) satisfies $f^*\circ T_\gamma=f^*$ and $i_*\circ T_\gamma=i_*$, hence preserves $\mathcal D_f$; and every orbit of $\langle T_\gamma\rangle$ on $\pi_0(\mathcal D_f)$ is infinite. For an arbitrary $f$ in the family, the transported curve $M^{-1}h_1(\gamma)$ has the same properties, with a picture-level description (crossing pattern with a circle through $B$) when the normal-form parameter $t$ lies in $(0,\tfrac{3-\sqrt5}2)$.

What the proof relies on without proof:
1. The standard facts (F1)–(F5): the modular function $\lambda$ and its transformation laws; Teichmüller spaces are contractible with free properly discontinuous mapping class group action; the Bers fibration with point-pushes acting as deck transformations on the fibres; the push formula $\mathrm{Push}(\delta)=T_{\delta_L}T_{\delta_R}^{-1}$ (used only up to sign, and the argument is symmetric under $m\mapsto-m$); continuity of $f^*$.

Scope caveats (not gaps in the main claim):
2. For non-real $t$ the curve is given as $M^{-1}h_1(\gamma)$ with $h_1$ the explicit isotopy of Lemma 9.1 along a chosen path in $W$; every choice of path works, but I give no path-independent picture.
3. The mapping-class description in §10 is heuristic and unproved; it is not used.

## 1. Definitions, conventions, citations

### 1.1 The skein algebras

**Stated skein algebra.** S_n(Σ) is the stated SL(n)-skein algebra of Lê–Sikora (arXiv 2201.00045).
- It is spanned by stated n-webs.
- Relations: the crossing relation q^{1/n}(pos) − q^{-1/n}(neg) = (q−q^{-1})(smoothing), the kink and unknot relations, the sink–source relation, and the boundary relations, including the height exchange (S3).
- The product αβ is α stacked above β.
- On each boundary edge, the heights of the endpoints increase along the positive orientation of the boundary, with the surface on the left.

**Reduced algebra.** S̄_n(Σ) = S_n(Σ)/(bad arcs), following Lê–Yu. A bad arc is a corner arc at an ideal boundary point whose states are in the bad order.

**The punctured bigon.**
- B^* is the punctured bigon: ideal vertices v_t (top) and v_b (bottom), left edge e_l, right edge e_r, and an interior puncture p.
- b_ij is the arc from e_l (state i) to e_r (state j) passing above p. It is a corner arc at v_t.
- c_ij is the same arc passing below p. It is a corner arc at v_b.
- B, C denote the matrices (b_ij), (c_ij).

**Embeddings of the bigon.** φ_top, φ_bot : 𝔅 → B^* are embeddings of the (unpunctured) bigon onto neighbourhoods of the part of B^* above, resp. below, p.
- Each is edge-preserving and sends the bigon arc u_ij to b_ij, resp. c_ij.
- They induce algebra maps on stated skein algebras, since all relations and stacking are local.

### 1.2 Cited facts (from memory; statements as used)

- **(S1) [Lê–Sikora].** S_n(𝔅) ≅ O_q(SL_n) as algebras, with u_ij ↦ x_ij after the normalisation fixed in LS. In particular:
  - the u_ij generate;
  - U = (u_ij) satisfies R U_1U_2 = U_2U_1R, with R as in Theorem D;
  - det_q U = Σ_σ (−q)^{ℓ(σ)} u_{1σ(1)}…u_{nσ(n)} = 1.

  If the LS normalisation produces R^{-1} or R_21^{-1} instead, nothing below changes: for a single matrix these RTT relations are equivalent (Hecke relation).
- **(S2) [splitting/decomposition; GAP-2].** The linear map O_q(SL_n) ⊗ O_q(SL_n) → S_n(B^*), x⊗y ↦ φ_top(x)φ_bot(y), is a linear isomorphism.
  - From memory, this is the "punctured bigon = braided tensor product" statement. It appears in Costantino–Lê for n = 2 and in Lê–Sikora / Higgins for general n, obtained by cutting B^* along ideal arcs v_t–p and p–v_b into two ideal triangles and using the triangle decomposition.
  - I have not reproduced its proof.
- **(S3) [height exchange; GAP-1].** Let two endpoints with states i, k sit consecutively on one boundary edge.
  - Swapping their heights expresses the diagram as Σ_{i',k'} H^{ik}_{i'k'}·(diagram with original height order and states i',k').
  - The matrix H is invertible and universal: it depends only on the orientations of the two strands at that edge, not on the rest of the diagram.
  - Up to a power q^{±1/n} and a permutation of tensor factors, H is one of R^{±1}, R_21^{±1}.
- **(S4) [Lê–Yu; GAP-3 for reversed arcs].**
  - A corner arc at an ideal vertex with states (i, j) is bad when its states are in the order specified by Lê–Yu.
  - For our oriented arcs, this makes a_ij bad exactly in one strict triangle (i<j or i>j), the triangle being opposite at v_t and at v_b because the corner arcs at v_t and v_b approach the edges from opposite sides.
  - Corner arcs with the opposite orientation (e_r → e_l) equal, up to invertible scalars, entries of S(U) under (S1), where S is the antipode. Bad reversed arcs correspond to bad entries.

## 2. Skein side

### 2.1 Generation
- S_n(B^*) is generated by the b_ij and c_ij. This follows from (S2) and (S1), since O_q(SL_n) is generated by the x_ij.
- Hence the same holds for S̄_n(B^*).

### 2.2 RTT relations
- φ_top and φ_bot are algebra maps.
- So by (S1): R B_1B_2 = B_2B_1R, R C_1C_2 = C_2C_1R, det_q B = det_q C = 1.

### 2.3 Mixed relation

**The non-crossing diagram D.** Draw b_ij above p and c_kl below p, disjoint. Call this planar diagram D^{ij}_{kl}. Compare its endpoint order on each edge with the height order.
- **On e_r.** The positive boundary orientation goes upward. The b-endpoint is nearer v_t, so it is higher in height. This matches the stacking order "b above c", i.e. the product bc.
- **On e_l.** The orientation goes downward. The c-endpoint is higher in height, which matches cb.

**Expressing bc and cb through D.** Neither product equals D^{ij}_{kl}; each differs from it by one height exchange.
- b_ij c_kl differs from D^{ij}_{kl} by one height exchange at e_l. By (S3),
  b_ij c_kl = Σ H_l{}^{ik}_{i'k'} D^{i'j}_{k'l}.
- c_kl b_ij differs from D^{ij}_{kl} by one height exchange at e_r, so
  c_kl b_ij = Σ H_r{}^{jl}_{j'l'} D^{ij'}_{kl'}.

**Consequence.** Eliminating D (H_l is invertible) gives
  c_kl b_ij = Σ (H_r ∘ H_l^{-1})-coefficients · b_{i'j'} c_{k'l'}.

This has the form Y_2X_1 = A X_1Y_2 A' with X = B, Y = C and A, A' ∈ {scalars·R^{±1}, R_21^{±1}}. The scalar factors q^{±1/n} cancel, because H_l and H_r enter through H_r ∘ H_l^{-1}.

**Which form? (GAP-1.)** With the exact LS coefficients this is a direct substitution. I state the two possibilities consistent with the triangularity of §2.4.
- **Case (α), found by one round-2 worker:** R B_1C_2 = C_2B_1R, with B lower and C upper in S̄.
- **Case (β), found by the other:** R C_1B_2 = B_2C_1R, with C lower and B upper.
- (Variants with R^{-1} are reduced to these by inverting the matrices; see §6.)

**Why the other combinations are excluded.** If the pair (A, A') were incompatible with the bad-arc triangularity, then the robustness fact quoted in the query (the span of monomials containing a bad entry is stable under rewriting only in the compatible cases) shows the following. The bad-arc ideal would contain a relation forcing a diagonal monomial to vanish, contradicting (S2) together with the Lê–Yu result that the reduced algebra of a triangle/bigon is nonzero with the expected basis. I record this exclusion argument as heuristic: it relies on that nondegeneracy.

### 2.4 Bad arcs and the reduced algebra

Work in case (α); case (β) is symmetric. Write:
- J_- ⊂ O_q(SL_n) for the ideal generated by the x_ij with i<j, so that O/J_- = O_q(B_-) is lower triangular;
- J_+ for the ideal generated by the x_ij with i>j.

**The ideal.** By (S4), the bad-arc ideal 𝓘 is generated by φ_top(x_ij) (i<j), by φ_bot(x_ij) (i>j), and by the reversed bad arcs.
- J_± are Hopf ideals: they are the kernels of the Hopf surjections onto the Borel quotients. Hence S(J_±) ⊂ J_±.
- So, by GAP-3, the reversed bad arcs already lie in φ_top(J_-) + φ_bot(J_+).
- Therefore 𝓘 is the two-sided ideal generated by φ_top(J_-) and φ_bot(J_+).

**Claim.** 𝓘 = φ_top(J_-)φ_bot(O) + φ_top(O)φ_bot(J_+) =: 𝓘_0.

*Proof.* Clearly 𝓘_0 ⊂ 𝓘. Conversely, 𝓘_0 contains the generators, so it suffices that 𝓘_0 is a two-sided ideal.
1. **Left multiplication by φ_top(O).** 𝓘_0 is stable, since J_- is an ideal.
2. **Right multiplication by φ_bot(O).** 𝓘_0 is stable, since J_+ is an ideal.
3. **Moving a c past a b, or a b past a c.** The mixed relation rewrites c·b-words into b·c-words and back. By the robustness fact (compatible case: X = B lower, Y = C upper), the span of b·c-words containing a bad entry is preserved by these rewritings.
4. Since 𝓘_0 is the image under (S2) of exactly such words, 𝓘_0 is stable under multiplication by any generator on either side. ∎

**Consequence.** By (S2), S̄_n(B^*) = S_n(B^*)/𝓘_0 ≅ O_q(B_-) ⊗ O_q(B_+) linearly, via x⊗y ↦ b(x)c(y).

**Diagonal entries.**
- The triangular quantum determinant reduces to the diagonal product: in det_q every term with σ ≠ id contains an entry above the diagonal and one below it. So ∏ b_ii = ∏ c_ii = 1.
- The diagonal entries commute (Lemma 1.2 of the spliced proof). Hence each b_ii is invertible, with b_ii^{-1} = ∏_{j≠i} b_jj, and likewise for the c_ii.

### 2.5 Presentation

Let A_S be the algebra generated by a lower-triangular B and an upper-triangular C, with invertible diagonal entries, subject to:
- RTT for B and for C;
- ∏ b_ii = ∏ c_ii = 1;
- the mixed relation R B_1C_2 = C_2B_1R.

**Proposition.** A_S → S̄_n(B^*) is an isomorphism.

*Proof.*
- **Well defined.** By §2.2–2.4.
- **Surjective.** By §2.1.
- **Injective.** The mixed relation lets every word be rewritten as (B-monomial)(C-monomial). So A_S is spanned by O^-·O^+, using the PBW bases of O^∓ (Lemma 1.1/Theorem 1.4 of the spliced proof). Their images are linearly independent by §2.4. ∎

## 3. Double side (Theorem D)

As established in this run, and spliced in the query, with every step proved there:
1. Φ: U_q(b_+) ≅ O^+ and Φ^-: U_q(b_-) ≅ O^-.
2. σ(L^+, L^-) = q^{-1/n}R_21, so the cross relation (X) on matrix entries is R L^-_1L^+_2 = L^+_2L^-_1R.
3. By the closure lemma and the description of D as (U_+ * U_-)/(X), D ≅ A_D, the algebra presented by:
   - lower-triangular L^- and upper-triangular L^+;
   - RTT for each, with ∏ l^±_ii = 1;
   - R L^-_1L^+_2 = L^+_2L^-_1R.
4. D/⟨l^+_ii l^-_ii − 1⟩ ≅ U_q^{ext}(sl_n).

## 4. Part (1): the isomorphism

A_S and A_D have identical presentations (case (α)). Hence there is an isomorphism Θ: S̄_n(B^*) → U_q(b_-) ⋈ U_q(b_+) with Θ(b_ij) = l^-_ij and Θ(c_ij) = l^+_ij. Explicitly:

| skein generator | image in the double |
|---|---|
| b_ii | K̂'_i^{-1} |
| b_{i+1,i} | (q^{-1}−q) F_i K̂'_{i+1}^{-1} |
| b_ki, k>i | (q^{-1}−q) F_ki K̂'_k^{-1} |
| b_ij, i<j | 0 (bad arc) |
| c_ii | K̂_i |
| c_{i,i+1} | (q−q^{-1}) K̂_{i+1} E_i |
| c_ik, i<k | (q−q^{-1}) K̂_k E_ik |
| c_ij, i>j | 0 (bad arc) |

The inverse map is:
- K̂_i ↦ c_ii and K̂'_i ↦ b_ii^{-1};
- E_i ↦ (q−q^{-1})^{-1} c_{i+1,i+1}^{-1} c_{i,i+1};
- F_i ↦ (q^{-1}−q)^{-1} b_{i+1,i} b_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.

In case (β), interchange the roles: C ↦ L^-, B ↦ L^+.

## 5. Part (2)

**Theorem.** S̄_n(B^*)/I ≅ U_q^{ext}(sl_n).

*Proof.*
- Θ(b_ii c_ii) = K̂'_i^{-1} K̂_i (the two factors commute in D), so Θ(I) = ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩.
- By Theorem D(4), D/⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩ ≅ U_q^{ext}(sl_n).
- The composite sends E_i, F_i, K̂_i to the classes of the formulas above.

The same holds in case (β), and in the inverse-matrix variants, since there b_ii c_ii ↦ (K̂_i K̂'_i^{-1})^{±1}.

## 6. Conventions

**Height-exchange relation with R^{-1}.** If the LS height exchange comes with R^{-1}, so that the mixed relation reads R^{-1} X_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1 R^{-1} with X lower and Y upper, use the inverse matrices. The algebra-level statements are unchanged.
- The inverses (L^±)^{-1} are triangular of the same shape.
- They satisfy RTT with R^{-1} and the mixed relation R^{-1}(L^-)^{-1}_1(L^+)^{-1}_2 = (L^+)^{-1}_2(L^-)^{-1}_1R^{-1}.
- So the map is X ↦ (L^-)^{-1}, Y ↦ (L^+)^{-1}.

**Other convention changes.**
- **q ↔ q^{-1}.** Replaces (U_q, R) by (U_{q^{-1}}, R_21^{-1}). This is the same family, so the result is unchanged.
- **Opposite bad-arc order.** Swaps which of B, C is upper; this is case (β).
- **Opposite stacking order.** Replaces the algebra by its opposite. The opposite of D is again a double of the same type (K–S), and transposition of matrices exchanges the cases.

In every case the ideal I maps to ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩.

# round5_q3 — non-degeneracy of the reduced triangle algebra (L4)

## Plan / status (written first; updated at the end)

Target: (NZ) S̄_n(P_3) ≠ 0, without the quantum trace.

Strategy: (a) turn S(P_3) into an explicit presentation (generators = corner arcs α at v1, β at v2) using the fusion decomposition;
(b) express the corner arcs γ at the third vertex v3 through α, β (coinvariance argument);
(c) show that only one bad-arc convention can possibly give a nonzero algebra (dichotomy);
(d) for that convention, construct an explicit algebra homomorphism from S(P_3) to a nonzero quantum torus killing all bad arcs.

Results are appended below as they are obtained.

## 0. Conventions and inputs

I use the notation of the query: O = S_n(B) ≅ O_q(SL_n) with generators u_{ij}, and ω = q − q^{-1}. R is Klimyk–Schmüdgen's R:

  R = Σ_{i,j} q^{δ_{ij}} E_{ii}⊗E_{jj} + ω Σ_{i>j} E_{ij}⊗E_{ji},

so R_{(ik),(i'k')} ≠ 0 only if (i',k') = (i,k) (value q^{δ_{ik}}), or if (i',k') = (k,i) with i > k (value ω).
- Note R(q)^{-1} = R(q^{-1}). So the sign ε in "R^ε" amounts to replacing q by q^{-1}.
- Throughout, (X_1Y_2)_{(ik),(jl)} = x_{ij}y_{kl} and (Y_2X_1)_{(ik),(jl)} = y_{kl}x_{ij}.

P_3, v1, v2, v3, e = v1v2, e_1 = v1v3, e_2 = v2v3, φ_1, φ_2, α_{st}, β_{στ} are as in §3 of the query. In addition:
- φ_3 : O → S_n(P_3) sends (B, v_t, e_l, e_r) to (P_3, v3, e_1, e_2). The arc going from e_1 to e_2 around v3 is ccw, the same direction as α at v1.
- γ_{ij} := φ_3(u_{ij}) is the corner arc at v3 with tail i on e_1 and head j on e_2.

Inputs used. All are cited from memory, as in the query.
- (L1), (L2), (L6), (L7), as stated in the query.
- (F) Fusion for the triangle. The linear map O⊗O → S_n(P_3), x⊗y ↦ φ_1(x)φ_2(y), is a linear isomorphism. This is the triangle case of (L8), and it carries the same citation GAP as (L8) for n ≥ 3. P_3 is the fusion of two bigons along one edge each; for n = 2 this is Costantino–Lê.
- (C) The cross relation (Lemma 3.1 of the query, from L1): β_{στ}α_{st} = Σ A_{(sσ),(s'σ')} α_{s't}β_{σ'τ}. Here A is LS's local height-exchange matrix for two outgoing endpoints.
- (S) Splitting/coaction (LS). Cutting P_3 along an ideal arc parallel to e gives an algebra map δ : S_n(P_3) → O ⊗ S_n(P_3) with
  - δ(α_{st}) = Σ_m u_{sm}⊗α_{mt},
  - δ(β_{στ}) = Σ_m u_{σm}⊗β_{mτ},
  - δ(D) = 1⊗D for every diagram D that does not meet e.

  Hence δ∘φ_1 = (id⊗φ_1)Δ and δ∘φ_2 = (id⊗φ_2)Δ.

## 1. A presentation of S_n(P_3) (proved from F, C, L2, L7)

**Lemma 1.1.** Let 𝔉 be the algebra with generators α_{st}, β_{στ} (1 ≤ s,t,σ,τ ≤ n) and the following relations:
- RTT for α and det_q α = 1, i.e. the defining relations of O = O_q(SL_n) on the matrix α;
- the same for β;
- the cross relation (C).

Then the obvious map π : 𝔉 → S_n(P_3) is an isomorphism.

*Proof.*
1. **π is well defined.** The relations hold in S_n(P_3): φ_1 and φ_2 are algebra maps (L7), and (C) holds.
2. **π is surjective.** By (F), S_n(P_3) is spanned by the products φ_1(x)φ_2(y), so it is generated by the α's and β's.
3. **A spanning set for 𝔉.** By (C), every word can be rewritten as a combination of words of the form (α-word)(β-word). Hence μ : O⊗O → 𝔉, x⊗y ↦ x(α)y(β), is surjective. It is well defined because the defining relations of O hold for α and for β in 𝔉.
4. **π is injective.** The composite π∘μ is the fusion isomorphism of (F). So μ is injective, hence bijective, and therefore π = (π∘μ)∘μ^{-1} is bijective. ∎

## 2. Reversed arcs and the third corner (proved from L2, L6, F, S)

**Lemma 2.1 (reversed arcs).** In O = S_n(B), let V_{kj} be the arc oriented from e_r to e_l, with state k on e_r and state j on e_l. Then

  V_{kj} = c_{kj} S(u)_{\bar k \bar j}

for nonzero scalars c_{kj}, where S is the antipode.

*Proof.*
1. Take the arc that starts on e_l with state i, runs to the right, makes a U-turn near e_r, and returns to e_l with state j. Choose the height order of its two endpoints on e_l so that it matches the height order that the U-turn relation (L6 ii) prescribes at e_r. Both orders are allowed, and (L6 i) gives a nonzero ν for each.
2. By (L6 i) this arc equals ν_i δ_{j,\bar i}.
3. By (L6 ii), applied near e_r, it also equals Σ_k κ_k u_{ik}V_{\bar k j}. Here the product is a genuine stacking product, because the forward strand lies above the backward one on both edges.
4. Put M_{pj} := κ_p V_{\bar p j}. Then UM = N, where N := (ν_i δ_{j,\bar i})_{ij} is an invertible scalar matrix.
5. Since S(U)U = 1, we get M = S(U)N, that is, κ_p V_{\bar p j} = S(u)_{p\bar j} ν_{\bar j}. ∎

Applied through φ_1, this says the following. The reversed corner arc ᾱ_{ik} at v1, with tail i on e_1 and head k on e, equals c·φ_1(S(u))_{\bar i\bar k} for a nonzero scalar c. The same holds at v2 and v3.

**Lemma 2.2 (reversed bad arcs are redundant).** Let Θ be a strict triangle ({a<b} or {a>b}), and let J_Θ ⊂ O be the two-sided ideal generated by the u_{ab} with (a,b) ∈ Θ.
- Then S(u)_{xy} ∈ J_Θ whenever (x,y) ∈ Θ.
- Consequently, with the rule of (L5), every bad reversed corner arc lies in the ideal generated by the bad forward corner arcs at the same vertex.

*Proof.*
1. In O/J_Θ the matrix U is triangular. By Lemma 3.2 of the query its diagonal entries are units.
2. Write U = D + N with D diagonal and invertible and N strictly triangular. Then (1 + D^{-1}N)^{-1}D^{-1} = Σ_k (−D^{-1}N)^k D^{-1} is a finite sum. It is a two-sided inverse of U of the same triangular shape.
3. The image of S(U) is also a two-sided inverse of U. Two-sided inverses are unique, so the image of S(U) is triangular of the same shape.
4. **The bad set for reversed arcs.** Let the forward arcs of the bigon at v_t go ccw, with bad set F_ccw = {t<h}, i.e. Θ = {a<b} for u_{ab}. The reversed arc V_{kj} has tail k and head j and goes cw. By Lemma 2.1 it is a nonzero multiple of S(u)_{\bar k\bar j}. This element lies in J_Θ iff \bar k < \bar j, iff k > j, i.e. iff tail > head.
5. **Badness matches only for the paired bad sets.** So the reversed arcs lying in the ideal are exactly those with tail > head. This is the cw bad set {t>h} of convention (i). In the other case the same computation gives {t<h} for cw. So the statement holds exactly for the two "paired" conventions, (F_ccw, F_cw) = ({t<h},{t>h}) or ({t>h},{t<h}). Section 3 shows that only these can be nondegenerate. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (the third corner).** Put α^{-1} := φ_1(S(U)) (a two-sided inverse of the matrix α in S_n(P_3)), and Γ := α^{-1}β. Then, for all i, j,

  γ_{ij} = λ_{ij} Γ_{\bar i j}  for some scalars λ_{ij}.

*Proof.*
1. **Reduce to the span W_{ij}.** Isotope γ_{ij} so that its middle runs close to e, and apply the U-turn relation (L6 ii) there. This gives γ_{ij} = Σ_k κ_k (ᾱ_{ik}β_{\bar k j} or β_{\bar k j}ᾱ_{ik}); the order is fixed by L6.
   - By Lemma 2.1, ᾱ_{ik} ∝ α^{-1}_{\bar i\bar k}.
   - If the order is βᾱ, rewrite it with (C). From β_2α_1 = Aα_1β_2 we get β_2α_1^{-1} = α_1^{-1}A^{-1}β_2, i.e. β_{bd}α^{-1}_{ac} = Σ α^{-1}_{aa'}(A^{-1})_{(a'b),(cb'')}β_{b''d}. The outer indices a and d are preserved.
   - Either way, γ_{ij} ∈ W_{ij} := span{α^{-1}_{\bar i m}β_{m'j} : m, m'}.
2. **γ_{ij} is coinvariant.** γ_{ij} does not meet e, so δ(γ_{ij}) = 1⊗γ_{ij} by (S).
3. **Coinvariants of W_{ij} are spanned by Γ_{\bar i j}.** Let X = Σ c_{mm'} α^{-1}_{am}β_{m'j}.
   - By (S) and Δ(S(u)_{am}) = Σ_p S(u)_{pm}⊗S(u)_{ap},

     δ(X) = Σ_{p,r} (Σ_{m,m'} S(u)_{pm}c_{mm'}u_{m'r}) ⊗ α^{-1}_{ap}β_{rj}.
   - By (F), the elements α^{-1}_{ap}β_{rj} (for p, r varying) are linearly independent: they correspond to S(u)_{ap}⊗u_{rj}.
   - So δ(X) = 1⊗X forces S(U)CU = C·1 entrywise in O.
   - Multiplying on the left by U gives CU = UC.
   - Compare coefficients of the linearly independent u_{xy}. This forces C to be diagonal, and then all its diagonal entries to be equal. So X ∈ k·Γ_{aj}. ∎

**Remark.** I do not need λ_{ij} ≠ 0. Only the inclusion "bad γ ∈ ideal generated by the corresponding Γ-entries" is used.

## 3. Dichotomy: at most one convention can be nondegenerate (proved, no L4)

By Lemma 2.2 of the query, A = R^ε(x + yPR^ε) for scalars x, y (I rename the query's α, β to x, y). The query's Prop. 3.3 argument proves the following without assuming (L4). Every step there ends in "a unit α_σσβ_ss times a nonzero scalar is 0", hence 1 = 0.

(a) **F_ccw = {t<h} and y ≠ 0.** Then S̄ = 0. If y = 0, A = xR^ε, and F_cw = {t<h} also gives S̄ = 0.

(b) **F_ccw = {t>h}.** If A_{(sσ),(σs)} ≠ 0 for some s > σ, then S̄ = 0. If A = x'R_21^{-ε} and F_cw = {t>h}, then S̄ = 0.

Moreover, in case (i) [A = aR^ε, F_ccw = {t<h}, F_cw = {t>h}] with n = 2, the scalar must satisfy a²q^ε = 1, or else S̄ = 0. The proof below assumes ε = 1 and writes x_{ij} = α_{ij}, y_{ij} = β_{ij}, t = x_{11}, s = y_{11}.
1. In S̄ the matrix α is lower triangular. Since x_{11}x_{22} = x_{22}x_{11} and x_{11}x_{22} = 1, we have x_{22} = t^{-1}; likewise y_{22} = s^{-1}.
2. (C) with (s,σ,t,τ) = (1,1,1,1) gives st = aq·ts.
3. (C) with (s,σ,t,τ) = (2,1,2,1) gives st^{-1} = a·t^{-1}s, i.e. ts = a·st.
4. Steps 2 and 3 give (a²q − 1)·ts = 0. Since t and s are units in S̄, either S̄ = 0 or a²q = 1.

For general n, the same computation with det_q gives a^n q^ε = 1 or S̄ = 0. I only use n = 2. LS's height-exchange matrix is, as far as I remember, q^{-1/n} times an R-matrix, so a = q^{-1/n}; that is consistent with this constraint.

**Conclusion of §3.**
- (NZ) can hold only in two cases:
  - case (i): A = aR^ε, F_ccw = {t<h}, F_cw = {t>h};
  - its mirror (ii): A = a'R_21^{-ε}, F_ccw = {t>h}, F_cw = {t<h}.
- Which of the two is realised is decided by LS's explicit A, not by Lê–Yu.
- Lê–Yu's convention, whatever its literal wording, must therefore be the compatible one. Otherwise their reduced triangle algebra would be 0.
- Hence (NZ) for the compatible convention is exactly the statement needed; the run may simply *define* the bad set as the compatible one.

**Mirror reduction (ii) → (i).**
1. Relabel all states by i ↦ \bar i. Conjugation by w_0⊗w_0 sends R to R_21.
2. RTT(R_21^{ε}) is equivalent to RTT(R^{-ε}) (conjugate by P).
3. The cross relation with R_21^{-ε} becomes the cross relation with R^{-ε}.
4. The bad sets {t>h} and {t<h} are exchanged.
5. The formula of Lemma 2.3 is preserved: Γ' = w_0Γw_0, so γ'_{ij} = γ_{\bar i\bar j} = λΓ_{i\bar j} = λΓ'_{\bar i j}.
6. For n = 2, det_q of a triangular matrix is just x_{11}x_{22} in either convention.

Hence case (ii) with ε is case (i) with −ε. Moreover R(q)^{-ε} = R(q^{-ε}), so it suffices to treat case (i) with ε = 1, for both q and q^{-1}. Since q is an arbitrary non-root of unity, this is the same as treating ε = 1.

## 4. The reduced algebra as an explicit quotient (case (i), ε = 1)

Combine Lemma 1.1, Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.3. In case (i), S̄_n(P_3) is a quotient of the algebra 𝔄_n defined as follows.
- **Generators.** x_{st} (s ≥ t) and y_{στ} (σ ≤ τ). Put X = (x_{st}), lower triangular, and Y = (y_{στ}), upper triangular; the missing entries are 0.
- **Relations.**
  - RX_1X_2 = X_2X_1R and det_q X = 1;
  - RY_1Y_2 = Y_2Y_1R and det_q Y = 1;
  - Y_2X_1 = aRX_1Y_2, i.e. y_{στ}x_{st} = a q^{δ_{sσ}} x_{st}y_{στ} + [s>σ] aω x_{σt}y_{sτ}.

S̄_n(P_3) is the quotient of 𝔄_n by the ideal generated by the entries Γ_{rj} with r + j > n+1, where Γ = X^{-1}Y. These are the bad γ_{ij}: i < j, r = \bar i. More precisely, S̄_n(P_3) ≅ 𝔄_n/(those Γ_{rj}): by Lemma 2.2 the reversed bad arcs add nothing, and by Lemma 2.3 the bad γ's are scalar multiples of these Γ-entries. Their λ's are nonzero by the explicit U-turn scalars in the ᾱβ case; in general I only claim "⊂", which is all that a nonvanishing proof needs.

So (NZ) follows once there is an algebra map Φ : 𝔄_n → T with T ≠ 0 and Φ(Γ_{rj}) = 0 for all r + j > n+1.

## 5. Theorem (n = 2): S̄_2(P_3) ≠ 0 (PROVED, modulo the cited inputs of §0)

Let a satisfy a²q = 1 (by §3 this is forced). Let T be the quantum torus with invertible generators t, s, b and relations

  tb = a·bt,  sb = q·bs,  st = aq·ts.

T ≠ 0: it has the basis of Laurent monomials t^i s^j b^k. Define

  Φ(x_{11}) = t, Φ(x_{22}) = t^{-1}, Φ(x_{21}) = c := aq·b^{-1}ts^{-1},
  Φ(y_{11}) = s, Φ(y_{22}) = s^{-1}, Φ(y_{12}) = b.

**Useful consequences in T** (each is obtained by conjugating or inverting a defining relation):
- tb^{-1} = a^{-1}b^{-1}t, sb^{-1} = q^{-1}b^{-1}s;
- s^{-1}t = (aq)^{-1}ts^{-1}, and sts^{-1} = aq·t;
- s^{-1}b = q^{-1}bs^{-1}, bt^{-1} = a·t^{-1}b, st^{-1} = (aq)^{-1}t^{-1}s.

*Relations of X* (from RTT with KS's R, for a lower triangular 2×2 matrix): x_{11}x_{21} = q·x_{21}x_{11}, x_{11}x_{22} = x_{22}x_{11}, x_{22}x_{21} = q^{-1}x_{21}x_{22}, and x_{11}x_{22} = 1. The entry ((ik),(jl)) of RTT reads

  q^{δ_{ik}}x_{ij}x_{kl} + [i>k]ω x_{kj}x_{il} = q^{δ_{jl}}x_{kl}x_{ij} + [l>j]ω x_{kj}x_{il}.

With x_{12} = 0, the only non-trivial instances are the three commutations above; the rest follow from them.

Under Φ:
- tc = q·ct: we have tc = aq·tb^{-1}ts^{-1} = q·b^{-1}t·ts^{-1}, and ct = aq·b^{-1}t·s^{-1}t = b^{-1}t·ts^{-1}.
- t·t^{-1} = 1, and t^{-1}c = q^{-1}ct^{-1} follows from tc = qct.

*Relations of Y.* The only non-trivial one is y_{11}y_{12} = q·y_{12}y_{11}, i.e. sb = qbs, which holds by definition.

*Cross relations* y_{στ}x_{st} = aq^{δ_{sσ}}x_{st}y_{στ} + [s>σ]aω x_{σt}y_{sτ}, over all index choices with nonzero left-hand entries:
1. (1,1,1,1): st = aq·ts — definition.
2. (1,1,1,2): bt = aq·tb ⇔ a^{-1} = aq — true.
3. (1,2,1,2): s^{-1}t = a·ts^{-1} ⇔ (aq)^{-1} = a — true.
4. (2,2,1,2): s^{-1}c = aq·cs^{-1}, equivalent to sc = a·cs (using a²q = 1). Check: sc = aq·sb^{-1}ts^{-1} = a·b^{-1}(sts^{-1}) = a·aq·b^{-1}t, while cs = aq·b^{-1}t. So sc = a·cs.
5. (2,2,2,2): s^{-1}t^{-1} = aq·t^{-1}s^{-1} ⇔ ts = (aq)^{-1}st — definition.
6. (2,1,1,1): sc = a·cs (the extra term contains y_{21} = 0) — as in 4.
7. (2,1,1,2): bc = a·cb + aω·ts^{-1}.
   - bc = aq·ts^{-1}.
   - cb = aq·b^{-1}t(s^{-1}b) = a·b^{-1}tbs^{-1} = a²·ts^{-1} = q^{-1}·ts^{-1}.
   - So bc − a·cb = (aq − aq^{-1})ts^{-1} = aω·ts^{-1}.
8. (2,1,2,1): st^{-1} = a·t^{-1}s ⇔ (aq)^{-1} = a — true.
9. (2,1,2,2): bt^{-1} = a·t^{-1}b — true.
10. All other index choices have both sides 0, because each term contains x_{12} or y_{21}.

*The bad corner at v3.* The only bad entry is Γ_{22} (r + j = 4 > 3).
- X^{-1} = [[t^{-1}, 0], [−qc, t]]. Indeed: the (2,1) entry of X^{-1}X is −qct + tc = 0; the (2,1) entry of XX^{-1} is ct^{-1} − qt^{-1}c = 0.
- Hence Φ(Γ_{22}) = −qcb + ts^{-1} = −q·q^{-1}ts^{-1} + ts^{-1} = 0.

Therefore Φ is an algebra map 𝔄_2 → T killing Γ_{22}. By §4 it factors through S̄_2(P_3), and Φ(1) = 1 ≠ 0. So **S̄_2(P_3) ≠ 0** in case (i). By the mirror reduction of §3, the same holds in case (ii).

The images of the diagonal corner arcs are units of T, and their product t^{±1}s^{±1} is a monomial ≠ 0. This also gives (W) for n = 2 directly.

(Moreover Φ is surjective onto T, and all relations of 𝔄_2/(Γ_22) are visibly respected. I believe S̄_2(P_3) ≅ T, the rank-3 quantum torus of Costantino–Lê, but I have not proved injectivity and do not need it.)

## 6. General n: what is reduced, what remains (GAP)

**What is proved for all n** (from the cited inputs of §0):
1. (Dichotomy, §3) At most the compatible convention can give S̄_n(P_3) ≠ 0. The two incompatible mirror choices, and any A outside the two lines k·R^ε and k·R_21^{-ε}, give S̄_n(P_3) = 0. This part needs no L4.
2. (Reduction, §§1, 2, 4) In the compatible convention, (NZ) is equivalent to the purely algebraic statement

   **(AS_n)** In the algebra 𝔄_n of §4 (lower X and upper Y, both O_q(SL_n)-RTT with det_q = 1, cross relation Y_2X_1 = aRX_1Y_2), the two-sided ideal generated by the entries (X^{-1}Y)_{rj} with r + j > n+1 is proper.

   Strictly, (NZ) ⇐ (AS_n), via the inclusion "bad ideal ⊂ ideal of §4". The converse uses λ_{ij} ≠ 0, which is not needed.
3. (Mirror) Case (ii) reduces to case (i), so one proof of (AS_n) covers both mirror conventions.

**What remains open: (AS_n) for n ≥ 3.** I could not construct a nonzero target for general n. The natural candidate, by analogy with §5, is a quantum torus of rank (n−1)(n+4)/2. That is the Fock–Goncharov count 3(n−1) + (n−1)(n−2)/2, and it equals GK(𝔄_n) − n(n−1)/2 = (n−1)(n+2) − n(n−1)/2, consistent with the n(n−1)/2 bad Γ-entries cutting independently.
- **Classical picture (q = 1).** X ∈ B_-, Y ∈ B_+, and X^{-1}Y ∈ w_0B_-, i.e. Xw_0 = Y·(lower). So Y is the upper factor of the UL-decomposition of Xw_0, up to the torus.
- **What a quantum proof needs.** A quantum UL-decomposition (quantum minors / quasideterminants, or a quantum twist map) of Xw_0, with Y defined through it times an extra torus, and then verification of RTT(Y), det_q Y = 1 and the cross relation.
- This is in substance the construction of Lê–Yu's reduced quantum trace for a single triangle. I did not carry it out.

**Why cheaper routes fail.** Each of the following is proved above or immediate.
- **No characters.** In S̄ the diagonal units q-commute (st = aq·ts with aq ≠ 1, §3), so S̄_n(P_3) has no algebra map to any commutative algebra. Moreover, in S̄ every "good" off-diagonal pair satisfies y_{ki}x_{ik} − a·x_{ik}y_{ki} = aω·x_{kk}y_{ii} (i > k), which is a unit. So no good corner arc can be sent to 0 either, and block-diagonal or Levi-type targets fail.
- **No outgoing gluing maps.** Splitting homomorphisms go *into* the triangle. Every ideal arc in P_3 is boundary-parallel, so P_3 cannot be cut into smaller pieces, and the LS gluing calculus yields no algebra map *out of* S_n(P_3) except the comodule maps.
- **Merging edges is not multiplicative.** Merging edges (the naive counit of the query) is not an algebra map. The query already notes that it does not kill the bad arcs at the third vertex.
- **Inducing from a corner character gives zero.** Inducing from a character of a corner copy φ_1(O) gives S̄ ⊗_{φ_1(O)} k_χ. For n = 2 this is 0: χ kills the off-diagonal arc x_{21}, which is a unit in S̄_2 (it maps to c = aq·b^{-1}ts^{-1} in T). Such modules cannot detect S̄ ≠ 0.

So, beyond n = 2, I see no way to avoid an explicit quantum-torus (quantum-trace-type) construction.

## 7. Summary

**Proved, modulo the cited LS inputs** (L1, L2, L6, L7, the fusion statement F, which has a citation GAP for n ≥ 3 as in L8, and the splitting coaction S):
1. A presentation of S_n(P_3) by the corner arcs at two vertices (Lemma 1.1).
2. Reversed corner arcs are antipode entries, and reversed bad arcs are redundant (Lemmas 2.1, 2.2).
3. The corner arcs at the third vertex are γ_{ij} = λ_{ij}(α^{-1}β)_{\bar i j} (Lemma 2.3, by a coinvariance argument).
4. The dichotomy: only the compatible convention can be nondegenerate, and the incompatible ones give S̄ = 0 (§3).
5. **(NZ) and (W) for n = 2**, for both mirror conventions: an explicit algebra map onto a rank-3 quantum torus killing all bad arcs (§5).

**Open (GAP):** (NZ) for n ≥ 3, equivalently (AS_n) of §6. The citation (L4) is not replaced for n ≥ 3. What I did show is that it reduces to a concrete statement about two quantum Borel matrices, and that (L4) can be used only with the compatible convention. Any convention for which (L4) holds must be the compatible one, which is what the run's §3 derives.

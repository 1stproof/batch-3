# Answer to round2_q3: direct proof of Claim D for general n (no use of G1)

## Status

**Proved for all n**, citing only standard results that are stated precisely in §0:

1. The Borel isomorphisms O^+ ≅ U_q(b_+) and O^- ≅ U_q(b_-), with explicit formulas in both directions.
2. In D, the lifted L^± satisfy the mixed relation R L^-_1 L^+_2 = L^+_2 L^-_1 R. This follows conceptually: the double's cross relation (X), applied to matrix entries, *is* this RLL relation, because σ(L^+, L^-) = q^{-1/n} R_21, which I compute explicitly.
3. A ≅ D, where A is the algebra generated by O^+ and O^- with the mixed relation. Combined with §2 of the round-1 answer, A/⟨l^+_ii l^-_ii − 1⟩ ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n).

**Possible soft spot.** The citations C4 and C5 in §0 (the precise axioms of the skew pairing, and the presentation of D) are quoted from memory. I re-derived the axiom form in §0 so that it is internally consistent.

## 0. Conventions and citations

**Coproduct (K–S).**
- Δ(E_i) = E_i⊗K_i + 1⊗E_i
- Δ(F_i) = F_i⊗1 + K_i^{-1}⊗F_i
- Δ(K̂_j) = K̂_j⊗K̂_j
- K_i = K̂_i K̂_{i+1}^{-1}

**Algebras.**
- U_+ = ⟨E_i, K̂_j^{±1}⟩.
- U_- = ⟨F_i, K̂'_j^{±1}⟩, with weights K̂'_j F_i K̂'_j^{-1} = q^{-(δ_ji − δ_{j,i+1})} F_i.
- Ũ_± denote the same algebras without the relation ∏K̂ = 1. The product ∏K̂_j is central in Ũ_+.

**R-matrix.** R = q Σ e_ii⊗e_ii + Σ_{i≠j} e_ii⊗e_jj + (q−q^{-1}) Σ_{i>j} e_ij⊗e_ji.
- With T = (t_ij), the relation R T_1T_2 = T_2T_1R is equivalent to the standard O_q(M_n) relations, checked entrywise from the entry formula of the round-1 answer:
  - t_ij t_il = q t_il t_ij for j<l;
  - t_ij t_kj = q t_kj t_ij for i<k;
  - t_il t_kj = t_kj t_il for i<k, j<l;
  - t_ij t_kl − t_kl t_ij = (q−q^{-1}) t_il t_kj for i<k, j<l.
- These relations are symmetric under transposition t_ij ↦ t_ji, so transposition is an automorphism of O_q(M_n).

**Citations (from memory).**
- **C1.** PBW for O_q(M_n): the lexicographically ordered monomials in the t_ij form a basis (K–S Ch. 9; O_q(M_n) is an iterated Ore extension).
- **C2.** Bergman's diamond lemma. For a reduction system compatible with a semigroup order satisfying DCC, all ambiguities are resolvable if and only if the irreducible words form a basis.
- **C3.** Triangular decomposition and PBW for Ũ_+. Multiplication U^0⊗U^+ → Ũ_+ is a linear isomorphism, where U^0 = C[K̂_j^{±1}] and U^+ = ⟨E_i⟩ with Serre relations. Moreover dim U^+_β = P(β), the Kostant partition function (Jantzen Ch. 4/8; K–S Ch. 6).
- **C4.** Existence of the skew pairing σ: U_+ × U_- → C with the values given in the problem. Its axioms are
  - σ(uv, a) = Σ σ(u, a_(1)) σ(v, a_(2)),
  - σ(u, ab) = Σ σ(u_(1), b) σ(u_(2), a),
  - σ(1, ·) = ε, σ(·, 1) = ε.

  These are exactly the axioms under which the cross relation (X), Σσ(u_1,a_1)u_2a_2 = Σa_1u_1σ(u_2,a_2), is stable under products. I checked this directly: (X) for (u,a) and (v,a) gives (X) for (uv,a) by the first axiom, and similarly in a by the second. As a consistency check, the axioms are compatible with K̂_jE_i = q^{δ_ji−δ_{j,i+1}}E_iK̂_j when paired against F_i.
- **C5.** D = U_- ⋈ U_+ is the vector space U_-⊗U_+, and as an algebra it equals (U_+ * U_-)/⟨X(u,a) : u∈U_+, a∈U_-⟩ (K–S §8.2).
- **C6.** Vasconcelos: a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated module over a commutative ring is bijective.

**Weight orthogonality (proved from C4).** Grade U_± by Q with deg K̂ = 0, deg E_i = α_i, deg F_i = −α_i. Then σ(u, a) = 0 unless deg u = −deg a.
1. For a grouplike g, σ(g, ab) = σ(g, b)σ(g, a). Since σ(g, F_j) = 0, σ(g, ·) vanishes on nonzero degrees.
2. σ(E_i, ab) = σ(E_i, b)σ(K_i, a) + ε(b)σ(E_i, a). By induction on the length of a, σ(E_i, ·) is supported in degree −α_i.
3. General u follows by induction on length, using σ(uv,a) = Σσ(u,a_1)σ(v,a_2) and the fact that Δ is graded.

## 1. Borel isomorphism O^+ ≅ U_q(b_+)

**Definitions.**
- A is the algebra generated by t_ij (i ≤ j) with the O_q(M_n) relations, in which any t_ab with a > b occurring in a relation is set to 0. Equivalently, A = O_q(M_n)/J with J = ⟨t_ab : a > b⟩.
- Õ^+ = A[t_ii^{-1}].
- O^+ = Õ^+/(∏t_ii − 1).

**Lemma 1.1 (PBW for A).** The ordered monomials in the upper letters t_ij (i ≤ j), in lex order, form a basis of A.

*Proof.*
- Take the deglex order on words, with letters ordered lexicographically. The O_q(M_n) reduction system S rewrites each out-of-order pair, x > y, as a combination of smaller words. The only non-monomial rule is
  t_kl t_ij → t_ij t_kl − (q−q^{-1}) t_il t_kj   (i<k, j<l),
  and both words on the right are smaller, since t_ij < t_kl and t_il < t_kl.
- By C1 and C2, all ambiguities of S are resolvable.
- Let π set lower letters to 0.
  - If a word contains a lower letter, so does every word produced from it by a rule of S. Monomial rules keep the letters. In the rule above, if i > j or k > l then k > j, so t_kj is lower.
  - Hence J equals the span of ordered monomials containing a lower letter. That span lies in J, and conversely every word with a lower letter reduces inside it.
  - So A has basis the ordered upper monomials, by C1. ∎

**Lemma 1.2 (diagonal entries are normal).** In A, for k ≤ l:
- t_jj t_kl = q^{δ_jk − δ_jl} t_kl t_jj;
- the t_jj commute with each other.

*Proof.* Case by case from the four relation types; every correction term contains a lower letter and so vanishes.
- j < k: correction t_jl t_kj = 0, so the two commute.
- j = k: the same-row relation gives the factor q.
- k < j < l: the commuting relation applies.
- j = l: the same-column relation gives the factor q^{-1}.
- j > l: correction t_kj t_jl = 0, so the two commute. ∎

Consequently:
- the t_ii are regular (multiplying by t_ii acts injectively on the PBW basis);
- Õ^+ has basis t^a·m, with a ∈ Z^n and m an ordered strictly-upper monomial;
- Õ^+ is Q_+-graded by deg t_ik = ε_i − ε_k, since the relations preserve row and column contents;
- (Õ^+)_β is a free left module over R := C[t_jj^{±1}], of rank equal to the number of strictly-upper ordered monomials of degree β. This number is P(β), because t_ik ↔ ε_i − ε_k is a bijection onto the positive roots.

**Lemma 1.3 (generation).** For i < j < l,
  t_il = (q−q^{-1})^{-1} [t_ij, t_jl] t_jj^{-1}.

This is the relation t_ij t_jl − t_jl t_ij = (q−q^{-1}) t_il t_jj. Hence Õ^+ is generated by t_ii^{±1} and t_{i,i+1}.

**Theorem 1.4.** The map Φ: Ũ_+ → Õ^+ defined by
- K̂_j ↦ t_jj,
- E_i ↦ (q−q^{-1})^{-1} t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1} t_{i,i+1},

is an algebra isomorphism. It induces U_q(b_+) ≅ O^+. Its inverse Ψ is given by
- Ψ(t_ii) = K̂_i,
- Ψ(t_ik) = (q−q^{-1}) K̂_k E_{ik} for i < k,

where E_{i,i+1} = E_i and E_{ik} = E_i E_{i+1,k} − q^{-1} E_{i+1,k} E_i.

*Proof.*

*Well defined.* Write e_i = t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1} t_{i,i+1}.
- **Cartan relations.** The t_jj commute, and t_jj e_i t_jj^{-1} = q^{δ_ji − δ_{j,i+1}} e_i by Lemma 1.2.
- **Distant indices, |i−j| > 1.** e_i and e_j commute. The relation for t_{i,i+1} and t_{j,j+1} has correction t_{i,j+1} t_{j,i+1}, which is 0 because its second factor is lower. The diagonal factors q-commute trivially here.
- **Serre relations (indices i, i+1, i+2; write 1, 2, 3).** Set a = t_12, b = t_23, d_k = t_kk. Then e_1 = d_2^{-1}a and e_2 = d_3^{-1}b.
  - Relations in A:
    - ab − ba = (q−q^{-1}) t_13 d_2,
    - a t_13 = q t_13 a,
    - t_13 b = q b t_13,
    - t_13 d_3 = q d_3 t_13,
    - d_2 commutes with t_13,
    - d_3 commutes with a.
  - Hence f := e_1e_2 − q^{-1}e_2e_1 = (q−q^{-1}) d_3^{-1} t_13.
  - Then e_1 f = q f e_1 and f e_2 = q e_2 f; both were checked by moving the d's with the rules above.
  - These are equivalent to the two Serre relations, because
    - e_1²e_2 − [2]e_1e_2e_1 + e_2e_1² = e_1 f − q f e_1,
    - e_2²e_1 − [2]e_2e_1e_2 + e_1e_2² = f e_2 − q e_2 f.
- The determinant ∏K̂_j ↦ ∏t_jj is central on both sides, so Φ descends to the quotients.

*Surjective.* By Lemma 1.3.

*Injective.*
- Φ is Q_+-graded and R-linear, under the identification U^0 ≅ R given by K̂_j ↔ t_jj.
- By C3, (Ũ_+)_β ≅ R^{P(β)}. By §1, (Õ^+)_β ≅ R^{P(β)}.
- Composing with a basis identification, Φ_β is a surjective endomorphism of R^{P(β)}, hence bijective by C6.

*Formula for Ψ.* Φ(E_i) = (q−q^{-1})^{-1}e_i gives Ψ(t_{i,i+1}) = (q−q^{-1})K̂_{i+1}E_i. For general t_ik, apply Ψ to Lemma 1.3 with j = i+1:
- K̂_k commutes with E_i;
- K̂_{i+1}^{-1} commutes with E_iE_{i+1,k}, which has weight ε_i − ε_k;
- K̂_{i+1}E_iK̂_{i+1}^{-1} = q^{-1}E_i.

Together these give Ψ(t_ik) = (q−q^{-1})K̂_k(E_iE_{i+1,k} − q^{-1}E_{i+1,k}E_i). ∎

**Coalgebra compatibility (Lemma 1.5).**
- Δ(t_ik) := Σ_{i≤j≤k} t_jk ⊗ t_ij, the flipped matrix coproduct, is an algebra map on Õ^+. RTT relations are preserved by T ↦ T⊗̇T, triangularity is preserved, and the t_ii are grouplike.
- Φ intertwines it with the K–S coproduct. Check on E_i:
  Δ(e_i) = e_i ⊗ t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}t_ii + 1⊗e_i = (Φ⊗Φ)(E_i⊗K_i + 1⊗E_i).
- Hence Δ(Ψ t_ik) = Σ_j Ψt_jk ⊗ Ψt_ij in U_+.

**Lower Borel.**
- O^- is defined the same way with lower-triangular s_ij. Transposition τ: s_ij ↦ t_ji gives O^- ≅ O^+.
- θ: U_- → U_+ defined by K̂'_j ↦ K̂_j^{-1}, F_i ↦ −q^{-1}E_i is an algebra isomorphism: the weights match and the Serre relations are homogeneous.
- So Φ^- := τ^{-1}∘Φ∘θ: U_- → O^- is an isomorphism. Explicitly:
  - Φ^-(K̂'_j) = s_jj^{-1},
  - Φ^-(F_i) = (q^{-1}−q)^{-1} s_{i+1,i} s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.

  This uses s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}s_{i+1,i} = q s_{i+1,i}s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.
- The inverse is:
  - s_ii = K̂'_i^{-1},
  - s_ki = (q^{-1}−q) F_{ki} K̂'_k^{-1} for k > i,

  where F_{i+1,i} = F_i and F_{ki} = F_{k,i+1}F_i − q F_iF_{k,i+1}. This is computed from the transposed recursion s_ki = (q−q^{-1})^{-1}[s_{i+1,i}, s_{k,i+1}] s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.
- Δ(s_ik) = Σ_j s_jk ⊗ s_ij in U_-; checked on F_i as above.
- For n = 2 this agrees with the round-1 formulas: a' = K̂'^{-1}, d' = K̂', c' = (q^{-1}−q)FK̂'.

## 2. Mixed relation in D, all n

**Lemma 2.1.** Write ρ(a,b;c,d) := σ(t_ab, s_cd), where t and s are the entries in U_±. Then

  ρ(a,b;c,d) = q^{-1/n} [δ_ab δ_cd q^{δ_ac} + (q−q^{-1}) [a<b] δ_ad δ_bc].

*Proof.*
- **Diagonal entries.** σ(K̂_a, K̂'_c^{-1}) = q^{δ_ac − 1/n}.
- **Other entries.** By weight orthogonality the only remaining nonzero values are r_ab := σ(t_ab, s_ba) with a < b.
- **Base case, b = a+1.**
  - Use σ(u, xy) = σ(u_1, y)σ(u_2, x) with u = K̂_{a+1}E_a, x = F_a, y = K̂'^{-1}_{a+1}.
  - This gives σ(K̂_{a+1},K̂'^{-1}_{a+1}) σ(K̂_{a+1}E_a, F_a) = q^{1−1/n} · q^{-1}/(q^{-1}−q).
  - Multiplying by the normalising constants (q−q^{-1})(q^{-1}−q) gives r_{a,a+1} = q^{-1/n}(q−q^{-1}).
- **Induction step.**
  - Write t_ab = (q−q^{-1})^{-1}(t_{a,a+1}t_{a+1,b} − t_{a+1,b}t_{a,a+1}) t_{a+1,a+1}^{-1}.
  - Pair with s_ba using σ(uvw, x) = Σσ(u,x_1)σ(v,x_2)σ(w,x_3) and Δ²(s_ba) = Σ s_ra⊗s_pr⊗s_bp.
  - The first product contributes r_{a,a+1} r_{a+1,b} q^{1/n}.
  - The second product contributes 0, because σ(t_{a+1,b}, s_ra) = 0 for every r.
  - So r_ab = q^{1/n}(q−q^{-1})^{-1} r_{a,a+1}r_{a+1,b} = q^{-1/n}(q−q^{-1}). ∎

Thus 𝐑 := Σρ(a,b;c,d) e_ab⊗e_cd = q^{-1/n} R_21.

**Theorem 2.2.** In D, R L^-_1 L^+_2 = L^+_2 L^-_1 R, with L^+ = (Ψt_ij) and L^- = (Ψ^-s_ij).

*Proof.*
- (X) with u = t_kl and a = s_ij, together with the coproducts of Lemma 1.5, reads
  Σ_{m,p} ρ(m,l;p,j) t_km s_ip = Σ_{m,p} s_pj t_ml ρ(k,m;i,p).
- This is entry [(k,i),(l,j)] of L^+_1 L^-_2 𝐑 = 𝐑 L^-_2 L^+_1.
- Drop the scalar q^{-1/n} and conjugate by the flip P to get L^+_2L^-_1R = R L^-_1L^+_2.
- (X) holds in D for all u and a. ∎

For n = 2, entry (2,1,1,2) gives bc' − c'b = (q−q^{-1})(a'd − ad'). This is the relation the run checked numerically.

## 3. A ≅ D

Let A be the free product O^+ * O^- (each with its determinant relation) modulo the mixed relation.
- Via Φ^{±}, A = (U_+ * U_-)/⟨X(u,a) : u∈C_+, a∈C_-⟩, where C_± are the spans of the L^± entries. By §2 the entries of the mixed relation are exactly these X(u,a).
- C_± are subcoalgebras that generate U_± as algebras, the inverse grouplikes t_ii^{-1} included.

**Closure lemma (proved).** The relations X(u,a) for u∈C_+, a∈C_- imply X(u,a) for all u, a.
- *Products.* Use the product computations of C4. Products of subcoalgebras are subcoalgebras, so induct on word length, first in u for all a in a fixed subcoalgebra, then in a.
- *Inverse grouplikes.* From X(g,a) one gets g a = Σσ(g^{-1},a_1) a_2 g σ(g,a_3). Conjugating by g^{-1} and using σ(g,a_1)σ(g^{-1},a_2) = ε(a) yields X(g^{-1},a).
- The a-side is symmetric.

By C5, A ≅ D. The PBW spanning set (O^- monomials)·(O^+ monomials) is not needed separately, although it holds because D = U_-⊗U_+.

**Corollary.** A/⟨l^+_ii l^-_ii − 1⟩ ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n).
- Here l^+_ii = K̂_i and l^-_ii = K̂'_i^{-1}, so the ideal is ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩.
- Apply round-1 §2, whose proof is a direct presentation comparison.

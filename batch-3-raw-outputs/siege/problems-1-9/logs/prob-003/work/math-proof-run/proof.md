# The reduced stated SL(n)-skein algebra of the punctured bigon and U_q(b_-) ⋈ U_q(b_+)

## 0. Reading of the problem, conventions, and status

### 0.1 Reading of the notation
The problem uses macros that are not defined in the statement. They are read as follows.

- **The skein algebra.** `\bcS_n(\fB^*)` is the **reduced stated SL(n)-skein algebra** S̄_n(𝔅*) of the **punctured bigon** 𝔅*.
  - S_n(Σ) is the stated SL(n)-skein algebra of Lê–Sikora (LS), with product given by stacking: in α·β, α is above β.
  - S̄_n(Σ) is its quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by the **bad arcs** of Lê–Yu (LY).
- **The surface.** 𝔅* is a closed disk with two boundary points removed and one interior puncture p.
  - The boundary ideal points are v_t (top) and v_b (bottom).
  - The boundary edges are e_l (left) and e_r (right).
- **The arcs.** b_ij and c_ij are the arcs oriented from e_l to e_r, with state i on e_l and state j on e_r. The arc b_ij passes above p and c_ij passes below p.
  - b_ij is a corner arc at v_t and goes **counterclockwise (ccw)** around v_t.
  - c_ij is a corner arc at v_b and goes **clockwise (cw)** around v_b.
  - Check: seen from v_t, an arc running from the left edge to the right edge below v_t turns through increasing angle; seen from v_b, it turns through decreasing angle.
- **The quantum group.** U_q(sl_n) is Klimyk–Schmüdgen's U_q^ext(sl_n).
  - Generators: E_i, F_i (1 ≤ i ≤ n−1) and K̂_j^{±1} (1 ≤ j ≤ n), with K̂_1⋯K̂_n = 1 and K_i := K̂_iK̂_{i+1}^{-1}.
  - Relations: K̂_jE_iK̂_j^{-1} = q^{δ_ji−δ_{j,i+1}}E_i, K̂_jF_iK̂_j^{-1} = q^{−δ_ji+δ_{j,i+1}}F_i, [E_i,F_j] = δ_ij(K_i−K_i^{-1})/(q−q^{-1}), and the quantum Serre relations.
  - Coproduct: Δ(E_i) = E_i⊗K_i + 1⊗E_i, Δ(F_i) = F_i⊗1 + K_i^{-1}⊗F_i, Δ(K̂_j) = K̂_j⊗K̂_j.
- **The Borels.** U_+ := U_q(b_+) = ⟨E_i, K̂_j^{±1}⟩. U_- := U_q(b_-) = ⟨F_i, K̂'_j^{±1}⟩. The prime marks the Cartan copy inside U_-, and both Borels carry the relation ∏K̂ = 1. In U_- write K'_i := K̂'_iK̂'^{-1}_{i+1}.

A sanity check supports this reading. D = U_- ⋈ U_+ has GK dimension n²+n−2 = 2(n(n+1)/2 − 1), the dimension of two quantum Borels. The unreduced S_n(𝔅*) is too large: it has GK dimension 2n²−2, by Theorem 7.1 below.

**Ground field.** k is a field of characteristic 0 containing q^{±1/n}, and q is not a root of unity. Write ω := q − q^{-1} ≠ 0 and ī := n+1−i.

### 0.2 Matrix conventions
The R-matrix is Klimyk–Schmüdgen's:

  R = q Σ_i e_ii⊗e_ii + Σ_{i≠j} e_ii⊗e_jj + ω Σ_{i>j} e_ij⊗e_ji.

Its entries R_{(ik),(i'k')} are nonzero only at (i',k') = (i,k), with value q^{δ_ik}, and at (i',k') = (k,i) with i > k, with value ω. P is the flip.

For n×n matrices X, Y with entries in an algebra:
- (X_1Y_2)_{(ik),(jl)} = x_ij y_kl;
- (Y_2X_1)_{(ik),(jl)} = y_kl x_ij;
- "RTT(𝓡) for T" means 𝓡T_1T_2 = T_2T_1𝓡.

RTT(R) is equivalent to the defining relations of O_q(M_n):
- t_ij t_il = q t_il t_ij for j < l;
- t_ij t_kj = q t_kj t_ij for i < k;
- t_il t_kj = t_kj t_il for i < k, j < l;
- t_ij t_kl − t_kl t_ij = ω t_il t_kj for i < k, j < l.

These relations are invariant under transposition t_ij ↦ t_ji. O := O_q(SL_n) adds det_q T = Σ_σ (−q)^{ℓ(σ)} t_{1σ(1)}⋯t_{nσ(n)} = 1.

Standard identities (checked symbolically for n = 2, 3):
- R^{-1} = R_21 − ωP;
- R(q^{-1}) = R(q)^{-1};
- PR_21P = R;
- RTT(R_21) ⇔ RTT(R^{-1}), by conjugating with P.

### 0.3 Status (honest summary)
The main theorem below proves parts (1) and (2) for all n ≥ 2 and q not a root of unity. It relies on foundational results of Lê–Sikora and Lê–Yu and on standard quantum-group facts, all listed in §0.4. **They are quoted from the literature and were not re-derived here.**

Two published conventions are not pinned down:
- **ε ∈ {±1}:** whether the Lê–Sikora bigon arc matrix satisfies RTT(R) or RTT(R^{-1}).
- **F_ccw:** whether Lê–Yu call a ccw corner arc with tail state t and head state h bad when t < h, or when t > h.

These two bits only decide *which* of four explicit formulas is the isomorphism (§1, Table). The proof shows that each case gives an isomorphism, and part (2) holds in all four cases. The expected case is ε = +1 and F_ccw = {t < h}, which gives b ↦ L^-, c ↦ L^+. This is a weak lean and is not certified. The section "Status" (§15) lists the remaining caveats.

### 0.4 Cited inputs (stated exactly as they are used)

**Quantum-group side.**
- **(C1)** PBW for O_q(M_n): the ordered monomials in the t_ij, in lexicographic order of letters, form a basis.
- **(C3)** Triangular decomposition for Ũ_+ (= U_+ without ∏K̂ = 1).
  - Multiplication U^0⊗U^+ → Ũ_+ is a linear isomorphism, where U^0 = k[K̂_j^{±1}] and U^+ = ⟨E_i⟩ with the Serre relations.
  - dim U^+_β = P(β), the Kostant partition function.
- **(C4)** Skew pairing (Drinfeld–Tanisaki, simply connected Cartan part). There is a bilinear σ: U_+ × U_- → k with the problem's values on generators:
  - σ(K̂_i, K̂'_j) = q^{1/n−δ_ij};
  - σ(E_i, F_j) = δ_ij/(q^{-1}−q);
  - σ(K̂_i, F_j) = σ(E_j, K̂'_i) = 0;
  - σ(1,·) = ε, σ(·,1) = ε.

  It satisfies σ(uv, a) = Σσ(u, a_(1))σ(v, a_(2)) and σ(u, ab) = Σσ(u_(1), b)σ(u_(2), a). (It is compatible with ∏K̂ = 1, since ∏_i q^{1/n−δ_ij} = 1.)
- **(C5)** The generalized double (Klimyk–Schmüdgen §8.2). D := U_- ⋈ U_+ is the vector space U_-⊗U_+. Both factors are subalgebras, and multiplication U_-⊗U_+ → D is the identity. The cross relation is, for u ∈ U_+ and a ∈ U_-,

  (X)  Σ σ(u_(1), a_(1)) u_(2) a_(2) = Σ a_(1) u_(1) σ(u_(2), a_(2)).

  Equivalently, D = (U_+ ∗ U_-)/⟨X(u,a)⟩.

  *Remark.* With KS's coproduct and the problem's values, this placement of coproducts in (C4) and (X) is forced. With σ(uv,a) = Σσ(u,a_2)σ(v,a_1) instead, pairing K̂_jE_i and E_iK̂_j against F_i gives the ratio q^{−(δ_ji−δ_{j,i+1})}, contradicting K̂_jE_i = q^{δ_ji−δ_{j,i+1}}E_iK̂_j. The other natural form of (X) is product-stable only under that inconsistent placement.
- **(C6)** Vasconcelos: a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated module over a commutative ring is bijective.

**Skein side (LS = Lê–Sikora, LY = Lê–Yu).**
- **(S1) Bigon.** Let 𝔅 be the bigon (vertices v_t, v_b; edges e_l, e_r), and let u_ij be the arc from e_l to e_r with states i on e_l and j on e_r.
  - S_n(𝔅) is a Hopf algebra, spanned by monomials in the u_ij.
  - Cutting along an ideal arc from v_t to v_b gives the coproduct Δ(u_ij) = Σ_k u_ik⊗u_kj.
  - There are ε ∈ {±1} and a scalar c ≠ 0 such that x_ij ↦ c·u_ij is an algebra isomorphism from the algebra O^{(ε)} (RTT(R^ε) and the corresponding quantum determinant equal to 1) onto S_n(𝔅). The u_ij are therefore linearly independent.
  - Robustness: any variant of LS's conventions (q ↔ q^{-1}, stacking order, transposition, i ↦ ī) gives RTT(R') with R' ∈ {R, R^{-1}, R_21, R_21^{-1}}. By §0.2 this is RTT(R^ε) for some ε.
  - That x_ij ↦ u_ij itself (c^n = 1) is an isomorphism is *proved* in Prop. 10.4.
- **(S2) Height exchange.** Take two endpoints of a stated web on one boundary edge that are consecutive in height. Exchanging their heights, with the diagram fixed, gives a linear combination of webs with the same diagram and the two states replaced.
  - When both endpoints point out of the edge, or both into it, the coefficients form a fixed invertible matrix. It depends only on this local type and on the direction of exchange, not on the rest of the web or the surface (locality).
  - Only existence and locality are used, never the entries.
- **(S3) Functoriality.** An orientation-preserving embedding of punctured bordered surfaces, sending each boundary edge into a boundary edge, induces an algebra homomorphism of stated skein algebras that acts on diagrams by taking images. More generally, embeddings of the thickenings Σ×(−1,1) that preserve the upward direction over boundary edges induce linear maps. Also S(Σ_1 ⊔ Σ_2) = S(Σ_1)⊗S(Σ_2).
- **(S4) Splitting.** For an ideal arc c in the interior of Σ there is a linear (indeed algebra) map θ_c: S(Σ) → S(Cut_cΣ). If a web W meets c×(−1,1) transversally at points of distinct heights, then θ_c(W) = Σ_s W(s), summed over all state assignments s to W ∩ c, both lifts of a point getting the same state. Splittings along disjoint arcs commute. Only existence is used, not injectivity.
- **(S5) Boundary relations: shapes only.**
  - (i) A returning arc (both endpoints adjacent on one edge, cobounding a disk with it) with states i, j equals δ_{j,ī} times a nonzero scalar.
  - (ii) An n-valent vertex joined to an edge by n strands is 0 unless the states form a permutation, and is then a scalar.
  - (iii) (CUT) A strand segment running parallel and adjacent to an edge e, with nothing in between, equals Σ_i κ_i·(the web cut there into two endpoints on e, at consecutive heights, with states i and ī).
- **(S6) Commutant (algebraic, standard; q generic).** For the matrix U of generators of O^{(ε)},
  {X ∈ End(k^n⊗k^n) : XU_1U_2 = U_1U_2X} = span{1, PR^ε}.
  - Reason: such X are comodule endomorphisms of V⊗V, which is the sum of two non-isomorphic simple comodules. Also PR^ε commutes with U_1U_2, because PR^εU_1U_2 = PU_2U_1R^ε = U_1U_2PR^ε.
- **(Y1) Shape of LY's bad-arc rule.** A corner arc at a boundary ideal point v joins the two edges adjacent to v inside a small neighbourhood of v, in either orientation.
  - Badness is intrinsic (invariant under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms) and local, so it depends only on the turning direction (ccw or cw around v) and on the pair (tail state t, head state h).
  - For each direction the bad set is a strict triangle: F_ccw, F_cw ∈ {{t<h}, {t>h}}.
  - Which triangles occur, and how F_ccw and F_cw relate, is **derived** below, not assumed.
  - This "two-sided" reading (corner arcs of both turning directions can be bad) is the one used in the main line of the proof. If LY's rule declares only one turning direction bad, see (S7) and the Remark after Cor. 9.4.
- **(S7) Reversed arcs and the antipode (used only for the one-sided reading of (Y1)).** In the bigon, the arc ū_ab running from e_r to e_l (state a on e_l, state b on e_r) equals λ_ab·S(u_{b̄ā}) with λ_ab ≠ 0, where S is the antipode of O = S_n(𝔅).
- **(Y2) Non-degeneracy.** For the ideal triangle P_3, S̄_n(P_3) ≠ 0, because LY's reduced quantum trace is a unital algebra map from S̄_n(P_3) to a quantum torus. For n = 2 an independent argument is sketched in §14.

## 1. Main Theorem

Let D = U_q(b_-) ⋈ U_q(b_+) be the double of (C5).

Define elements of U_± (root vectors):
- E_{i,i+1} := E_i, and E_{ik} := E_i E_{i+1,k} − q^{-1}E_{i+1,k}E_i for i+1 < k;
- F_{i+1,i} := F_i, and F_{ki} := F_{k,i+1}F_i − qF_iF_{k,i+1} for k > i+1.

Define the matrices
- L^+ (upper triangular): l^+_ii = K̂_i and l^+_ik = (q−q^{-1})K̂_kE_ik for i < k;
- L^- (lower triangular): l^-_ii = K̂'^{-1}_i and l^-_ki = (q^{-1}−q)F_kiK̂'^{-1}_k for k > i.

**Main Theorem.** Let ε and F_ccw be fixed by the LS and LY conventions (§0.4).

**(1)** There is an algebra isomorphism Φ: S̄_n(𝔅*) → U_q(b_-) ⋈ U_q(b_+). Let T_low be the one of the matrices B = (b_ij), C = (c_ij) that is lower triangular in S̄_n(𝔅*), and T_up the other. Then Φ is given by

  T_low ↦ (L^-)^ε,  T_up ↦ (L^+)^ε  (entrywise).

Explicitly, there are four cases:

| case | ε | F_ccw | T_low | Φ(b_ij) | Φ(c_ij) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | +1 | {t<h} | B | l^-_ij | l^+_ij |
| B | +1 | {t>h} | C | l^+_ij | l^-_ij |
| C | −1 | {t<h} | B | ((L^-)^{-1})_ij | ((L^+)^{-1})_ij |
| D | −1 | {t>h} | C | ((L^+)^{-1})_ij | ((L^-)^{-1})_ij |

In every case the "bad" entries (the strictly upper ones of T_low and the strictly lower ones of T_up) are 0 in S̄_n(𝔅*) and map to 0.

Case A written out:
- b_ii ↦ K̂'^{-1}_i, and b_ki ↦ (q^{-1}−q)F_kiK̂'^{-1}_k for k > i;
- c_ii ↦ K̂_i, and c_ik ↦ (q−q^{-1})K̂_kE_ik for i < k.

Its inverse is
- K̂_i ↦ c_ii and K̂'_i ↦ b_ii^{-1};
- E_i ↦ (q−q^{-1})^{-1}c_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}c_{i,i+1};
- F_i ↦ (q^{-1}−q)^{-1}b_{i+1,i}b_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.

The inverse matrices in cases C and D are triangular and are computed by back substitution. For example:
- ((L^+)^{-1})_ii = K̂_i^{-1} and ((L^+)^{-1})_{i,i+1} = −(q−q^{-1})q^{-1}K̂_i^{-1}E_i;
- ((L^-)^{-1})_ii = K̂'_i and ((L^-)^{-1})_{i+1,i} = (q−q^{-1})q F_iK̂'_i.

These were checked numerically for n = 3.

**(2)** In every case Φ(b_iic_ii) = (K̂_iK̂'^{-1}_i)^{±1}, a central element. Φ maps the ideal I = ⟨b_iic_ii − 1 : 1 ≤ i ≤ n⟩ onto ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩, and

  S̄_n(𝔅*)/I ≅ D/⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩ ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n).

**Plan of proof.**
- Part I (§§2–5) presents D by triangular L-matrices (Theorem D).
- Part II (§§6–11) presents S̄_n(𝔅*) by triangular arc matrices (Theorem MS).
- Part III (§§12–13) glues the two presentations.

# Part I. The double

## 2. Cross relations in D, and the quotient

**Proposition 2.1.** In D:
- (a) K̂_iK̂'_j = K̂'_jK̂_i;
- (b) K̂_iF_jK̂_i^{-1} = q^{−δ_ij+δ_{i,j+1}}F_j;
- (c) K̂'_jE_iK̂'^{-1}_j = q^{δ_ij−δ_{j,i+1}}E_i;
- (d) E_iF_j − F_jE_i = δ_ij(K_i − K'^{-1}_i)/(q−q^{-1}).

*Proof.* Apply (X). Pairings between grouplikes are bimultiplicative, so σ(g, h^{-1}) = σ(g,h)^{-1}. Hence σ(K̂_i, K'^{-1}_j) = q^{δ_ij−δ_{i,j+1}} and σ(K_i, K̂'_j) = q^{−δ_ij+δ_{i+1,j}}.

(a) For u = K̂_i and a = K̂'_j, both sides of (X) carry the same scalar factor σ(K̂_i, K̂'_j), and (X) reduces to K̂_iK̂'_j = K̂'_jK̂_i.

(b) Take u = K̂_i and a = F_j, with ΔF_j = F_j⊗1 + K'^{-1}_j⊗F_j.
- The left side is σ(K̂_i, K'^{-1}_j)K̂_iF_j.
- The right side is F_jK̂_i, since σ(K̂_i, F_j) = 0.

(c) Take u = E_i and a = K̂'_j.
- The left side is E_iK̂'_j.
- The right side is σ(K_i, K̂'_j)K̂'_jE_i.

(d) Take u = E_i and a = F_j.
- The left side is δ_ijK_i/(q^{-1}−q) + E_iF_j.
- The right side is F_jE_i + δ_ijK'^{-1}_i/(q^{-1}−q). ∎

Each Cartan copy therefore acts on all the E's and F's with the U_q weights.

**Lemma 2.2 (presentation).** Let 𝒫 be the algebra generated by the generators of U_+ and U_- (with the inverses K̂^{-1}, K̂'^{-1}), subject to the relations of each Borel and the relations of Prop. 2.1. Then 𝒫 ≅ D.

*Proof.*
- 𝒫 → D is well defined and surjective.
- The relations of Prop. 2.1, together with their consequences for K̂^{-1} and K̂'^{-1}, let one move every U_+-generator to the right of every U_--generator. So 𝒫 is spanned by U_-·U_+.
- The composite U_-⊗U_+ → 𝒫 → D is the linear isomorphism of (C5). Hence 𝒫 → D is injective. ∎

**Corollary 2.3.**
- z_i := K̂_iK̂'^{-1}_i is central, and z_1⋯z_n = 1.
- D/⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i : i⟩ ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n), via K̂_i, K̂'_i ↦ K̂_i, E_i ↦ E_i, F_i ↦ F_i.
- ⟨z_i − 1⟩ = ⟨z_i^{-1} − 1⟩ = ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩.

*Proof.*
- **Centrality.** By Prop. 2.1, K̂_i and K̂'_i act with the same weight on every E_j and F_j, and they commute with all Cartan elements. So z_i is central.
- **The quotient.** Adding K̂_i = K̂'_i to the presentation of Lemma 2.2 gives exactly the defining relations of U_q^ext(sl_n). In particular (d) becomes [E_i,F_j] = δ_ij(K_i−K_i^{-1})/(q−q^{-1}).
- **The ideals.** K̂_i − K̂'_i = (z_i − 1)K̂'_i and z^{-1} − 1 = −z^{-1}(z − 1). ∎

## 3. Borel isomorphisms

Let A := O_q(M_n)/J with J = ⟨t_ab : a > b⟩. Let Õ^+ := A[t_ii^{-1}] and O^+ := Õ^+/(∏t_ii − 1).

**Lemma 3.1.** The ordered monomials in the upper letters t_ij (i ≤ j) form a basis of A.

*Proof.*
- **Rewriting rules.** Order letters lexicographically and words by deglex. The O_q(M_n) relations give rewriting rules sending each out-of-order adjacent pair to a combination of smaller words. The only non-monomial rule is t_kl t_ij → t_ij t_kl − ω t_il t_kj (i < k, j < l), and both words on the right are smaller.
- **Termination and spanning.** Rewriting terminates (deglex satisfies DCC on words of fixed length). By (C1) the ordered words form a basis of O_q(M_n).
- **Lower letters are preserved.** If a word contains a lower letter (a > b), every word obtained from it by a rule does too. The monomial rules keep the letters. In the non-monomial rule, if i > j or k > l then k > j, so t_kj is lower.
- **Conclusion.** J is the span of the ordered words containing a lower letter. This span lies in J. Conversely, each element of J is a combination of words containing a lower letter, and these reduce, within the span of such words, to ordered words containing a lower letter. The claim follows from (C1). ∎

**Lemma 3.2.** In A, for k ≤ l, t_jj t_kl = q^{δ_jk−δ_jl}t_kl t_jj, and the t_jj commute.

*Proof.* Check the cases j < k, j = k, k < j < l, j = l and j > l from the four relation types. Every correction term contains t_kj with k > j or t_jl with j > l, which is a lower letter and hence 0. ∎

Hence:
- the t_ii are normal and regular;
- Õ^+ has basis t^a·m, with a ∈ ℤ^n and m an ordered strictly-upper monomial;
- Õ^+ is graded by deg t_ik = ε_i − ε_k;
- (Õ^+)_β is a free module over 𝐑 := k[t_jj^{±1}] of rank P(β), because t_ik ↔ ε_i − ε_k is a bijection with the positive roots.

**Lemma 3.3.** For i < j < l, t_il = ω^{-1}[t_ij, t_jl]t_jj^{-1}. Hence Õ^+ is generated by the t_ii^{±1} and the t_{i,i+1}.

**Theorem 3.4.** Φ: Ũ_+ → Õ^+, K̂_j ↦ t_jj, E_i ↦ ω^{-1}t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}t_{i,i+1}, is an algebra isomorphism. It induces U_+ ≅ O^+. The inverse Ψ is Ψ(t_ii) = K̂_i and Ψ(t_ik) = ωK̂_kE_ik for i < k.

*Proof.* Write e_i := t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}t_{i,i+1}.

*Cartan relations.* These follow from Lemma 3.2.

*Distant indices, |i−j| > 1.* e_i and e_j commute: the relevant correction term contains a lower letter.

*Serre relations.* Take indices 1, 2, 3 and put a = t_12, b = t_23, d_k = t_kk. In A:
- ab − ba = ω t_13 d_2;
- a t_13 = q t_13 a and t_13 b = q b t_13;
- t_13 d_3 = q d_3 t_13;
- d_2 commutes with t_13, and d_3 commutes with a.

Hence f := e_1e_2 − q^{-1}e_2e_1 = ω d_3^{-1}t_13, and e_1f = q fe_1, fe_2 = q e_2f. These are equivalent to the two Serre relations, because e_1²e_2 − [2]e_1e_2e_1 + e_2e_1² = e_1f − qfe_1 and similarly for the other.

*∏K̂.* ∏K̂ ↦ ∏t_jj, which is central, so Φ descends to the quotients.

*Surjectivity.* By Lemma 3.3.

*Injectivity.* Φ is graded and 𝐑-linear (with U^0 ≅ 𝐑). Both graded pieces are free of rank P(β), by (C3) and by Lemma 3.2's consequences. So each graded piece of Φ is a surjective endomorphism of 𝐑^{P(β)}, hence bijective by (C6).

*Formula for Ψ(t_ik).* Apply Ψ to Lemma 3.3 with j = i+1, using:
- K̂_k commutes with E_i;
- K̂_{i+1}^{-1} commutes with E_iE_{i+1,k};
- K̂_{i+1}E_iK̂_{i+1}^{-1} = q^{-1}E_i. ∎

**Lemma 3.5 (coalgebra).** Δ(t_ik) := Σ_{i≤j≤k} t_jk⊗t_ij (the flipped matrix coproduct) is an algebra map on Õ^+. Φ intertwines it with KS's coproduct: Δ(e_i) = e_i⊗t_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}t_ii + 1⊗e_i = (Φ⊗Φ)(E_i⊗K_i + 1⊗E_i). Hence Δ(Ψt_ik) = Σ_jΨt_jk⊗Ψt_ij.

**Lower Borel.**
- Let O^- be built in the same way from lower-triangular s_ij. Transposition τ: s_ij ↦ t_ji is an isomorphism O^- ≅ O^+.
- θ: U_- → U_+, K̂'_j ↦ K̂_j^{-1}, F_i ↦ −q^{-1}E_i, is an algebra isomorphism.
- Φ^- := τ^{-1}Φθ is an isomorphism U_- → O^-, with Φ^-(K̂'_j) = s_jj^{-1} and Φ^-(F_i) = (q^{-1}−q)^{-1}s_{i+1,i}s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.
- Its inverse is s_ii ↦ K̂'^{-1}_i and s_ki ↦ (q^{-1}−q)F_kiK̂'^{-1}_k (k > i), computed from the transposed recursion.
- **Coproduct.** Δ(s_ik) := Σ_j s_jk⊗s_ij is an algebra map on Õ^- (as in Lemma 3.5: it is the coproduct of O_q(M_n)^{cop}, and J^- = ⟨s_ab : a < b⟩ is a coideal, since in Σ_j s_jb⊗s_aj with a < b each term has j < b or a < j; and Δ(s_jj) = s_jj⊗s_jj is grouplike, so it extends to s_jj^{-1}). It is intertwined by Φ^- with KS's coproduct. This cannot be transported through θ, which is not a coalgebra map, so we check it directly on generators. For the grouplikes, Φ^-(K̂'_j) = s_jj^{-1} is grouplike. For f := s_{i+1,i}s_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}, triangularity gives Δ(s_{i+1,i}) = s_ii⊗s_{i+1,i} + s_{i+1,i}⊗s_{i+1,i+1}, hence Δ(f) = s_iis_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}⊗f + f⊗1. Since Φ^-(F_i) = (q^{-1}−q)^{-1}f and Φ^-(K'^{-1}_i) = Φ^-(K̂'^{-1}_iK̂'_{i+1}) = s_iis_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}, this is (Φ^-⊗Φ^-)(F_i⊗1 + K'^{-1}_i⊗F_i) = (Φ^-⊗Φ^-)(ΔF_i). Both coproducts are algebra maps that agree on generators, so they agree everywhere, and Δ(Ψ^-s_ik) = Σ_jΨ^-s_jk⊗Ψ^-s_ij.

Write L^+ := (Ψt_ij) and L^- := (Ψ^-s_ij). These are the matrices of §1.

## 4. The pairing on L-entries and the mixed relation

**Lemma 4.1.** ρ(a,b;c,d) := σ(l^+_ab, l^-_cd) = q^{-1/n}[δ_abδ_cd q^{δ_ac} + ω[a<b]δ_adδ_bc]. Hence

  𝐑 := Σρ(a,b;c,d)e_ab⊗e_cd = q^{-1/n}R_21.

*Proof.*
- **Diagonal entries.** σ(K̂_a, K̂'^{-1}_c) = q^{δ_ac−1/n}.
- **Weight orthogonality.** Grade by deg E_i = α_i, deg F_i = −α_i, deg K̂ = 0. Then σ(u,a) = 0 unless deg u = −deg a. This follows by induction from the axioms of (C4), because σ(g, ·) vanishes on F_j for grouplike g and Δ is graded. So the only off-diagonal values are r_ab := σ(l^+_ab, l^-_ba) with a < b.
- **Base case, b = a+1.**
  - σ(u, xy) with u = K̂_{a+1}E_a, x = F_a, y = K̂'^{-1}_{a+1} equals σ(K̂_{a+1}, K̂'^{-1}_{a+1})σ(K̂_{a+1}E_a, F_a).
  - This is q^{1−1/n}·q^{-1}/(q^{-1}−q).
  - With the normalising factors ω(q^{-1}−q), we get r_{a,a+1} = q^{-1/n}ω.
- **Induction step.**
  - Write l^+_ab = ω^{-1}(l^+_{a,a+1}l^+_{a+1,b} − l^+_{a+1,b}l^+_{a,a+1})(l^+_{a+1,a+1})^{-1}.
  - Pair it with l^-_ba using Δ²(l^-_ba) = Σ l^-_ra⊗l^-_pr⊗l^-_bp.
  - Only the first product contributes, giving r_{a,a+1}r_{a+1,b}σ(K̂_{a+1}^{-1}, K̂'^{-1}_b) = r_{a,a+1}r_{a+1,b}q^{1/n}, since b ≠ a+1.
  - So r_ab = q^{1/n}ω^{-1}(q^{-1/n}ω)² = q^{-1/n}ω. ∎

**Theorem 4.2.** In D, RL^-_1L^+_2 = L^+_2L^-_1R. Also RTT(R) holds for L^+ and for L^- (Theorem 3.4), and ∏l^±_ii = 1.

*Proof.*
- **(X) on L-entries.** For u = l^+_kl and a = l^-_ij, (X) reads Σ_{m,p}ρ(m,l;p,j)l^+_km l^-_ip = Σ_{m,p} l^-_pj l^+_ml ρ(k,m;i,p). This is the ((k,i),(l,j)) entry of L^+_1L^-_2𝐑 = 𝐑L^-_2L^+_1.
- **Removing the scalar.** Drop q^{-1/n}.
- **Conjugating by P.** Conjugation by P keeps the written order of letters: (PMP)_{(ik),(jl)} = M_{(ki),(lj)}. Using PR_21P = R, it gives L^+_2L^-_1R = RL^-_1L^+_2.
- **Numerical check.** For n = 3 these relations, and the explicit formulas of §1, were checked numerically in a faithful-enough representation of D (U_q(sl_3) on (k^3)^{⊗3} tensored with generic scalars). The variant RL^+_1L^-_2 = L^-_2L^+_1R fails. ∎

## 5. Theorem D

Let A_D be the algebra generated by l^+_ij (i ≤ j) and l^-_ij (i ≥ j), with the other entries set to 0, subject to:
- RTT(R) for L^+;
- RTT(R) for L^-;
- RL^-_1L^+_2 = L^+_2L^-_1R;
- ∏l^+_ii = ∏l^-_ii = 1.

**Theorem D.** The map A_D → D sending L^± to the matrices of §1 is an algebra isomorphism.

*Proof.*
- **The diagonals are units in A_D.** The RTT entry ((i,j),(i,j)) gives x_iix_jj − x_jjx_ii = ±ωx_ijx_ji, and this is 0 for a triangular matrix. So the diagonal entries commute, and their product is 1. Hence each is a unit (x_ii^{-1} = ∏_{j≠i}x_jj). The relations on L^+ alone therefore present O^+ ≅ U_+ (Theorem 3.4), and likewise for L^-. (The subalgebra of A_D generated by L^+ is a priori only a quotient of O^+; this is not used.)
- **A_D as a quotient of U_+ ∗ U_-.** Via Ψ, Ψ^-, A_D = (U_+ ∗ U_-)/⟨X(u,a) : u ∈ C_+, a ∈ C_-⟩, where C_± is the span of the entries of L^±. By §4 the entries of the mixed relation are exactly these X(u,a).
- **Closure.** C_± are subcoalgebras (Lemma 3.5) that generate U_± as algebras. Then X(u,a) for u ∈ C_+, a ∈ C_- implies X(u,a) for all u, a:
  - *Products.* From X(u,a) and X(v,a) for all a in a subcoalgebra C, the first axiom of (C4) gives X(uv,a). Products of subcoalgebras are subcoalgebras, so induction on length works, first in u and then symmetrically in a.
  - *Inverse grouplikes.* Inverse grouplikes are not even needed, since t_ii^{-1} = ∏_{j≠i}t_jj.
- **Conclusion.** By (C5), A_D ≅ D. ∎

# Part II. The skein algebra of the punctured bigon

Notation:
- O := S_n(𝔅). By (S1) and Prop. 10.4, x_ij ↦ u_ij is an isomorphism O^{(ε)} ≅ O; we identify the two.
- U = (u_ij). RTT(R^ε) holds for U.
- φ_top, φ_bot: O → S_n(𝔅*) are the algebra maps (S3) induced by embeddings of 𝔅 onto the parts of 𝔅* above and below p, edges into edges.

## 6. Counit

**Lemma 6.1.** ε(u_ij) = δ_ij, where ε is the counit of O. It is an algebra map.

*Proof.* The counit axiom applied to Δu_ij = Σ_k u_ik⊗u_kj gives u_ij = Σ_k ε(u_ik)u_kj. The u_kj are linearly independent. ∎

## 7. Decomposition of the unreduced algebra

**Theorem 7.1.** m: O⊗O → S_n(𝔅*), x⊗y ↦ φ_top(x)φ_bot(y), is a linear isomorphism, for every n ≥ 2.

*Proof of surjectivity.*
- **The cutting path.** Choose z_l ∈ e_l, z_r ∈ e_r and simple paths π_l from z_l to p and π_r from z_r to p, so that π_l ∪ π_r is a simple path from e_l to e_r through p. It cuts 𝔅* into an upper part H_t and a lower part H_b. Each, with its two edge pieces, is a bigon, and we may take φ_top, φ_bot to be onto H_t, H_b.
- **Induction.** For a stated web W in general position, let N(W) = |W ∩ (π_l ∪ π_r)|. We induct on N.
- **N > 0.** Say W meets π_l, and let P be the intersection point nearest z_l along π_l. Push a small arc of the strand through P along [z_l, P] (a finger move). The strand now makes a U-turn adjacent to e_l at z_l, crossing π_l only at its tip. Perturb the tip's height so that it differs from all endpoint heights on e_l. Then (CUT) (S5)(iii) at the tip writes W as a combination of webs with N one smaller.
- **N = 0.** Then W = W_t ⊔ W_b, with W_t ⊂ H_t and W_b ⊂ H_b.
  - On each edge, bubble-sort the heights with (S2): whenever a W_b-endpoint lies immediately above a W_t-endpoint, exchange them. This keeps the diagram, so N = 0 and the splitting are preserved.
  - Afterwards every W_t-endpoint is above every W_b-endpoint on each edge.
  - A vertical isotopy then moves W_t into heights (0,1) and W_b into (−1,0), giving φ_top(x)φ_bot(y).

  Closed loops around p and webs with vertices are covered by the induction, since they have N > 0 or lie in one half.

*Proof of injectivity (explicit left inverse).*
- **The cut.** Choose four disjoint ideal arcs:
  - a_1 from v_t to p and a_2 from p to v_b, both to the left of p;
  - r_1 from v_t to p and r_2 from p to v_b, both to the right of p.

  Cutting along them gives 𝔅_1 ⊔ 𝔅_2 ⊔ T_l ⊔ T_r, where:
  - 𝔅_1 is a bigon with edges a_1, r_1;
  - 𝔅_2 is a bigon with edges a_2, r_2;
  - T_l is a triangle with edges e_l, a_1, a_2;
  - T_r is a triangle with edges r_1, r_2, e_r.

  Let θ be the composite of the four splittings (S4).
- **The map f_l.** Let g_l: T_l → 𝔅 open the corner of T_l at p. It sends e_l into the left edge e_L of 𝔅, and a_1, a_2 onto disjoint subintervals of the right edge e_R, with a_1 nearer the top.

  Choose increasing height reparametrisations σ_z of (−1,1), depending smoothly on z (by a partition of unity):
  - σ_z = id near e_l;
  - σ_z maps into (0,1) near a_1;
  - σ_z maps into (−1,0) near a_2.

  Then f_l(z,h) = (g_l(z), σ_z(h)) is an embedding of thickenings. Over e_R it puts all a_1-endpoints above all a_2-endpoints.

  *Marking repair.* In the marked-3-manifold model, take the marking of e_R to be an upward arc in the wall over e_R that passes through both image segments, running through the gap between g_l(a_1) and g_l(a_2), which lies outside f_l(T_l×(−1,1)). This arc is isotopic in the wall to the standard marking. Alternatively, work in the model where endpoints lie anywhere over an edge at distinct heights.

  So (S3) gives a linear map f_{l*}: S(T_l) → O. Define f_{r*} similarly, merging r_1 above r_2 into the left edge.
- **The left inverse.** Set Ψ := ((ε∘f_{l*}) ⊗ id ⊗ id ⊗ (ε∘f_{r*})) ∘ θ: S_n(𝔅*) → O⊗O.
- **Ψ∘m = id.** Take monomials x = u_{i_1j_1}⋯u_{i_pj_p} and y = u_{i'_1j'_1}⋯.
  - Represent m(x⊗y) by parallel arcs above p (heights in (0,1)) and below p (heights in (−1,0)). Each upper arc crosses a_1 and r_1 once; each lower arc crosses a_2 and r_2 once.
  - θ gives the sum over intermediate states of τ_l ⊗ (u_{k l}-word) ⊗ (u_{k'l'}-word) ⊗ τ_r.
  - f_{l*}(τ_l) is a system of disjoint parallel arcs with the same height orders on both edges, so it is the stacked product u_{i_1k_1}⋯u_{i'_1k'_1}⋯.
  - ε applied to it gives ∏δ_{i_mk_m}∏δ_{i'_mk'_m} (Lemma 6.1), and likewise on the right.
  - Summing over states gives x⊗y. ∎

## 8. Locality: the mixed relation is the bigon's self-exchange relation

**Lemma 8.1.** Let A be the (S2) matrix, at e_l, for two outgoing endpoints, that rewrites the configuration in which the upper-placed endpoint belongs to the arc nearer v_b (as in c_kl b_ij) in terms of the exchanged configuration; equivalently, A is defined by the displayed identities below. Let A' be the corresponding matrix at e_r for two incoming endpoints. Then:
- in O: U_2U_1 = AU_1U_2A';
- in S_n(𝔅*): C_2B_1 = AB_1C_2A' (and B_2B_1 = AB_1B_2A', C_2C_1 = AC_1C_2A').

*Proof.* Consider c_klb_ij, in which the c-arc is on top.
- On e_l the two endpoints are consecutive. Exchange them with (S2): every resulting web has the same disjoint diagrams, with the b-arc now higher on e_l.
- Do the same on e_r. Now the b-arc is higher on both edges, so each web is a product b_{i'j'}c_{k'l'}.

The coefficients depend only on the local data (states, orientations, height order), so they are the same as for two parallel arcs u_kl u_ij in 𝔅. The entry convention (Y_2X_1)_{(ik),(jl)} = y_kl x_ij turns this into the matrix identities. ∎

**Lemma 8.2.** A' = A^{-1}, and A = R^ε(α·1 + β·PR^ε) for some scalars α, β.

*Proof.*
- **A' = A^{-1}.** Apply the counit entrywise to U_2U_1 = AU_1U_2A'. The left side gives δ_klδ_ij = 1_{(ik),(jl)}, and the right side gives (AA')_{(ik),(jl)}. So AA' = 1, and A' = A^{-1}.
- **Commutation.** RTT(R^ε) gives U_2U_1 = R^εU_1U_2R^{-ε}. Hence X := R^{-ε}A satisfies XU_1U_2 = U_1U_2X.
- **Conclusion.** By (S6), X ∈ span{1, PR^ε}. (Equivalently, A^{-1}R^ε = X^{-1} commutes too, since the commutant is a finite-dimensional algebra.) ∎

**Block form.** Fix i > k and the basis f_1 = (i,k), f_2 = (k,i). Then:
- R = [[1,ω],[0,1]], R^{-1} = [[1,−ω],[0,1]];
- R_21 = [[1,0],[ω,1]], R_21^{-1} = [[1,0],[−ω,1]];
- P = [[0,1],[1,0]], PR^ε = [[0,1],[1,εω]].

Hence

  A = [[α+εωβ, β+εω(α+εωβ)], [β, α+εωβ]],

so A_{(ki),(ik)} = β and A_{(ik),(ki)} = β + εω(α+εωβ). On the pair (i,i), A acts by q^ε(α+βq^ε).

The family contains R^ε, at (α,β) = (1,0), and R_21^{-ε}, at (α,β) = (1+ω², −εω). The latter was checked symbolically for n = 2, 3 and both ε.

## 9. The triangle argument: compatibility is forced

**Set-up.** Let P_3 be the ideal triangle with vertices v_1, v_2, v_3 and edges e = v_1v_2, e_1 = v_1v_3, e_2 = v_2v_3. Draw e vertical on the left with v_1 at the top and v_3 to the right.

By (S3) there are algebra maps:
- φ_1: O → S_n(P_3), sending (𝔅, v_t, e_l, e_r) to (P_3, v_1, e, e_1). Write α_st := φ_1(u_st), a ccw corner arc at v_1 with tail state s on e and head state t on e_1.
- φ_2: O → S_n(P_3), sending (𝔅, v_b, e_l, e_r) to (P_3, v_2, e, e_2). Write β_στ := φ_2(u_στ), a cw corner arc at v_2 with tail σ and head τ.

**Lemma 9.1.** In S_n(P_3), β_στα_st = Σ_{s',σ'}A_{(sσ),(s'σ')}α_{s't}β_{σ'τ}.

*Proof.* The endpoints on e_1 and e_2 lie on different edges, so only the height exchange at e is involved. Its local type is that of e_l in Lemma 8.1: two outgoing endpoints, and the endpoint of α is nearer v_1. ∎

**Lemma 9.2 (diagonals are units).** Let Θ be a strict triangle and J_Θ the ideal of O generated by the u_st with (s,t) ∈ Θ. In O/J_Θ:
- the u_ii commute, because the RTT entry ((i,j),(i,j)) gives u_iiu_jj − u_jju_ii = ±ωu_iju_ji ∈ J_Θ;
- ∏u_ii = 1, because det_q U = 1 (Prop. 10.4) and every σ ≠ id contributes a letter in Θ.

So each u_ii is a unit in O/J_Θ. Hence α_ss and β_ss are units in S̄_n(P_3): by (Y1), φ_1(J_{F_ccw}) and φ_2(J_{F_cw}) lie in the bad ideal.

**Proposition 9.3.**
- (i) If F_ccw = {t<h}, then A = αR^ε with α ≠ 0, and F_cw = {t>h}.
- (ii) If F_ccw = {t>h}, then A = α'R_21^{-ε} with α' ≠ 0, and F_cw = {t<h}.

*Proof.* Work in S̄ := S̄_n(P_3), which is nonzero by (Y2).

(i), first claim. Take s < σ, and apply Lemma 9.1 with t = σ, τ = s. Row (s,σ) of A is supported on (s,σ) and (σ,s), so

  β_σs α_sσ = A_{(sσ),(sσ)}α_sσβ_σs + β·α_σσβ_ss.

- α_sσ is bad, so the left side and the first term on the right vanish.
- So β·α_σσβ_ss = 0, and α_σσβ_ss is a unit. Hence β = 0, since otherwise 1 = 0 in S̄.
- So A = αR^ε, with α ≠ 0 because A is invertible.

(i), second claim. Suppose F_cw = {t<h}. Take k < p, so β_kp is bad. Lemma 9.1 with (s,t,σ,τ) = (p,k,k,p) gives

  0 = A_{(pk),(pk)}α_pkβ_kp + A_{(pk),(kp)}α_kkβ_pp.

- The first term vanishes because β_kp is bad.
- A_{(pk),(kp)} = εαω ≠ 0.
- So α_kkβ_pp = 0, which gives 1 = 0, a contradiction.

(ii), first claim. Take s > σ, so α_sσ is bad. The same identity gives A_{(sσ),(σs)} = 0, that is, εωα + (1+ω²)β = 0. This is one nontrivial linear condition on (α,β). R_21^{-ε} satisfies it, so A = α'R_21^{-ε}.

(ii), second claim. Suppose F_cw = {t>h}. Take k > p. Then A_{(pk),(kp)} = −εα'ω ≠ 0 gives a contradiction as before. ∎

**Corollary 9.4 (mixed relation).** Recall that b_ij is ccw at v_t (tail i, head j) and c_kl is cw at v_b (tail k, head l).
- **Case (i), F_ccw = {t<h}.**
  - The bad b's are those with i < j, so B is lower triangular in S̄_n(𝔅*). The bad c's are those with k > l, so C is upper triangular.
  - C_2B_1 = R^εB_1C_2R^{-ε}, that is, R^εX_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1R^ε with X = B and Y = C.
- **Case (ii), F_ccw = {t>h}.**
  - B is upper triangular and C lower triangular.
  - C_2B_1 = R_21^{-ε}B_1C_2R_21^{ε}. Conjugating by P, with P(C_2B_1)P = C_1B_2, P(B_1C_2)P = B_2C_1 and PR_21P = R, gives R^εC_1B_2 = B_2C_1R^ε, that is, R^εX_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1R^ε with X = C and Y = B.

In both cases X is lower triangular, Y is upper triangular, and both satisfy RTT(R^ε).

**Support property.** R^{±1}_{(ik),(i'k')} ≠ 0 forces i' ≤ i and k' ≥ k, and R^{∓1}_{(j'l'),(jl)} ≠ 0 forces j' ≥ j and l' ≤ l. So the rewriting y_kl x_ij = Σ R^ε_{(ik),(i'k')} x_{i'j'} y_{k'l'} R^{-ε}_{(j'l'),(jl)} sends a bad x-letter (i < j) to bad x-letters, and a bad y-letter (k > l) to bad y-letters.

*Remark (one-sided reading of (Y1); conditional on (S7)).* Suppose LY's rule declares bad only the corner arcs of **one** turning direction, with bad set a strict triangle F for that direction, and no corner arc of the other direction. Then the conclusions of Prop. 9.3 (first claims), Cor. 9.4 and Prop. 10.5 still hold, with the "effective" bad set of the other direction being the complementary strict triangle. The argument uses (S7) and Lemma 10.3 below.

- **S(J_Θ) = J_Θ for a strict triangle Θ.** Lemma 10.3 gives S(u_xy) ∈ J_Θ for (x,y) ∈ Θ. The same back-substitution argument in the opposite algebra (O/J_Θ)^op gives S^{-1}(u_xy) ∈ J_Θ: there U is still triangular with unit diagonal, the Hopf identity Σ_k u_kj S^{-1}(u_ik) = δ_ij says that the image of S^{-1}(U) is a left inverse of U in the opposite algebra, and a left inverse equals the two-sided triangular inverse. Since S and S^{-1} are anti-automorphisms, they map the ideal generated by a set onto the ideal generated by its image. So S(J_Θ) ⊆ J_Θ and S^{-1}(J_Θ) ⊆ J_Θ, hence S(J_Θ) = J_Θ.
- **Reversed corner arcs generate the complementary ideal.** Take a corner arc of the non-bad direction at a vertex v, given as φ(u_xy) for the relevant embedding φ of the bigon. The reversed corner arcs at v, φ(ū_ab), have the bad direction; φ(ū_ab) is bad iff (tail, head) = (b, a) ∈ F. By (S7), φ(ū_ab) = λ_ab φ(S(u_{b̄ā})). The map (a,b) ↦ (b̄, ā) carries {(a,b) : (b,a) ∈ F} onto the strict triangle Θ' complementary to F: if F = {t<h}, then b < a gives b̄ > ā, and if F = {t>h}, then b̄ < ā. So the bad reversed arcs at v generate the ideal generated by φ(S(J_{Θ'}))=φ(J_{Θ'}).
- **Consequences.** In P_3 the effective bad sets are F_ccw and F_cw, one of which is F and the other complementary to it. So Lemma 9.2 applies to both α and β, and the first claims of Prop. 9.3 go through verbatim; only the compatible cases (i) and (ii) arise. In 𝔅*, the LY bad ideal is generated by the bad arcs of the bad direction at v_t and v_b, and by the previous bullet it equals the ideal I_f generated by the bad b_ij and bad c_ij of Cor. 9.4. That is the conclusion of Prop. 10.5, so Theorem MS and the Main Theorem hold unchanged.

This remark is the only place where (S7) is used.

## 10. Reversed arcs and the determinant

**Lemma 10.1 (edge weights).** Let Λ = ℤ^n/ℤ(1,…,1), with images ε_i of the basis vectors. For a web α and an edge e, set wt_e(α) = Σ w(x) over the endpoints x on e, where w(x) = ε_{s(x)} if x is a tail and w(x) = −ε_{\overline{s(x)}} if x is a head. Then every defining relation of S_n is homogeneous, so S_n(Σ) is graded and the grading is additive under stacking.

*Proof.*
- Interior relations do not change endpoints.
- A returning arc has weight ε_i − ε_{j̄}, which is 0 exactly when j = ī, which is exactly when (S5)(i) is nonzero.
- A vertex near the boundary has weight 0 on permutation states.
- In (CUT), each term on the right has one head and one tail with states {i, ī}, so weight 0, and so does the left side.
- Derived relations, such as (S2), are then automatically homogeneous. ∎

In O, set D := wt_{e_l} − φ(wt_{e_r}), where φ(ε_c) = −ε_c̄. Then:
- D(u_ij) = ε_i − ε_j;
- for the reversed arc ū_ab (oriented from e_r to e_l, state a on e_l, state b on e_r), D(ū_ab) = ε_b̄ − ε_ā.

**Proposition 10.2.** If a < b then ū_ab ∈ J_+. If a > b then ū_ab ∈ J_-.

*Proof.* J_+ is homogeneous. O/J_+ is spanned by monomials in the u_ij with i ≥ j, so it is concentrated in degrees in Q_- = −ℕ{ε_k − ε_{k+1}}. For a < b, D(ū_ab) ∈ Q_+∖{0}, and Q_+ ∩ Q_- = 0. So ū_ab maps to 0 in O/J_+. The case of J_- is symmetric. ∎

**Lemma 10.3.** For a strict triangle Θ, S(u_xy) ∈ J_Θ for (x,y) ∈ Θ.

*Proof.* In O/J_Θ, U is triangular with unit diagonal entries (Lemma 9.2). Back substitution gives a two-sided inverse W of the same shape. The image V of S(U) satisfies VU = 1, so V = VUW = W. ∎

Lemma 10.3 is used only in the Remark after Cor. 9.4 (the one-sided reading of (Y1)).

**Proposition 10.4 (normalisation).** In O, det_q U = 1 exactly; more precisely, c^n = 1 in (S1), so x_ij ↦ u_ij is itself an isomorphism O^{(ε)} ≅ O. Hence ∏b_ii = ∏c_ii = 1 in S̄_n(𝔅*).

*Proof.*
- By (S1), u_ij = c^{-1}·(image of x_ij), and det_q is homogeneous of degree n, so det_q U = c^{-n}·(image of det_q X) = c^{-n} is a scalar.
- Applying the algebra map ε gives c^{-n} = ε(det_q U) = Σ_σ(−q)^{ℓ(σ)}∏_iδ_{iσ(i)} = 1, by Lemma 6.1. This holds for any quantum-determinant expression whose identity term has coefficient 1.
- So c^n = 1. Then x_ij ↦ c·x_ij is an automorphism of O^{(ε)} (RTT is homogeneous and det_q is multiplied by c^n = 1). Composing with it, x_ij ↦ u_ij itself is an isomorphism O^{(ε)} ≅ O, and det_q U = 1 exactly.
- In the reduced algebra the products follow from Lemma 9.2 transported by φ_top and φ_bot. ∎

**Proposition 10.5 (the bad ideal of 𝔅*).** The LY bad-arc ideal I_LY of S_n(𝔅*) equals the ideal I_f generated by the bad b_ij and the bad c_ij.

*Proof.*
- **Which corner arcs occur.** The corner arcs at v_t are, up to isotopy, the arcs b_ij = φ_top(u_ij) and b̄_ab := φ_top(ū_ab). The arc b̄_ab is cw at v_t, with tail b and head a. Likewise the corner arcs at v_b are c_kl and c̄_ab := φ_bot(ū_ab), the latter ccw with tail b and head a.
- **Case (i)** (F_ccw = {t<h}, F_cw = {t>h}, Prop. 9.3).
  - b̄_ab is bad iff b > a. Then ū_ab ∈ J_+ (Prop. 10.2), and φ_top(J_+) is the ideal generated by bad b's.
  - c̄_ab is bad iff b < a. Then ū_ab ∈ J_-, and φ_bot(J_-) is generated by bad c's.
- **Case (ii)** is identical with the inequalities reversed. ∎

*Remark.* A second argument, conditional on the exact shapes of the U-turn relations, gives ū_ab = λ_ab S(u_{b̄ā}) with λ_ab ≠ 0, and then uses Lemma 10.3. The weight argument above needs only the pairing i ↔ ī in (S5)(i).

## 11. Theorem MS (presentation of S̄_n(𝔅*))

For 𝓡 = R^ε let P(𝓡) be the algebra generated by x_ij (i ≥ j) and y_ij (i ≤ j), with the other entries set to 0, subject to:
- RTT(𝓡) for X;
- RTT(𝓡) for Y;
- 𝓡X_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1𝓡;
- ∏x_ii = ∏y_ii = 1.

As in Theorem D, the diagonal entries commute and are units.

**Theorem MS.** Let (Z, W) = (B, C) in case (i) and (Z, W) = (C, B) in case (ii). Then:
- in S̄_n(𝔅*), z_ij = 0 for i < j and w_ij = 0 for i > j;
- g: P(R^ε) → S̄_n(𝔅*), x_ij ↦ z_ij, y_ij ↦ w_ij, is an algebra isomorphism.

*Proof.* We give case (i) in detail. Case (ii) is the same after the renaming of Cor. 9.4, with one change: there Z = C and W = B, so the composite g∘f of step 5 is x⊗y ↦ φ_bot(x)φ_top(y), with the c-word *above* the b-word, which is not the map m of Theorem 7.1. One needs instead that m': x⊗y ↦ φ_bot(x)φ_top(y) is a linear isomorphism O⊗O → S_n(𝔅*). The proof of Theorem 7.1 applies verbatim with the roles of the two halves reversed: in the surjectivity proof, bubble-sort so that every W_b-endpoint lies above every W_t-endpoint on each edge and then move W_b into heights (0,1) and W_t into (−1,0); in the injectivity proof, choose σ_z mapping into (0,1) near a_2 and into (−1,0) near a_1 (and similarly near r_2, r_1), so that over the merged edge the a_2-endpoints lie above the a_1-endpoints, matching the height order of m'(x⊗y), in which the lower (c-)arcs are on top. The left-inverse computation is then identical. In step 1 one uses V := m'(J_+⊗O + O⊗J_-), where now φ_bot(J_+) is generated by the bad c's and φ_top(J_-) by the bad b's.

1. **The ideal.** Let V := m(J_+⊗O + O⊗J_-). It contains the generators of I_f and lies inside I_f. It is a two-sided ideal:
   - left multiplication by b's and right multiplication by c's are clear;
   - to multiply on the left by a c-letter or on the right by a b-letter, move the letter through the word with the mixed rewriting. By the support property this keeps bad letters bad, and J_± are spanned by words containing a bad letter.

   So V = I_f = I_LY (Prop. 10.5). By Theorem 7.1, m induces a linear isomorphism (O/J_+)⊗(O/J_-) ≅ S̄_n(𝔅*).
2. **The Borel quotients.** O/J_+ ≅ P_-, the algebra of a lower-triangular T with RTT(R^ε) and ∏t_ii = 1. The maps P_- → O/J_+ and O → P_- are well defined:
   - for the first, ∏u_ii ≡ det_q U = 1 mod J_+;
   - for the second, det_q T = ∏t_ii for triangular T.

   They are mutually inverse. Similarly O/J_- ≅ P_+.
3. **Well-definedness of g.**
   - RTT holds because φ_top and φ_bot are algebra maps.
   - The mixed relation is Cor. 9.4.
   - Triangularity holds by the definition of S̄.
   - The determinants are Prop. 10.4.
4. **The linear map f.** Let f: P_-⊗P_+ → P(R^ε), x⊗y ↦ xy. It is surjective: the mixed relation moves y's to the right of x's, and x_ii^{-1} = ∏_{j≠i}x_jj.
5. **Bijectivity.** g∘f is the isomorphism of step 1 composed with step 2, so it is bijective. Hence f is injective, so f is bijective, and therefore g is bijective. ∎

# Part III. Gluing, and proof of the Main Theorem

## 12. From P(R^{-1}) to A_D

**Lemma 12.1.** There is an algebra isomorphism P(R^{-1}) ≅ A_D given by X ↦ (L^-)^{-1}, Y ↦ (L^+)^{-1}.

*Proof.*
- **Triangular inverses.** In any algebra, a triangular matrix whose diagonal entries are units has a two-sided inverse of the same shape. Write it as Δ(1+N) with N strictly triangular, hence nilpotent; the inverse is (1+N)^{-1}Δ^{-1}, where (1+N)^{-1} = Σ_k(−N)^k is a finite sum. Its diagonal entries are the inverses of the original diagonal entries.
- **Units in A_D and P(R^{-1}).** In A_D the l^±_ii are units (Theorem D). In P(R^{-1}) the x_ii and y_ii are units. Indeed, RTT(R^{-1}) is equivalent, by P-conjugation, to RTT(R_21), which gives the O_q(M_n) relations with the index order reversed. So x_iix_jj − x_jjx_ii = ±ωx_ijx_ji = 0, and the product is 1.
- **Inverting relations.** Products such as X_1Y_2 are genuine products in M_{n²}(algebra), and (X^{-1})_1 = (X_1)^{-1}.
  - Inverting R^{-1}X_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1R^{-1} gives Y_2^{-1}X_1^{-1}R = RX_1^{-1}Y_2^{-1}.
  - Inverting RTT(R^{-1}) gives RTT(R) for the inverse matrices.
  - The products of the diagonal entries of the inverses are 1.
- **Conclusion.** So X ↦ (L^-)^{-1}, Y ↦ (L^+)^{-1} and L^- ↦ X^{-1}, L^+ ↦ Y^{-1} are well-defined algebra maps. They are mutually inverse on generators, because algebra maps send matrix inverses to matrix inverses.
- **Numerical check.** For n = 3, the matrices (L^±)^{-1} in D were checked numerically to satisfy RTT(R^{-1}) and R^{-1}(L^-)^{-1}_1(L^+)^{-1}_2 = (L^+)^{-1}_2(L^-)^{-1}_1R^{-1}. ∎

## 13. Proof of the Main Theorem

**Part (1).**
- By Theorem MS, S̄_n(𝔅*) ≅ P(R^ε) via T_low = Z ↔ X and T_up = W ↔ Y.
- If ε = +1, P(R) = A_D literally, with X = L^-, Y = L^+, and Theorem D gives A_D ≅ D.
- If ε = −1, Lemma 12.1 gives P(R^{-1}) ≅ A_D, and Theorem D again gives A_D ≅ D.
- The composite is the map Φ of the Table in §1. The inverse in case A comes from Theorem 3.4 and its lower analogue.

**Part (2).**
- In every case Φ(b_ii) and Φ(c_ii) are K̂'^{∓1}_i and K̂_i^{±1}, in some order. These commute (Prop. 2.1(a)), so Φ(b_iic_ii) = z_i^{±1}.
- Hence Φ(I) = ⟨z_i^{±1} − 1⟩ = ⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩ (Cor. 2.3).
- Therefore S̄_n(𝔅*)/I ≅ D/⟨K̂_i − K̂'_i⟩ ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n) (Cor. 2.3).
- Explicitly, in case A the composite S̄_n(𝔅*)/I → U_q^ext(sl_n) is: b_ii ↦ K̂_i^{-1}, c_ii ↦ K̂_i, c_{i,i+1} ↦ (q−q^{-1})K̂_{i+1}E_i and b_{i+1,i} ↦ (q^{-1}−q)F_iK̂^{-1}_{i+1}. ∎

## 15. Status

**Main claim.** The main claim is the Main Theorem of §1: parts (1) and (2) of the problem, for all n ≥ 2, over a field of characteristic 0 containing q^{1/n}, with q not a root of unity. It is **proved, modulo the cited foundational results** listed in §0.4. The algebraic chain (Parts I–III) is complete and written out in full. Its skein-theoretic premises are quoted from Lê–Sikora and Lê–Yu and are not re-derived here. The answer to (1) is the explicit map Φ of the Table in §1 (in the expected convention, case A: b_ij ↦ l^-_ij, c_ij ↦ l^+_ij). The answer to (2) is S̄_n(𝔅*)/I ≅ U_q^ext(sl_n), induced by Φ, with b_iic_ii ↦ (K̂_iK̂'^{-1}_i)^{±1}.

**Remaining caveats (stated plainly).**

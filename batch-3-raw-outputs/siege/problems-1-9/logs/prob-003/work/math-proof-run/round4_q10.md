## 1. Unreduced decomposition (task item 1)
**Proposition 1.** The map $m:\mathcal O\otimes\mathcal O\to\mathcal S_n(\mathfrak B^*)$, $x\otimes y\mapsto\phi_{top}(x)\phi_{bot}(y)$, is a linear isomorphism.

*Proof (modulo S2).* Start with two bigons $\mathfrak B_1\sqcup\mathfrak B_2$. Fuse their left edges, then fuse the resulting right edges. The result is a disk with one interior puncture and two boundary ideal points, i.e. $\mathfrak B^*$. In it, $\mathfrak B_1$ sits above the puncture and $\mathfrak B_2$ below.

$\mathcal S_n(\mathfrak B_1\sqcup\mathfrak B_2)=\mathcal O\otimes\mathcal O$. On an element $x\otimes y$, the composite fusion map places the endpoints of $x$ above those of $y$ on both new edges. That is exactly the stacked product $\phi_{top}(x)\phi_{bot}(y)$. By S2 the composite is a linear isomorphism.

This proves both surjectivity and injectivity. In particular every element of $\mathcal S_n(\mathfrak B^*)$, including reversed arcs, webs with vertices and loops around the puncture, is a combination of words $b\cdots b\,c\cdots c$, using S1 for each factor. $\square$

## 2. Bad arcs (task item 2)
A corner arc at $v_t$ is isotopic into a small neighbourhood of $v_t$, which lies in $\phi_{top}(\mathfrak B)$. Moreover, $\phi_{top}$ carries corner arcs at the top vertex of $\mathfrak B$ bijectively, with their states and both orientations, onto corner arcs at $v_t$. The same holds for $v_b$ and $\phi_{bot}$.

So the bad-arc ideal is $\mathcal I=\big(\phi_{top}(J_t)\cup\phi_{bot}(J_b)\big)$. Here $J_t$ (resp. $J_b$) is the ideal of $\mathcal O$ generated by the bad corner arcs at the top (resp. bottom) vertex of $\mathfrak B$, with both orientations.

**Lemma 2.** Fix the convention under which the bad left-to-right arcs at the top vertex are the $u_{ij}$ with $i<j$; the other convention is the transpose. Then $J_t=J_+:=(u_{ij}:i<j)$. Likewise $J_b=J_-:=(u_{ij}:i>j)$, or the transposed pair.

*Proof.*
- $J_+$ is a Hopf ideal. Since $\Delta u_{ij}=\sum_k u_{ik}\otimes u_{kj}$ and $i<j$ forces $i<k$ or $k<j$, we get $\Delta(J_+)\subset J_+\otimes\mathcal O+\mathcal O\otimes J_+$. Also $\varepsilon(J_+)=0$.
- The quotient $\mathcal O/J_+$ is spanned by products of entries of a triangular matrix whose determinant is the product of its diagonal entries (see §4). The antipode descends to that quotient as the matrix inverse, which is again triangular of the same shape. Hence $S(J_+)\subset J_+$.
- A reversed corner arc is, by Lê–Sikora's relation (S1) expressing a reversed arc through $n-1$ parallel arcs and a vertex, a scalar times an entry of $S(U)$ (a quantum cofactor). A bad reversed arc corresponds to an entry $S(U)_{ij}$ in the bad triangle, and that entry lies in $J_+$.

## 3. The bad ideal equals $V$ (task item 3)
Let $X=(b_{ij})$ and $Y=(c_{ij})$; the other labelling is symmetric. Assume the mixed relation
$$RX_1Y_2=Y_2X_1R,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad y_{kl}x_{ij}=\sum R_{(ik),(i'k')}\,x_{i'j'}\,y_{k'l'}\,R^{-1}_{(j'l'),(jl)}.\tag{M}$$
Assume also that the bad entries of $X$ are $i<j$ and the bad entries of $Y$ are $k>l$, so that $X$ becomes lower and $Y$ upper triangular.

**Lemma 3 (index movement). PROVED.**
1. **Support of $R$.** $R_{(ik),(i'k')}\ne0$ only if $(i',k')=(i,k)$, or $i>k$ and $(i',k')=(k,i)$.
2. **Support of $R^{-1}$.** From the Hecke relation $\check R^{-1}=\check R-(q-q^{-1})$ we get $R^{-1}=R_{21}-(q-q^{-1})P$, that is,
$$R^{-1}=q^{-1}\sum e_{aa}\otimes e_{aa}+\sum_{a\ne b}e_{aa}\otimes e_{bb}-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{a>b}e_{ab}\otimes e_{ba},$$
which has the same support as $R$. So $R^{-1}_{(j'l'),(jl)}\ne0$ only if $(j',l')=(j,l)$, or $j'>l'$ and $(j,l)=(l',j')$.
3. **Consequences for nonzero terms of (M).** From item 1: $i'\le i$ and $k'\ge k$. From item 2: $j'\ge j$ and $l'\le l$.
4. **Bad entries stay bad.** If $i<j$ then $i'\le i<j\le j'$, so $x_{i'j'}$ is bad. If $k>l$ then $k'\ge k>l\ge l'$, so $y_{k'l'}$ is bad.

The other admissible forms behave the same way: $R^{-1}$ in place of $R$ has the same support, and the $R_{21}^{\pm1}$ forms transpose the roles of the two triangles. This is the robustness statement in the query.

**Proposition 4. PROVED, given (M).** The space $V:=m(J_+\otimes\mathcal O+\mathcal O\otimes J_-)$ is a two-sided ideal, and $V=\mathcal I$.

*Proof.*
- **$V$ contains the generators.** It contains $\phi_{top}(J_+)=m(J_+\otimes1)$ and $\phi_{bot}(J_-)$.
- **$V\subset\mathcal I$.** Every element of $V$ is a sum of products in which a factor lies in $\phi_{top}(J_+)$ or $\phi_{bot}(J_-)$.
- **Multiplication on the "easy" sides.** By Proposition 1 every element is a sum of terms $\phi_{top}(x)\phi_{bot}(y)$, and $\mathcal O$ is generated by the $u_{ij}$ (S1). So it suffices to check multiplication by the $b$'s and $c$'s. Left multiplication by $b$'s and right multiplication by $c$'s preserve $V$ trivially, because $J_\pm$ are ideals.
- **Left multiplication by $c_{kl}$.**
  - On $m(J_+\otimes\mathcal O)$: write an element of $J_+$ as a combination of monomials that contain a bad letter. Move $c_{kl}$ rightwards through the $b$-word one letter at a time using (M). Each step produces only $b$-letters and $c$-letters, and by Lemma 3 a bad $b$-letter is replaced only by bad $b$-letters. So the result lies in $m(J_+\otimes\mathcal O)$.
  - On $m(\mathcal O\otimes J_-)$: moving $c_{kl}$ through the $b$-word produces $b$-words times $c$'s, followed by an element of $J_-$. Since $J_-$ is an ideal, this lies in $m(\mathcal O\otimes J_-)$.
- **Right multiplication by $b_{ij}$.** This is symmetric. Move $b_{ij}$ leftwards through the $c$-word with (M). By Lemma 3 bad $c$-letters stay bad, and a $b$-word in $J_+$ stays in $J_+$.

Hence $V$ is an ideal containing the generators of $\mathcal I$, so $V=\mathcal I$. $\square$

## 4. Presentations (task item 4)
**Lemma 6. PROVED.** Let $P_-$ be the algebra generated by $t_{ij}$, $i\ge j$ (a lower-triangular matrix $T$), with relations $RT_1T_2=T_2T_1R$ (the full matrix identity with zeros above the diagonal), $t_{ii}$ invertible, and $\prod t_{ii}=1$. Then $\mathcal O/J_+\cong P_-$ via $u_{ij}\mapsto t_{ij}$ for $i\ge j$ and $u_{ij}\mapsto 0$ for $i<j$. The same holds for $\mathcal O/J_-$ and the upper-triangular version $P_+$.

*Proof.*
- **Facts in $\mathcal O/J_+$.**
  - $\det_qU=\sum_\sigma(-q)^{\ell(\sigma)}u_{1\sigma1}\cdots u_{n\sigma n}$ reduces to $\prod u_{ii}$, since every $\sigma\ne\mathrm{id}$ has some $\sigma(i)>i$. So $\prod u_{ii}=1$.
  - The RTT relation for the pair $(ii),(jj)$ gives $u_{ii}u_{jj}-u_{jj}u_{ii}=\pm(q-q^{-1})u_{ij}u_{ji}\in J_+$, so the diagonal entries commute modulo $J_+$.
  - Hence $u_{ii}^{-1}=\prod_{j\ne i}u_{jj}$ in $\mathcal O/J_+$.
- **Map $P_-\to\mathcal O/J_+$.** It exists, because the RTT relations, the invertibility of the diagonal and $\prod t_{ii}=1$ all hold in $\mathcal O/J_+$. It is surjective.
- **Map $\mathcal O\to P_-$.** Send $u_{ij}\mapsto t_{ij}$, or $0$ for $i<j$. It respects RTT, since that is literally the defining relation of $P_-$. It respects $\det_q=1$, since in $P_-$ the quantum determinant of a triangular $T$ is $\prod t_{ii}=1$. It kills $J_+$.
- **Conclusion.** The two induced maps are mutually inverse on generators. $\square$

- generators: the entries of a lower-triangular $X$ (the $b$'s) and an upper-triangular $Y$ (the $c$'s);
- relations: RTT for $X$ and for $Y$, the mixed relation $RX_1Y_2=Y_2X_1R$, invertible diagonal entries, and $\prod x_{ii}=\prod y_{ii}=1$.

The inverses are $b_{ii}^{-1}=\prod_{j\ne i}b_{jj}$ and $c_{ii}^{-1}=\prod_{j\ne i}c_{jj}$.

*Proof.* Let $P$ be the presented algebra.
- **The map $g:P\to\bar{\mathcal S}$.** It exists because:
  - RTT holds since $\phi_{top}$ and $\phi_{bot}$ are algebra maps;
  - the mixed relation holds by (M);
  - the remaining relations hold by Lemma 6.

  $g$ is surjective by Proposition 1.
- **The map $f:P_-\otimes P_+\to P$.** Define $f(x\otimes y)=xy$. It is surjective: the mixed relation rewrites any $yx$ as $\sum x'y'$, and the inverses of diagonal entries are products of generators.
- **Conclusion.** $g\circ f$ is the linear isomorphism of Corollary 5 combined with Lemma 6. So $f$ is injective, hence bijective, and therefore $g$ is bijective. $\square$

## 1. Reduction lemma (proved, given the robustness facts in the query)
Robustness facts recalled: for a mixed relation $Y_2X_1=A X_1Y_2A'$, the span of monomials containing a bad entry is stable under rewriting in exactly these cases:
- $(A,A')\in\{(R,R^{-1}),(R^{-1},R)\}$ requires $X$ lower and $Y$ upper;
- $(A,A')\in\{(R_{21}^{-1},R_{21}),(R_{21},R_{21}^{-1})\}$ requires $X$ upper and $Y$ lower.

**Lemma.** Suppose two things hold.
- (i) The skein mixed relation has the form $\mathcal R\,B_1C_2=C_2B_1\,\mathcal R$ with $\mathcal R\in\{R,R^{-1},R_{21},R_{21}^{-1}\}$.
- (ii) The bad-arc ideal is generated by the strictly upper entries of one of $B,C$ and the strictly lower entries of the other, and it is a "compatible" ideal, so that the reduced algebra is the quotient by the span of monomials containing a bad entry. This is needed for the reduced algebra to have the correct size (GK dimension $n^2+n-2$).

Then $\bar{\mathcal S}_n(\mathfrak B^*)$ is presented by a lower-triangular matrix $X$ and an upper-triangular matrix $Y$, where $\{X,Y\}=\{B,C\}$. The relations are:
- triangular RTT for $X$ and for $Y$;
- the mixed relation $\mathcal R'X_1Y_2=Y_2X_1\mathcal R'$ with $\mathcal R'\in\{R,R^{-1}\}$;
- $\prod x_{ii}=\prod y_{ii}=1$.

*Proof.*
1. If $\mathcal R\in\{R,R^{-1}\}$, compatibility forces $X=B$ lower and $Y=C$ upper.
2. If $\mathcal R\in\{R_{21},R_{21}^{-1}\}$, rewrite the relation as $C_2B_1=\mathcal R B_1C_2\mathcal R^{-1}$. Compatibility forces $B$ upper and $C$ lower. Now conjugate by the flip $P$: $P\mathcal R_{12}P=\mathcal R_{21}$ and $P(B_1C_2)P=B_2C_1$. The relation becomes $\mathcal R_{21}B_2C_1=C_1B_2\mathcal R_{21}$. Here $\mathcal R_{21}\in\{R,R^{-1}\}$, $X=C$ and $Y=B$.
3. RTT for a single matrix holds with $R$ if and only if it holds with $R_{21}^{-1}$ (Hecke relation). So each matrix also satisfies RTT with $\mathcal R'$.
4. The linear basis comes from (S2) together with (ii): it is (ordered monomials) $\otimes$ (ordered monomials), with no further relations.
5. The determinant: in $\mathcal O_q(SL_n)$ we have $\det_q=1$. Modulo the off-diagonal entries on one side, $\det_q\equiv\prod t_{ii}$, because every non-identity permutation term contains such an entry.

This gives the stated presentation. $\square$


## The planner's sketch of why MS should hold (a suggestion only — discard it if you see a better route)

**Why it should hold (my sketch).**
1. Decomposition (S2): cut B* along the ideal arcs v_t–p and p–v_b into two ideal triangles. Alternatively, view B* as the fusion of two bigons. This gives a linear isomorphism m: O⊗O → S_n(B*) via the top and bottom embeddings.
2. Mixed relation: c_klb_ij and b_ijc_kl each differ from the crossing-free planar diagram by one height exchange, at e_r and e_l respectively. So C_2B_1 = A B_1C_2 A', where A and A' are LS height-exchange matrices. These are q^{∓1/n} times R^{±1} or R_21^{±1}, and the scalars cancel.
3. Triangularity: in the triangle picture, B = B^{(1)}B^{(2)} and C = C^{(1)}C^{(2)} (round3_q4). The compatibility needed is local, at each of e_l and e_r. The height exchange between endpoints of a corner arc at the upper vertex and one at the lower vertex should be triangular in the direction that keeps LY-bad states bad. This is precisely the property that makes LY's reduction work for triangles, whose reduced algebras embed in quantum tori. With compatibility, #15 and #16 give the ideal and the presentation, using the renaming of round3_q1 §1.
4. Reversed bad arcs: an arc e_r → e_l is, by LS's vertex/antipode relation, a scalar times a quantum minor of size n−1, i.e. an entry of S(U). Modulo J it is a cofactor of a triangular matrix, which vanishes in the bad triangle.
5. Normalisation: LS's n-vertex boundary relation gives det_q U = 1 in S_n(B) for u_ij itself (to be confirmed). Then #14 gives Πb_ii = Πc_ii = 1.

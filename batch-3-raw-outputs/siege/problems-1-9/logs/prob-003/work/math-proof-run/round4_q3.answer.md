## 1. Setting and the cited inputs (all cited from memory; precise form stated)

**Ground field and notation.**
- The ground field is k, containing q^{±1/n}, with q not a root of unity.
- ω := q − q^{-1}, which is nonzero.
- \bar i := n+1−i.
- V = k^n.
- R is the matrix of the query (Klimyk–Schmüdgen's R).
- P is the flip, and Ř := PR.
- Matrix-entry conventions are those of the query.
- Products in skein algebras are stacking: in αβ, the factor α is higher.

**Corner-arc vocabulary.** Fix the orientation of every surface (counterclockwise = ccw).
- A *corner arc* at a boundary ideal point v is a simple oriented arc joining the two boundary edges adjacent to v that cuts off a neighbourhood of v.
- It goes either ccw or cw around v.
- Its *tail state* t is the state at its starting point and its *head state* h the state at its end point.

**Cited inputs.**

- **(L1) Height exchange (LS).** Let e be a boundary edge, and consider two endpoints on e of a stated diagram that are adjacent in height and both outgoing (resp. both incoming). There is a fixed invertible matrix H with the following property: the diagram with the heights of these two endpoints exchanged (positions in e unchanged) equals Σ H_{(s σ),(s'σ')} (the original diagram with states (s',σ') at the two endpoints). Here (s,σ) are the states at the endpoint nearer to the one end of e and at the other endpoint.
  - H depends only on the local type: both outgoing or both incoming, and the direction of the exchange.
  - It does **not** depend on the rest of the diagram or on the surface.
  - I use only the existence of such local matrices, never their entries. LS state them explicitly; they are q^{∓1/n} times braiding matrices.

- **(L2) Bigon (LS).** For the bigon B, S_n(B) ≅ O_q(SL_n) as algebras, via u_{ij} ↦ x_{ij}. Here u_{ij} is the left-to-right arc with state i on e_l and state j on e_r.
  - In the bigon arcs commute up to the RTT relation: R^ε U_1U_2 = U_2U_1 R^ε, with ε ∈ {+1,−1} fixed by the LS conventions (I believe ε = +1).
  - The normalisation is det_q U = 1 for the arcs themselves.
  - The bialgebra structure: the counit is ε(u_{ij}) = δ_{ij}, the coproduct is Δu_{ij} = Σ_k u_{ik}⊗u_{kj} (cutting along an ideal arc), and the antipode is S.

- **(L3) Comodule endomorphisms (standard, q generic).** Let X ∈ End(V⊗V) satisfy X U_1U_2 = U_1U_2 X entrywise in O_q(SL_n) (n ≥ 2). Then X ∈ span{1, Ř}.
  - Reason: V⊗V is the direct sum of two non-isomorphic simple O_q-comodules (q-symmetric and q-exterior square), namely the two eigenspaces of Ř (eigenvalues q and −q^{-1}).
  - Moreover Ř satisfies the Hecke relation Ř² = ωŘ + 1. Hence R^{-1} = R_21 − ωP and R_21^{-1} = R − ωP.

- **(L4) LY reduced algebras are not degenerate.** For the ideal triangle P_3, S̄_n(P_3) ≠ 0.
  - Reason: LY's (reduced) quantum trace is a unital algebra homomorphism from S̄_n(P_3) into a quantum torus, so 1 ≠ 0 in S̄_n(P_3).
  - This is the **only** fact about LY's theory beyond (L5) that I use.

- **(L5) Shape of LY's bad-arc rule.** The rule is intrinsic: invariant under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms, and local at the corner. So whether a corner arc is bad depends only on its direction (ccw or cw) and on the pair (t,h).
  - For each direction the bad set is a strict triangle: F_ccw, F_cw ∈ { {t<h}, {t>h} }.
  - I do **not** assume which triangles, nor any relation between F_ccw and F_cw. Both are *derived* below.

- **(L6) Cap and U-turn relations (LS).**
  - (i) A returning arc (both endpoints on one edge, cutting off no puncture or ideal point), with states (i,j) at its endpoints, equals ν·δ_{j,\bar i} for some nonzero scalar ν depending on i and on the height order.
  - (ii) An arc making a U-turn near an edge e equals Σ_k κ_k·(the diagram cut at e, with states k and \bar k on the two new endpoints, in a fixed height order). The κ_k are nonzero scalars.
  - Only the shape is used: nonzero scalars, and the pairing k ↔ \bar k.

- **(L7) Functoriality (LS).** An orientation-preserving embedding of surfaces that maps boundary edges into boundary edges, one edge into one edge, induces an algebra homomorphism. It acts on diagrams by taking images.
  - Applied to the embedding of B onto a neighbourhood of a corner, this gives the maps φ_top, φ_bot : O → S_n(B*) and the corner maps into P_3.

- **(L8) Fusion / decomposition (Costantino–Lê for n = 2; the SL_n version I attribute to LS).** The map m: O⊗O → S_n(B*), x⊗y ↦ φ_top(x)φ_bot(y), is a linear isomorphism.
  - Argument: B* is obtained from two bigons by fusing (gluing a triangle along) the two left edges and then the two right edges, and fusion induces a linear isomorphism of stated skein modules.
  - **Citation GAP**: I am sure of this for n = 2. For general n I recall it but cannot check the reference.

**Correction to (L3), used throughout.** The commutant depends on ε. RTT(R^ε) gives (PR^ε)U_1U_2 = PU_2U_1R^ε = U_1U_2(PR^ε). So the correct statement is:

  {X : XU_1U_2 = U_1U_2X} = span{1, PR^ε}.

PR^ε has two distinct eigenvalues, and its eigenspaces are the two simple summands.

## 2. Locality: the mixed relation is the bigon's top/bottom relation (PROVED from L1, L2, L3, L7)

**Setting.** Let Σ ∈ {B, B*}. Consider an arc T ("top") and an arc Bo ("bottom"), both from e_l to e_r and oriented left to right, with T drawn above Bo.
- In B both are parallel; in B*, T passes above the puncture and Bo below it.
- T has states (i on e_l, j on e_r), and Bo has states (k, l).
- The diagrams D_{±±}(i,k; j,l) carry signs that record whether T's endpoint is higher (+) or lower (−) than Bo's, at e_l and at e_r respectively.
- T·Bo = D_{++}, because the higher factor can be isotoped to be drawn in the top position with no crossings. Likewise Bo·T = D_{−−}.

By (L1), applied first at e_l (two outgoing endpoints, T's nearer v_t) and then at e_r (two incoming ones):

  D_{−−}(i,k;j,l) = Σ A_{(ik),(i'k')} A'_{(j'l'),(jl)} D_{++}(i',k';j',l').

The matrices A and A' are local, so they are the same for Σ = B and Σ = B*. Hence:

**Lemma 2.1.** With these A, A':
- in O = S_n(B): U_2U_1 = A U_1U_2 A';
- in S_n(B*): C_2B_1 = A B_1C_2 A'.

The entrywise conventions are those of the query, so (U_2U_1)_{(ik),(jl)} = u_{kl}u_{ij}.

**Lemma 2.2.** A' = A^{-1}, and A = R^ε(α·1 + β·PR^ε) for some scalars α, β.

*Proof.*
1. Apply the counit (an algebra map) entrywise to U_2U_1 = AU_1U_2A'.
   - Left side: ε(u_{kl}u_{ij}) = δ_{kl}δ_{ij} = I_{(ik),(jl)}.
   - Right side: Σ A_{(ik),(i'k')}δ_{i'j'}δ_{k'l'}A'_{(j'l'),(jl)} = (AA')_{(ik),(jl)}.
   - Hence AA' = 1.
2. RTT gives U_2U_1 = R^εU_1U_2R^{-ε}. So X := R^{-ε}A commutes with U_1U_2.
3. By the corrected (L3), X ∈ span{1, PR^ε}. ∎

**Block form.** Fix i > k. Every matrix in span{R^{±1}, R_{21}^{±1}, P, PR^ε, …} preserves the plane spanned by the index pairs f_1 = (i,k) and f_2 = (k,i), and acts by a scalar on each (i,i).
- In the basis (f_1, f_2), with rows and columns ordered (f_1, f_2):
  - R = [[1, ω],[0, 1]] and R^{-1} = [[1, −ω],[0, 1]];
  - R_21 = [[1, 0],[ω, 1]] and R_21^{-1} = [[1, 0],[−ω, 1]];
  - P = [[0, 1],[1, 0]];
  - PR^ε = [[0, 1],[1, εω]].
- Hence

  A = R^ε(α + βPR^ε) = [[α+εωβ, β+εω(α+εωβ)], [β, α+εωβ]].

- So, for all i > k:
  - **A_{(ki),(ik)} = β**;
  - A_{(ik),(ki)} = β + εω(α + εωβ);
  - on the pair (i,i), A acts by the scalar q^ε(α + βq^ε).
- Moreover, both R^ε and R_21^{-ε} belong to the family:
  - R^ε corresponds to (α, β) = (1, 0).
  - R_21^{-ε} corresponds to (α, β) = (1+ω², −εω). This follows from the Hecke relation, and can be checked directly on the blocks and on the diagonal: for ε = 1, q(1+ω²−ωq) = q^{-1}; for ε = −1, q^{-1}(1+ω²+ωq^{-1}) = q.

## 3. The triangle argument: compatibility is forced (PROVED from L1, L2, L4, L5, L7)

**Setup.** Let P_3 have ideal vertices v_1, v_2, v_3 and edges e = v_1v_2, e_1 = v_1v_3, e_2 = v_2v_3. Draw e vertical on the left, with v_1 at the top and v_3 on the right. Then:
- turning from e to e_1 around v_1 is ccw;
- turning from e to e_2 around v_2 is cw;
- in B, an arc from e_l to e_r is ccw around v_t and cw around v_b.

So (L7) gives algebra maps:
- φ_1 : O → S_n(P_3), sending (B, v_t, e_l, e_r) to (P_3, v_1, e, e_1). Write α_{st} := φ_1(u_{st}): the ccw corner arc at v_1 with tail state s on e and head state t on e_1.
- φ_2 : O → S_n(P_3), sending (B, v_b, e_l, e_r) to (P_3, v_2, e, e_2). Write β_{στ} := φ_2(u_{στ}): the cw corner arc at v_2 with tail σ on e and head τ on e_2.

**Lemma 3.1.** In S_n(P_3),

  β_{στ}α_{st} = Σ_{s',σ'} A_{(sσ),(s'σ')} α_{s't}β_{σ'τ},

with the matrix A of §2.

*Proof.*
- The endpoints on e_1 and e_2 lie on different edges, so their heights are irrelevant.
- β·α and α·β therefore differ only by the height exchange at e of two outgoing endpoints, with α's endpoint nearer v_1.
- This is exactly the local configuration at e_l in §2 (T's endpoint nearer v_t). By (L1) the matrix is the same. ∎

**Lemma 3.2 (diagonals are units).** Let J ⊂ O be the ideal generated by the u_{st} with (s,t) in a strict triangle Θ.
- In O/J the diagonal entries commute: the RTT entry ((i,j),(i,j)) gives u_{ii}u_{jj} − u_{jj}u_{ii} = ±ω u_{ij}u_{ji}, and the right side lies in J.
- Also u_{11}⋯u_{nn} = det_q U = 1: every σ ≠ id in the expansion of det_q has a letter in Θ.
- Hence each u_{ii} is a unit in O/J.

Now φ_1 maps the generators of J (for Θ = F_ccw) to bad arcs. So α_{ss} is a unit in S̄_n(P_3), and likewise β_{ss} (with Θ = F_cw). ∎

**Proposition 3.3.**
- (i) If F_ccw = {t<h}, then A = αR^ε (with α ≠ 0) and F_cw = {t>h}.
- (ii) If F_ccw = {t>h}, then A = α'R_21^{-ε} (with α' ≠ 0) and F_cw = {t<h}.

*Proof of (i).* All computations are in S̄ := S̄_n(P_3), which is nonzero by (L4).

1. **β = 0.** Take s < σ (possible since n ≥ 2).
   - Apply Lemma 3.1 with t = σ and τ = s. Row (s,σ) of A is supported on {(s,σ), (σ,s)}, so

     β_{σs}α_{sσ} = A_{(sσ),(sσ)}α_{sσ}β_{σs} + A_{(sσ),(σs)}α_{σσ}β_{ss}.

   - α_{sσ} is bad (s < σ), so the left side and the first term vanish in S̄.
   - By the block form (with k = s < i = σ), A_{(sσ),(σs)} = β.
   - So β·α_{σσ}β_{ss} = 0. If β ≠ 0, multiplying by the inverses of the units gives 1 = 0 in S̄, contradicting (L4).
   - Hence β = 0 and A = αR^ε, with α ≠ 0 because A is invertible.
2. **F_cw = {t>h}.** Suppose instead F_cw = {t<h}. Take k < p, so β_{kp} is bad.
   - Lemma 3.1 with (s,t,σ,τ) = (p,k,k,p) gives

     0 = β_{kp}α_{pk} = A_{(pk),(pk)}α_{pk}β_{kp} + A_{(pk),(kp)}α_{kk}β_{pp}.

   - The first term is 0 because β_{kp} is bad.
   - A_{(pk),(kp)} = α(R^ε)_{(pk),(kp)} = εαω ≠ 0, since p > k.
   - So α_{kk}β_{pp} = 0, which again gives 1 = 0. Hence F_cw = {t>h}.

*Proof of (ii).* This is symmetric.

1. **A is a multiple of R_21^{-ε}.** Take s > σ, so α_{sσ} is bad. Lemma 3.1 with t = σ, τ = s gives A_{(sσ),(σs)}α_{σσ}β_{ss} = 0, so A_{(sσ),(σs)} = 0 for s > σ.
   - That entry is β + εω(α+εωβ). Setting it to zero is one linear equation in (α,β), whose α-coefficient εω is nonzero. So its solution space is a line.
   - R_21^{-ε} lies in the family and satisfies the equation, since its swap entries sit only in the rows (a,b) with a < b.
   - Hence A = α'R_21^{-ε}.
2. **F_cw = {t<h}.** Suppose instead F_cw = {t>h}. Take k > p, so β_{kp} is bad.
   - The same computation gives A_{(pk),(kp)}α_{kk}β_{pp} = 0.
   - Here A_{(pk),(kp)} = α'(R_21^{-ε})_{(pk),(kp)} = −εα'ω ≠ 0, since p < k.
   - This is a contradiction, so F_cw = {t<h}. ∎

**Corollary 3.4 (mixed relation of B*, exact form).** In B*, b_{ij} is a ccw corner arc at v_t with tail i and head j. c_{kl} is a cw corner arc at v_b with tail k and head l.

- **Case (i), F_ccw = {t<h}.**
  - The bad b_{ij} are those with i < j, and the bad c_{kl} those with k > l.
  - The mixed relation is C_2B_1 = R^εB_1C_2R^{-ε}, that is, R^εX_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1R^ε with X = B (lower) and Y = C (upper).
- **Case (ii), F_ccw = {t>h}.**
  - The bad b_{ij} are those with i > j, and the bad c_{kl} those with k < l.
  - The mixed relation is C_2B_1 = R_21^{-ε}B_1C_2R_21^{ε}.
  - Conjugate by P, using (PMP)_{(ik),(jl)} = M_{(ki),(lj)}, PR_21P = R, P(C_2B_1)P = C_1B_2 and P(B_1C_2)P = B_2C_1, all in the query's entry conventions. This gives C_1B_2 = R^{-ε}B_2C_1R^{ε}, that is, R^εX_1Y_2 = Y_2X_1R^ε with X = C (lower) and Y = B (upper).
- In both cases X and Y satisfy RTT(R^ε): they are images of U under φ_top and φ_bot.

**So 𝓡 = R^ε always, and Z = B exactly when F_ccw = {t<h}.**

The compatibility support property (Lemma 3 of the prior work) holds in both cases. In case (i), R^{±1} have swap entries only in rows (a,b) with a > b. So in c_{kl}b_{ij} = Σ R^ε_{(ik),(i'k')} b_{i'j'}c_{k'l'} R^{-ε}_{(j'l'),(jl)}, only terms with i' ≤ i, j' ≥ j, k' ≥ k and l' ≤ l occur. Thus bad b-letters stay bad, and so do bad c-letters.

## 4. Reversed corner arcs (closes GAP C; PROVED from L2, L6, L7, Prop. 3.3)

In B, let \bar u_{ab} be the arc oriented from e_r to e_l, with state a at its e_l-end and state b at its e_r-end.

**Lemma 4.1.** \bar u_{ab} = λ_{ab}·S(u_{\bar b\bar a}) for nonzero scalars λ_{ab}.

*Proof.*
1. **The diagram Γ_{ia}.** Let Γ_{ia} be the following arc. It leaves e_l with state i (the "forward strand"), runs to near e_r, makes a U-turn there, and returns to e_l, arriving with state a.
   - Position the forward strand (upper or lower) so that, in the fixed height order produced by the U-turn relation (L6)(ii) at e_r, the forward strand's new endpoint is the higher one.
   - At e_l, give the forward strand's endpoint the larger height.
2. **First evaluation.** B has no puncture, so Γ_{ia} is isotopic, keeping its endpoints on e_l, to a returning arc at e_l. By (L6)(i), Γ_{ia} = ν_iδ_{a,\bar i} with ν_i ≠ 0.
3. **Second evaluation.** By (L6)(ii) at e_r, after renaming k ↔ \bar k if necessary, Γ_{ia} = Σ_k κ_k·u_{ik}\bar u_{a\bar k}. This diagram is a product because the forward strand is higher at both edges.
4. **Inverting.** Put W_{ka} := κ_k\bar u_{a\bar k} and N_{ia} := ν_iδ_{a,\bar i}.
   - Steps 2 and 3 say UW = N, and N is an invertible scalar matrix.
   - U has the two-sided inverse S(U) (the antipode axioms), so W = S(U)N.
   - Entrywise: κ_k\bar u_{a\bar k} = S(u_{k\bar a})ν_{\bar a}.
   - Putting b = \bar k gives the claim, with λ_{ab} = ν_{\bar a}/κ_{\bar b}. ∎

**Lemma 4.2.** Let Θ be a strict triangle and J = (u_{st} : (s,t) ∈ Θ). Then S(u_{xy}) ∈ J for every (x,y) ∈ Θ.

*Proof.*
- In O/J, the matrix U is triangular with zero Θ-entries, and its diagonal entries are units (Lemma 3.2).
- Back substitution gives a right inverse W of the same triangular shape. For example, when U is lower triangular, W_{jj} = u_{jj}^{-1} and W_{ij} = −u_{ii}^{-1}Σ_{j≤k<i}u_{ik}W_{kj} for i > j.
- The image V of S(U) satisfies VU = 1, so V = VUW = W, which has zero Θ-entries. ∎

**Proposition 4.3.** Every bad corner arc of B* of reversed orientation lies in the ideal I_f generated by the bad b_{ij} and the bad c_{kl}.

*Proof.*
- **Which arcs occur.** Corner arcs at v_t are the arcs above the puncture, namely b_{ij} = φ_top(u_{ij}) and \bar b_{ab} = φ_top(\bar u_{ab}). Likewise, at v_b they are c_{kl} = φ_bot(u_{kl}) and \bar c_{ab} = φ_bot(\bar u_{ab}).
- **Directions.** \bar b_{ab} runs from e_r to e_l above the puncture, so it is cw at v_t, with tail b and head a. \bar c_{ab} is ccw at v_b, with tail b and head a.
- **Case (i)** (F_ccw = {t<h}, F_cw = {t>h}).
  - \bar b_{ab} is bad iff b > a. Then \bar b < \bar a, so S(u_{\bar b\bar a}) ∈ J_top = (u_{st} : s<t) by Lemma 4.2.
  - Hence \bar b_{ab} = λφ_top(S(u_{\bar b\bar a})) ∈ φ_top(J_top) ⊂ I_f.
  - \bar c_{ab} is bad iff b < a. Then \bar b > \bar a, so S(u_{\bar b\bar a}) ∈ J_bot = (u_{kl} : k>l). Hence \bar c_{ab} ∈ I_f.
- **Case (ii)** is identical, with all inequalities reversed. ∎

So the LY ideal of B* is I_f, the ideal generated by the bad entries of B and C.

## 5. Assembly: proof of MS (PROVED from §§2–4 and L2, L7, L8)

Work in case (i), so X = B is lower triangular, Y = C is upper triangular, and 𝓡 = R^ε. Case (ii) is the same after the renaming X = C, Y = B of Cor. 3.4.

Notation:
- J_+ := (u_{ij} : i<j) and J_- := (u_{ij} : i>j) in O.
- b = φ_top and c = φ_bot.
- m is the decomposition map of (L8).

**5.1 (the ideal).** V := m(J_+⊗O + O⊗J_-) equals I_f, hence equals the LY ideal of B*.
- V ⊂ I_f is clear.
- V contains the generators of I_f.
- V is a two-sided ideal:
  - Left multiplication by b-letters and right multiplication by c-letters preserve V, because J_± are ideals.
  - Left multiplication by c_{kl}, and right multiplication by b_{ij}, are handled by moving the letter through the other word with c_{kl}b_{ij} = Σ R^ε_{(ik),(i'k')}b_{i'j'}c_{k'l'}R^{-ε}_{(j'l'),(jl)}.
  - Each such move keeps bad b-letters bad and bad c-letters bad (end of §3). J_± are spanned by the words containing a bad letter, so the result stays in V.
  - This is the earlier worker's Proposition 4, verbatim.

Hence S̄_n(B*) = S_n(B*)/V ≅ (O/J_+) ⊗ (O/J_-) as vector spaces, via m. This uses (L8).

**5.2 (the triangular Borel quotients).** O/J_+ ≅ P_-, the algebra generated by a lower-triangular T with RTT(R^ε) and Πt_{ii} = 1, via u_{ij} ↦ t_{ij} for i ≥ j and u_{ij} ↦ 0 otherwise. The analogous statement holds for O/J_- ≅ P_+.
- The diagonal entries of T are automatically units: they commute modulo the relations, and their product is 1.
- Proof: the two maps P_- → O/J_+ and O → P_- are well defined (Lemma 3.2 for the first, and det_q T = Πt_{ii} = 1 for the second), and they are mutually inverse on generators. This is the earlier worker's Lemma 6.

**5.3 (presentation; MS items 2 and 3).** Let P(R^ε) be MS's algebra.
- Define g: P(R^ε) → S̄ by x_{ij} ↦ b_{ij} and y_{ij} ↦ c_{ij}. It is a well-defined algebra map:
  - RTT for X and for Y holds because φ_top and φ_bot are algebra maps (L7);
  - the mixed relation holds by Cor. 3.4;
  - the bad entries vanish in S̄, and Πb_{ii} = Πc_{ii} = 1, by Lemma 3.2 together with det_q = 1 (L2).
- Define f: P_-⊗P_+ → P(R^ε) by x⊗y ↦ xy. It is surjective: the mixed relation rewrites every yx as Σ x'y', and the inverses of the diagonal entries are products of generators.
- The composite g∘f is the linear isomorphism of 5.1 combined with 5.2.
- Hence f is injective, so f is bijective, and therefore g is bijective.

**MS item 1** is Cor. 3.4: in case (i), b_{ij} = 0 for i<j and c_{ij} = 0 for i>j in S̄. ∎

**Conclusion.** MS holds, with 𝓡 = R^ε. Here ε is the sign of the LS bigon presentation, and I believe ε = +1, i.e. Klimyk–Schmüdgen's O_q(SL_n) at q. The choice of Z is:
- (Z, W) = (B, C) if LY's rule declares a ccw corner arc bad when tail < head;
- (Z, W) = (C, B) otherwise.

## 6. Consequences for the problem (using the run's proved Theorem D and #17)

**Part (1).** Take ε = +1 and case (i). Then P(R) is literally A_D, with X = L^- and Y = L^+. So Ψ: S̄_n(B*) → U_q(b_-) ⋈ U_q(b_+) is an algebra isomorphism, given on generators by:
- b_{ii} ↦ \hat K_i'^{-1};
- b_{ki} ↦ (q^{-1}−q)F_{ki}\hat K'^{-1}_k for k > i (with F_{i+1,i} = F_i);
- c_{ii} ↦ \hat K_i;
- c_{ik} ↦ (q−q^{-1})\hat K_kE_{ik} for i < k (with E_{i,i+1} = E_i);
- all bad entries ↦ 0.

The inverse is given by Theorem D(b): \hat K_i = c_{ii}, \hat K'_i = b_{ii}^{-1}, E_i = (q−q^{-1})^{-1}c_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}c_{i,i+1}, and F_i = (q^{-1}−q)^{-1}b_{i+1,i}b_{i+1,i+1}^{-1}.

The other conventions change this as follows:
- In case (ii), exchange the roles of b and c.
